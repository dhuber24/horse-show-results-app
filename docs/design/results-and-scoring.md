# Results, Scoring And The Judge's Card

Read before changing results, a scribe screen, the judge's card, card types, publishing, patterns, or the show-record reports. Live boards and high-point standings are in [live-boards-and-high-point.md](live-boards-and-high-point.md); the screens are described in [frontend.md](../frontend.md) (Scribe Form Branching) and the APHA rules behind them in [apha.md](../apha.md).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## What the app does about judging, and what it does not

`Claude.md` says the app records a judge's card and adds it up, and that it does not judge classes, decide what a maneuver is worth or which penalty applies, or enforce association judging rules. The list once said the app did not "score maneuvers" or "calculate penalties":

**This list used to say "score maneuvers" and "calculate penalties", and migration 122 changed that deliberately rather than eroding it by accident.** Once the app holds the maneuvers and the penalties, it is either computing the number that drives placings, side pot standings and futurity Hi-Point, or it is holding a card next to a separately typed total that will eventually disagree with it — and a card the app refuses to add up is a scan with extra steps. What has not moved is the boundary that matters: every number on a card was called by a judge and written down by a scribe, the arithmetic is a sum the app performs on their behalf, and a human can overrule the result (`judge_cards.override_score`, audited).

## Results, one card per judge

- `results`: placings, **one card per judge** — `judge_id` points at `show_judges` (the assignment, not the registry judge) since migration 095, and NULL means unattributed. For `pattern`/`time` classes, `raw_score` is the source of truth and `place` is recomputed server-side on every change, ranked *within each judge's card*. Visibility is gated by `classes.results_published_at` — see below.
- `results.outcome`: **what happened on this judge's card** (migration 121) — `placed`, `zero_score`, `no_score`, `disqualified` or `eliminated`, with `place` now nullable. `place` was NOT NULL, so every row had to claim a placing and the other four states had to be typed as a blank — indistinguishable from a horse the scribe had not reached yet. **Only `placed` and `zero_score` rank** (`placings.RANKED_OUTCOMES`): a declared zero is a number the sheet compares, which is the distinction SC-265.E.4-6 draws against a No Score, and it belongs below everyone who scored rather than off the card. The outcome decides whether the app *ranks* a row; a human decides whether it carries a place, because AM-111.D keeps a rider eliminated during an Over Fences ride-off in the placings, last among that group, and the app cannot know a ride-off happened. Per card rather than per entry — the coarser `entries.is_disqualified` still means an entry that is out before anybody judges it.
- `results.tiebreak_rank`: **how the judge broke a tie** (migration 121), lowest first, kept apart from `raw_score` so recording the decision never edits the number the judge called. Every pattern class in the rule book leaves ties to the judge (AM-115.B.2); before this the only way to record the answer was to alter one of the two scores. `rank_card` sorts on `(score, tiebreak_rank)`, so a broken tie takes two places and an unbroken one stays shared. `rules.ties_must_be_broken(cls)` is False by default and True for APHA on scored classes only — a `placement` tie is one the scribe ticked deliberately, a scored tie is one the app derived from two equal numbers.
- `backend/placings.py`: **reading a placing off a card that may not have one.** A nullable `place` breaks `min(results, key=lambda r: r.place)` the first time a judge throws a horse out, and every screen that showed a best-of-panel figure did exactly that. Two rules are settled here once so the screens cannot disagree: an unplaced card is not a candidate for "best" (a judge who disqualified an entry did not rank it last), and a non-ranked outcome earns nothing in side pot standings or futurity Hi-Point. Every pre-121 row backfilled to `placed`, so no existing standing moved.
- For `pattern`/`time` classes the scribe enters `raw_score` and the backend recomputes `place` and `is_tie` on insert/update/delete — **grouped by judge**. Every judge marks the same run on their own sheet, so a 71.5 from one and a 71.5 from another are two firsts, not a tie; pooling them would also make each judge's placings shift as other judges filed. Audit rows are only written for `placement` classes.
- **A tie is broken by ranking it, never by editing a score.** `results.tiebreak_rank` exists so the judge's decision and the number they called stay separate facts. Nudging one 71.5 to 71.6 to make the sort come out right falsifies the card, and the card is what APHA asks the show to retain for a year (SC-110.J).
- Unassigning a show judge who has already filed placings returns **409**, not a cascade. `results.judge_id` is `ON DELETE RESTRICT` on purpose: the answer to "this judge has 40 placings on file" is never to delete them as a side effect of tidying the panel.
- The app **does not combine judges' cards into an official result**, and adding one is a rules decision, not a display improvement. Each judge places independently and they disagree; the public class page orders rows by mean placing purely so the sheet reads top-down and says so on the page. Side pot standings currently take the entry's *best* card per class — deterministic and identical to the old behaviour on the single-judge shows pots have actually run on — but show bills settle multi-judge pots "from combined judge score sheets", which is a sum. Changing it would silently reprice existing pots, so it needs a deliberate call.

## Card types and the judge's card

- `judging_systems` / `judging_penalties`: **how a judge's card is marked** (migration 122). There is no single card shape — equitation on the flat scores maneuvers −3 to +3 in half points against fixed 3/5/10 penalty tiers, Equitation Over Fences (AM-111.F) scores each fence −1.5 to +1.5 on a 0–100 scale, cow work (SC-265.E) uses 1/3/5 penalties with letter codes — so the scale is declared per system and the sheet is built from whichever applies. Keyed on `show_types` rather than `associations`, like `association_standard_classes`: how a class is scored is the breed body's catalog question. `show_type_id` NULL is a generic system, always offered. A penalty carries either a fixed `value` or a `min_value`/`max_value` range the judge picks within, which about a third of AM-111.F's table is. **The catalog is a convenience, not the vocabulary** — the seed holds the scales and the tier *values*, which is what the rule text states, and the ~35 named penalties are not loaded because inventing labels under APHA's name is worse than an empty list. Assigned per class in setup **Step 8: Scoring** (`/admin/shows/[id]/classes/judging`, beside the show's high-point chart), as the sheet of a Scored or Equitation card — see `classes.card_type` — a step of its own since it is a per-class designation made once the schedule exists, and that screen is the only place the card shapes are explained.
- `classes.card_type`: **how each class is placed, as the office names it** (migration 155) — `placing` (Placing Cards: rank order, the rail classes), `scored` (Scored / Numeric Cards: maneuvers or fences with deductions — reining, cutting, trail, over fences), `equitation` (Equitation / Pattern Cards: rider position, accuracy and precision through a ground or mounted pattern — showmanship, horsemanship, equitation) or `timed`. **`score_type` stays the engine's field** — the scribe screens, ranking, side pots and the pattern-class duplicate-horse rule all branch on it — and each card type maps onto exactly one (`card_types.SCORE_TYPE_FOR`); scored and equitation both map to `pattern`, which is the whole reason the column exists. **Read it through `effective_card_type`, never raw**: NULL means nobody chose, and a stored value that no longer agrees with `score_type` (the class editor or an import changed it) is ignored — both are derived from the score type, then the class's sheet, then its discipline and name (showmanship / horsemanship / equitation → equitation, any other pattern class → scored). The class list serves it as `effective_card_type`. Set on setup **Step 8, Scoring**, by `POST /shows/{id}/classes/scoring` — one card type (and optionally one sheet) for **a whole ticked list of classes at once**, since a schedule is 150 classes and most share an answer; a row's own picker is the same call with one class in it. It writes `score_type` with `card_type`, so the two cannot disagree. **A class with placings filed keeps its score type** (`scoring_blocker`, 409 `SCORE_TYPE_LOCKED`, all or nothing and by name): a filed rank read as a score is a different result. Scored ↔ equitation is always allowed — both are scored. `judging_systems.card_type` says which of the two score-based types a sheet belongs to (the three APHA sheets are classified in 155; NULL is offered to both), and the endpoint refuses a sheet on a placing or timed class, or on the wrong type.
- `judge_cards` / `card_maneuvers` / `card_penalties`: **one judge's worksheet for one entry** (migration 122). Keyed on `(class_id, entry_id, judge_id)` and **not** on a `results` row: `bulk_save_results` is a delete-all-then-insert-all and the scribe screens autosave on a settle, so anything FK'd to `results.id` would be destroyed every time somebody typed. `computed_score` comes from `backend/judging.py` (base + Σ maneuvers − Σ penalties, clamped to `score_max`, never below zero); `override_score` is a human disagreeing with the arithmetic and writes a `result_audit` row. **The card does not write `results`** — the scribe screen carries `effective_score` into the ordinary save, so `results.raw_score` keeps exactly one writer.
- **The card computes; the result records — and only one thing writes `results`.** `save_card` deliberately does not touch `results.raw_score`, even though it would be convenient. The results bulk save deletes and reinserts a whole card on a 1.5s settle; a second writer over the same number is how a score goes missing. The scribe screen takes `effective_score` off the card save response and lets the ordinary autosave carry it.
- **An empty card scores nothing, not its base.** `compute_score` returns None when no maneuver is marked and no penalty is recorded. Returning 70 would put every entry nobody has judged yet into the placings at the same score. A penalty on its own *is* a marked card — the judge called something.
- **A card never goes negative.** Far enough below zero is a zero score, which is a `results.outcome` (migration 121), not a negative number on the sheet.
- **A penalty with no catalog row is unconstrained on purpose.** `card_penalties.label` is free text beside the optional `penalty_id`, because a penalty the judge called that nobody has loaded is still a penalty — and validating it against the nearest catalog row would send the scribe looking for the closest wrong answer. Only a penalty that *names* a catalog row is checked against it.
- What the scribe screen captures today is the **final number**, not the judge's card. `results.raw_score` holds one score per entry per judge, which is the office tabulation step. Real scribing is maneuver-by-maneuver (base 70, maneuver scores, penalty tiers of 3/5/10, Form & Effectiveness, judge comments) and is not modeled yet. Open work, not an oversight to paper over.

## Posting results

- `classes.results_published_at`: the **publish gate** (migration 094). NULL means the class's placings are a staff-only draft; a timestamp means they are on the public `/results` and class results pages. The scribe screens autosave continuously into the draft and a human posts the class with one button. Reads split on it: `GET /shows/{id}/classes/{cid}/results/` returns everything to show staff and `[]` to everyone else, and `GET /shows/{id}/results-index` (public, no caller to except) filters on it outright.
- **How deep a class must be placed before posting** is the association's call, not the app's. `rules.required_published_places(cls)` returns None by default — an OPEN show answers to nobody about placing depth, and inventing a number would block a jackpot that pays three — and 7 for APHA (SC-110.I: one through seven, under every judge). `placing_shortfall()` in `routers/results.py` reports what each card is missing and `POST .../results/publish` refuses with `PLACINGS_INCOMPLETE` until `acknowledge_incomplete` is passed. **Cards are the assigned judges, not the judges who filed** — a panel where one judge has entered nothing is the exact case the rule is about, and reading the judges out of the results would call it complete. The depth is capped by the entries that could be placed, which leaves out scratches and **no-shows at the gate** (migration 162) — a 7-horse class with two no-shows can place five. It confirms rather than blocks because the app cannot see a class placed deliberately shallow, or a horse that left the ring without the gate recording it; it is not silent because the scribe form's own warning only catches *interior* gaps, so a card that stops at third looked finished to it.
- **The publish gate asks two separate questions, under two separate flags.** `acknowledge_incomplete` posts past an association placing-depth shortfall (SC-110.I); `acknowledge_ties` posts a shared place past an unbroken tie (AM-115.B.2). They are not one flag because they are not one question — a shortfall asks whether the card is finished, a tie asks which of two horses won, and only the judge can answer the second. `PublishBar` accumulates the answers across rounds, since the backend checks ties first and meeting a shortfall afterwards must not silently re-ask the tie.
- **Autosave is only safe because of the publish gate.** The scribe forms have no Save button; they commit the whole class on a 1.5s settle. That is only tolerable because results land in a draft first — a placement card reads 1, _, 3 until the last horse is entered, and results go straight to public at-the-rail screens. Do not add an autosaving surface that writes results without checking `results_published_at`, and do not "simplify" the gate away. The same reasoning moved the gap warning from Save to Post: mid-entry gaps are now normal.
- **Audit rows start at publish, not at first keystroke.** `bulk_save_results` writes `result_audit` only when `results_published_at` is already set. Before that there is no published value for an edit to have changed, and autosave would otherwise write a row per settle — turning the record of who changed an official placing into keystroke noise. Corrections *after* posting are the thing the audit exists for, and those are still captured.
- **The scribe screens list the riders who went into the ring.** A scratch and a no-show at the gate (`entries.gate_no_show`, migration 162) are left off — **unless the rider already has a placing on some card**, who stays, labelled *(scratched)* or *(no-show at the gate)*. The bulk save below replaces a judge's whole card with what the screen sends, so a placed rider the screen did not list would lose that placing on the next autosave. The scribe page filters (`scribe/page.tsx`); `GET .../entries/` still returns every entry, because the public class page and the office read it too.
- The results bulk save is **delete-all-then-insert-all within one judge's card**, so two in flight can interleave and lose a score. `useAutosave` keeps one request at a time and queues the next; anything else calling that endpoint on a timer needs the same care. The `judge_id` on the request envelope is what scopes the delete — omit it and the save lands on the unattributed card rather than the judge's, which looks like the placings vanishing.
- Reading results as staff requires **passing auth headers** — `fetchResults(showId, classId, headers?)`. The endpoint returns `[]` for an unposted class to an anonymous caller, which is the gate working; a staff screen that forgets the headers silently renders an empty card over a real draft.
- `result_audit`: placing change history (placement classes only — derived placings are not audited, and **only once the class is published**). `new_place`/`old_place` may be NULL since migration 121, which is what an entry moving into or out of a non-placed outcome looks like.

## Finding results: the filters

- **The public Results page and the Show Results report filter the same way, through one module.** Name, Back #, Horse and Class, plus a *My classes* toggle for a signed-in exhibitor, matched by `frontend/lib/results-filter.ts` on both and drawn by `components/ResultsFilterBar.tsx`. Two copies of the matching is how "Back # 14" would come to find 142 in the office and not at the rail.
- **Every field filled in must match the same placing.** The Results page had one search box matched against everything in a class, so "reed dusty" found a class where Reed rode one horse and somebody else rode Dusty Gold. A field per question is the only way to say "this person on this horse".
- **A back number is matched whole** (`matchesWhole`: 14 does not find 142; a typed `#` is ignored), and **a number typed in any other field matches a whole number** (`matchesWords`: class "12" finds 12 and 12A, not 112). Substring matching listed every class with the digit in it.
- **My classes is the reader's own entries, read off the dashboard endpoint** (`fetchMyShowEntries` in `lib/my-class-ids.ts`), minus `WITHDRAWN` ones. It lists every class they are in, posted or not — which of mine are still to come is half the question — and under each posted one their own placing, one line per judge's card. It matches on `entry_id`, carried on `results-index` for this (already public on the class results payload), never on a name, which two exhibitors can share. Offered only to an account with an exhibitor record, like the schedule's Registered filter: anybody else has no classes, so the toggle would be dead.
- **A report's filters are declared by the registry, beside its columns** (`show_reports._filter`, served as `ReportOut.filters`), so a renamed column cannot leave a filter matching nothing (`test_every_results_filter_reads_keys_the_rows_carry`). A filter may read a row key that is not a column: the results rows carry `class_id` and `entry_id` for *My classes* without printing two UUIDs on a sheet the office forwards. `components/FilteredReport.tsx` renders any show report and offers whatever it declares — none, for every report but Show Results.
- **A filter narrows the page, not the report.** The report is built and served whole; CSV and Print take the rows on screen, because printing one exhibitor's results is the reason to filter. **A filtered copy says it is one** — a *Filtered —* line above the table that prints, a `Filtered:` line in the CSV and `-filtered` in its file name — since a part of the show's record forwarded as the whole of it is the failure the publish gate and the report notes exist to prevent.

## Patterns

- `classes.pattern_posted_at` / `pattern_notes`: **when the pattern went up, and which one** (migration 120). Every pattern class in the rule book requires the judge to post it at least an hour before the class. Two things the app deliberately does not do: it **cannot check the hour**, because `classes` carries a date and no start time, so there is nothing to measure back from — recording *whether* and *when* is the half that is answerable; and this is **the posting at the in-gate**, which stays the official copy — the file exhibitors read ahead is `show_patterns` (migration 146, below), and uploading one is not a posting and does not set this. Nothing is refused over either — refusing a class would not have posted its pattern any earlier. Recorded from the gate screen at `PATCH /shows/{id}/gate/classes/{cid}/pattern`, which takes the timestamp itself so a class cannot claim after the fact that it met the deadline.
- `show_patterns` / `show_pattern_classes`: **the show's patterns, on file and assigned to the classes that run them** (migration 146). Migration 120 argued the app should not store a pattern because a second copy could disagree with the one posted at the gate; that gave way for the reason migration 127 gave way on the show bill — shows already e-mail and post their patterns ahead, so refusing the upload never prevented a second copy, it only made it one the office could not replace and an exhibitor could not find from their class. The hazard is managed instead: **one current file per pattern** (`PUT .../patterns/{id}/file` replaces it in place, keeping the classes, and moves `file_uploaded_at`), the replacement time is printed on every reader ("Changed Sat 2:14 PM"), and the exhibitor's page says the in-gate copy is official. **Attaching a pattern to a class is optional** — adding it is what makes it available to exhibitors, and a pattern attached to nothing is still on their list. **A class runs one pattern and a pattern runs many** (one showmanship pattern across every division), so `show_pattern_classes` is keyed on `class_id` and set from the class's side: `PUT /shows/{id}/patterns/assignments` points one class, or a discipline's worth, at a pattern or at none, and the response is every pattern, because the ones those classes left changed too. A join table rather than `classes.pattern_id` so the hottest table in the app gains no column a running release could fail to map. The class list payload carries `pattern_id` / `pattern_name`, which is how the **schedule** (a one-tap *Pattern* button per class), the **gate** and the **class page** link to it with no second request. Reads are public (`GET /shows/{id}/patterns`, `.../{pid}/file`, served `no-cache` and named for the pattern); writes are the show office's. Managed at `/admin/shows/[id]/patterns` (a dashboard tile, not a setup step — patterns arrive in the days before the show and change on the day): add, what is on file, then a **class list** with a pattern dropdown per class, grouped by discipline with a *Set all* per group. The dropdown changes the moment it is picked and saves behind it, **one request at a time** — every answer is the whole list, so two in flight could land out of order and put back a row already changed again; only the last answer in a run is drawn, and the page asks before it is closed mid-save. Read at `/shows/[id]/patterns` — each pattern with **View** and **Download** and the classes it is used in — which puts a signed-in exhibitor's own classes first. `file_data` is `deferred` and nothing relates to the model, the `ShowDocument` rule: a list of patterns must not drag a dozen photographs through memory.

## Show-record reports

- **A results report shows only *posted* classes.** Posting is what makes a placing official, and a report the office forwards must not be the first place a half-typed card counts as a result. The count of unposted classes rides along as a note so a missing class is never a mystery. **Entry Cards is the exception** — an entry is a document in its own right and exists whether or not the class has been judged.
- **The retention bundle does not replace the paper, and says so — in wording no association owns.** It is offered at every show, whatever body sanctions it, so it names no association's rule (APHA's SC-110.J is one of the rules it serves); how long to keep the record, and whether a processed copy comes back, is left to the sanctioning body. It is an ordinary tile on the Show Record page, not a highlighted notice. `GET /shows/{id}/reports/archive` puts the class summary, results, entry cards and judges' cards on one printable page, generated from the show's own data the way the show bill is. Its `caveats` state plainly that the *signed* judge's cards are paper the judge hands the office and nothing the app generates is that document — Judges' Cards is what the scribe recorded off them. A printout that implied otherwise would be worse than no bundle.
- **The compliance sheet is an output, never an input.** AM-300.E.4 says an exhibitor who fails the ownership requirement loses their points and **keeps their placings**, and everyone else's placings are explicitly unchanged. So nothing reads the sheet back into a class. Nothing on it is verified by the app either — a membership number is what somebody typed, an attestation is what somebody declared, and what the office physically inspected lives in `show_verifications`.
