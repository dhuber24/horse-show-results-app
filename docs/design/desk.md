# The Registration Desk, Paperwork And The Show Office

Read before changing `GET /shows/{id}/desk`, anything the office does to an exhibitor at the counter, paperwork sign-offs, health flags, waivers, the show's inbox, or joining two records of one person. The exhibitor's side of the same records is in [registration.md](registration.md); the walkthrough is in [show-workflow.md](../show-workflow.md) (The Registration Desk, Paperwork Check-In).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Key Files

| Area | File |
| --- | --- |

## The desk

- **The registration desk** (`GET /shows/{id}/desk`): not a table. Everything the show office does to one exhibitor, in one read — back number, class entries, paperwork checks, and their balance. Entries, back numbers, and check-in were three screens and are one conversation at the counter; `/admin/shows/[id]/entries`, `/back-numbers`, and `/check-in` all redirect to `/admin/shows/[id]/desk`. **The desk computes nothing of its own**: money comes from `_load_financials` → `build_account`, paperwork from `build_verification_checklist`, and every button posts to the endpoint that already owned that job. The writes it adds are its own: `POST /shows/{id}/desk/exhibitors` creates the `show_entries` shell row those endpoints assume — a back number and a pot entry both hang off it, so before this a walk-up could not be given either without first inventing a class entry for them — and `POST .../desk/exhibitors/new` creates the **exhibitor record itself** for somebody the app has never seen (migration 140). A first and last name is the whole requirement, because that is what is on the paper entry blank being handed across; an email and a phone number are optional, and **no `users` row is created**, since a login belongs to the person who will sign in to it and inventing an address and a password for somebody who has not asked is not the office's business. Deliberately **not idempotent**, unlike adding an existing exhibitor: two people at one show really can be called Sarah Johnson. **An email is asked about first**, because an address is the person where a name is not: one already on any exhibitor record is a `409 EMAIL_ON_FILE` naming each record and whether it is on this show, the form offers to add or open it instead, and `acknowledge_email_on_file` creates anyway (a youth entered under a parent's address). The add form's name search likewise lists people **already on this show** once a name is typed — leaving them out is how a second record of somebody already entered got made. The horse is a separate press against the endpoint that already owns it (`POST /shows/{id}/exhibitors/{id}/horses`), offered once the person exists, because most walk-ups already have theirs on file and a second form between a name and a back number is how a queue builds up. It is **asked in the roster column and answered in the panel** (`ExhibitorPanel`'s `openAddHorse`): the column is `minmax(0,20rem)` and `StaffAddHorseForm` is built for the panel beside it. That form is a `@container` with its grid on `@sm:` rather than `sm:` — a viewport breakpoint is the wrong question for a card in a narrow column on a wide screen — and its association row wraps on `min-w-0`, since a `<select>` is as wide as its longest `<option>` until told otherwise. **The Classes section quotes the bill** (`bill` on each desk exhibitor) rather than summing `entry_fee_cents`, which read $0 for every futurity class — and a futurity class entered with no enrollment behind it bills nothing at all, because the enrollment's category is its price. So the desk carries the show's `futurities`, flags each (futurity, horse) entered but not enrolled, and the add-entry form asks for the category and enrolls in the same press — **with no category preselected**, since the categories price the same class $75, $100 or $150. **A class row's fee is everything the bill charges on that class**: its entry fee, any per-class club sanction, and the show's per-class fees (`per_entry`, `per_judge_per_entry`), which `build_bill` puts against the class entries it counted (`class_lines[].charges`, `class_charge_total_cents`, the charge line marked `per_class`). Without that a $0 class carrying a $5 per-judge assessment read $0 on the row while the header billed $5. **The exhibitor's bill follows the same rule** (`ShowBillBreakdown`, on My Shows and What I Owe): one line per class at that total, and the `per_class` charge lines are *not* listed again below it — a $0 class with a "+ $5.00" note and the same $5.00 as its own line read as a class entered twice. The class picker quotes the same figure before the press from `per_class_charges` on the preview (`billing.per_class_charges`, which shares `fee_counts_class` with `charge_lines`, so the quote cannot drift from the bill). The attribution is the backend's, not the browser's, because the scoping that decided the charge lives in `billing.py`. **Charges that belong to no class stay out of the Classes section** — a per-horse office fee, a club's per-horse sanction fee: the section is the classes, and the header's billed figure carries the rest. They were listed under the table for one release and taken out again at the show manager's request. And a per-judge fee reads $0 everywhere on a show with no judges assigned yet, which is correct and moot once the panel exists. The sections fold by `hidden` rather than unmounting so a half-filled entry form survives it. Classes and Contact fold remembered per browser; **Paperwork folds itself** once the exhibitor is in good standing and opens again when something needs doing — what decides it is that person's paperwork, not the office's habit, and a press on its heading overrides it for that exhibitor until the answer changes. An exhibitor with no horse entered stays open, because "+ Add a horse" lives in it and the class picker sends staff there.
- **Every Paperwork row the desk still owes is outlined red and says what to do** (`desk/NeedsAction.tsx`: `rowFrame` and `ToDo`). A badge reading "8 to check" over a dozen rows that differ only by a grey pill left staff to find the eight; the outline is what the eye lands on, and the sentence inside it is what makes the outline a next step rather than a puzzle. One frame for every kind of row — membership, papers, health, emergency contact, release — so red means the same thing everywhere. **The red rows, the section badge and the jump link at the top of the panel count the same set** (`paperworkToDo` in `desk/types.ts`): `paperwork_outstanding`, plus — at a show that does not ask to see health originals — each health document the file does not cover, which owes no sign-off and so is not in the backend's count but is flagged at the top of the panel and cleared by one press. The jump walks them in the order they are drawn, health before age and papers within a horse; it used to skip the emergency contact and the releases, so an exhibitor whose only outstanding item was a release was sent to the top of the section.
- **An aggregate screen quotes; it does not compute.** `show_desk.py` assembles the whole desk from `_load_financials` and `build_verification_checklist` rather than re-deriving a total or a check status, and its buttons post to the existing entry / back-number / side-pot / verification endpoints. A screen that sums `entry_fee_cents` itself is faster and will eventually disagree with the bill the exhibitor reads on My Shows; one that writes `entries` directly skips association validation and the closed-class rule. Applies to any future "one screen for X" — the aggregate is a read, not a second implementation.
- **The desk carries the exhibitor's own contact details, and none of them are a check.** `show_office._build_contact` reads email, telephone, postal address and a youth exhibitor's guardian through a `ShowExhibitorView` -- what the exhibitor gave *this show's* registration where they changed it there, the profile otherwise (migration 145) -- beside `_build_emergency_contact`, which reads the same way. It is assembled **after `outstanding` is counted** and deliberately carries no `status` — an exhibitor with no email on file is somebody the office telephones instead, not paperwork anybody owes, and a contact row in the chase list would be one staff could never clear. **Which email wins is the one real decision**: `users.email` is what somebody signs in with, and `exhibitors.email` (migration 140) is what the office wrote on a paper entry blank, which for a walk-up record is the only address there is. The account wins where there is one — the precedence the exhibitor registry already uses — `email_source` says which was taken, and a *different* office address rides alongside as `office_email` rather than being dropped, since that is either the better address or a typo and both are worth a glance before a bill goes to the wrong inbox. The same address in a different case is one fact, not two. **Show-office tier by virtue of the endpoint**: the desk is ADMIN / SHOW_MANAGER / SHOW_SECRETARY, and no scribe or gate-steward screen reads this payload — "show staff" is ambiguous in this app and this is deliberately not the wide reading. **The office takes them over the counter, and none is required** (`PATCH /shows/{id}/exhibitors/{exhibitor_id}/contact`, `desk/ContactForm.tsx`): telephone, postal address and a youth exhibitor's guardian go to **this show's copy** (`take_details`, as the emergency contact does), and the email to `exhibitors.email` — the office's own address on the record, migration 140, which no registration edits — never to `users.email`, which is a login. A field left out of the body is untouched and one sent blank is cleared; the form sends only what changed, so an untouched blank cannot clear an office address that happened to match the account's. It was read-only until then, because `PATCH /exhibitors/{id}` is ADMIN-or-self. **`ShowDeskExhibitorOut` declares `contact` (`ExhibitorContactOut`)**: the desk's response model drops any key it does not name, and for as long as it did not, the Contact section read "nothing on file" for every exhibitor at every show while `_build_contact` filled the block in correctly.
- **Staff taking an emergency contact at the desk write the show's copy, never the profile.** `PATCH /shows/{id}/exhibitors/{exhibitor_id}/emergency-contact` puts the contact on that show's `show_registration_profiles` row (migration 145) — show data does not write back to the profile, from the exhibitor's registration or from the desk. This reverses the earlier rule that it wrote `exhibitors`; the cost, accepted, is that the details step at that show stops following the profile. `PATCH /exhibitors/{id}` is ADMIN-or-self, which is why this exists at all — a secretary could not otherwise take a number over the counter without asking somebody to go and edit their own account. Both halves or neither: a name with no number still reads as missing everywhere it is checked, so accepting one would let staff save something and watch the row go on saying "no emergency contact". **With no contact on file the form is simply open** in its red row, pre-filled with whatever half there is, and Enter saves it. It used to be a sentence with "Take one now" underlined in the middle, and the office could not see where to click.
- Show staff creating a horse for an exhibitor is scoped to that show's roster, not to staff rank. `POST /shows/{id}/exhibitors/{exhibitor_id}/horses` 403s unless the exhibitor has a `show_entries` row or a class entry at that show. The horse is owned by the exhibitor and linked via `exhibitor_horses` — ownership alone does not put it on their profile list, which reads `created_by_exhibitor_id` **or** a link row.
- `show_contact_messages`: the show's inbox (migration 091). Anyone — including visitors with no account — can message a show from its page; staff read it at `/admin/shows/[id]/messages`. **Stored, never emailed**: `mailer.py` does nothing without SMTP, and a forward-only contact form would accept a message, say "sent", and lose it. Every sender field the writer *types* is self-reported and joined to nothing. Since migration 103 a message sent by a signed-in caller also carries `sender_user_id` / `sender_exhibitor_id`, **stamped from the session and never from the body**, and the inbox badges it with that person's back number at this show. That is the whole point of the addition: a secretary reading a name in a free-text field cannot tell the exhibitor holding number 42 from somebody who has never been to the show, and the answer to the question usually depends on which it is. Being signed in does not gate sending — a stranger asking about stalls is still the case the endpoint was built for, and an anonymous message is a NULL stamp rather than a rejection. **Every show's inbox can also be read at once** (`GET /my-messages`, `routers/my_messages.py`, `/admin/messages`) — what the top bar's envelope opens away from a show. It is **read-only and scoped exactly as the per-show inbox is**: every show for an ADMIN, otherwise `show_access.worked_show_ids` (a per-show row or the show's company), so one club's secretary never reads another's mail. Marking read or archived goes through the per-show endpoint with the message's own `show_id`, so there is still one writer and one access check.

## Exhibitor records at the counter

- **The desk's By Exhibitor list is a list of people, not of exhibitor records.** A back number lives on `show_entries`, one per (show, exhibitor), so somebody on the desk twice with two numbers is two `exhibitors` rows with one name — a record left behind when an account was deleted (`exhibitors.user_id` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`), an office record and the account its owner opened afterwards, or two people who are genuinely both called Sarah Johnson. `DeskClient` groups the roster by normalised name and shows every record's panel stacked under one entry, with a note saying they are separate records. **Grouped for display, never merged automatically**: a name is not proof of identity, so nothing here decides two records are one person — each keeps its own classes, paperwork and account until somebody says otherwise. What changed with `backend/exhibitor_merge.py` is that saying so is now a button rather than a deletion; see the next bullet.
- **Merging two exhibitor records moves everything and then deletes one, and the app never decides that two records are one person.** `exhibitor_merge.merge_exhibitors(keep, remove, db)` is the single implementation behind both doors — the desk's (`POST /shows/{id}/desk/exhibitors/{id}/merge`, scoped to a record on that show's roster) and the admin's (`POST /exhibitors/{id}/merge`, any two) — for the same reason the exhibitor's cancellation and the office's are one function: the *permission* differs and what it leaves behind must not. Fifteen FK columns across thirteen tables are repointed (a show's `show_registration_profiles` row folds like its draft: the survivor's stands), and the handful with a uniqueness rule are **folded rather than dropped**: two roster rows at one show become one (`_fold_show_entry`), two bookings of one fee are **added together keeping the earlier `reserved_at`** (repricing an April booking at a July date is the one thing an early rate promises against), and the survivor takes the **better standing of the two** — a live sign-up beats a shell row and beats a cancellation, because somebody who cancelled under an old record and signed up under a new one is entered. A back number is adopted only where the survivor has none. **The caller chooses which record survives; the login is not theirs to lose** — an account on the record being removed moves across, or the merge would leave somebody unable to sign in to their own entries. The three judgement calls (`adopt_standing`, `merge_reservation`, `fill_profile_blanks`) are pure functions written against plain attributes precisely so they are pinned by tests without a database. **Two records that both hold accounts are refused at the desk** (`TWO_ACCOUNTS`) and allowed for an admin: deciding which of somebody's two logins is the real one is not a registration-counter question. `merge_candidates` ranks suggestions and says why — an email the office wrote down matching the one an account was opened with (`matched_on: "email"`) is the best reason this app can offer, and a shared name (`"name"`) is no evidence at all. Both are still only a reason to ask.
- **An exhibitor record is not a user, and `/admin/users` will never list one that has no login.** That screen is logins, roles and approval; an exhibitor the office typed in at a desk has none of those on purpose. Every exhibitor *record* is at `/admin/exhibitors` — `GET /exhibitors/registry`, which carries `has_account` and `office_record` (the two facts that say what a row is) plus show and class counts from **two aggregates over the whole result**, never a query per row. **Deliberately not deduplicated**, unlike `/exhibitors/names`: an owner picker wants one row per person, a registry is asking which records exist, and collapsing one behind another is the question it was opened to answer. Do not "fix" the missing-user confusion by creating a `users` row at the desk — a fake account surfaces in every role dropdown and staff picker, and the person it names never asked for a login. Renaming an accountless record lives here because nothing else offers it (a typo at the counter prints on entries, back-number lists and published results); a record **with** an account takes its name from the account through `_sync_linked_exhibitor_name`, so that rename belongs under Users and is not offered. **The registry also joins any two rows**, which the duplicate list above it cannot: that list only carries pairs the app *spotted* (a shared name or email) and never an accountless row nobody claims, so two records spelled differently — or one of them a seed leftover — appear in neither. A screen that shows somebody a duplicate and gives them no button is worse than one that never showed them. `holdingText` and the merge types are in `lib/exhibitor-merge.ts`, because three screens print that line.

## Paperwork sign-offs and waivers

- `show_verifications`: what the show office **physically inspected** at the desk (migrations 090, 098). One table, four `kind`s — `horse_age` (foaling date on the papers), `horse_registration` (a registration number, per association), `exhibitor_membership` (a membership card, per association), `horse_health_document` (a Coggins, CVI, or vaccination record, keyed on `(horse_id, document_type)`) — because the actor, the question, and the staleness rule are identical. A `trainer_membership` kind existed briefly (migration 098, reversed by 100): the trainer is not at the counter, has no entry and no back number, and their card is the association's business rather than this show's, so the check sat permanently unverified and inflated every outstanding count. Per show on purpose: this is a show attesting its own office saw the paper, not a permanent property of the horse or person. `verified_value` snapshots what was on file at sign-off, so a later edit flips the check to stale instead of leaving it green; for a health document that snapshot is the standing derived **from the documents on file** (`valid:2027-05-03`, or `missing:none` when the office signed off on paper the app has never seen). A health sign-off clears the horse's health flag for this show, and may also carry `attested_expiry` (migration 101) — the date read off the paper, where somebody recorded one; the desk no longer asks. Records only; nothing here gates entry.
- A paperwork verification is never taken from the client. `POST /shows/{id}/verifications` accepts the *subject* only; the backend reads the current value off the record and stores that. A caller able to name the value it "verified" could attest to a number nobody has on file. For the same reason `verified_value` is a snapshot, not a live join — that is the whole staleness mechanism.
- **A show may only ask for the paperwork of the bodies it runs under** (`backend/show_associations.py`). An exhibitor's profile carries every membership they hold and a horse carries every body it is papered with, because both belong to the person and the animal rather than to one weekend; what the office may ask them to produce is the intersection with the show's own breed body and its sanctioning clubs. `exhibitor_profile` prompts from that list and `horse_eligibility` flags against it, and **the desk was the third reader and did not** -- so an Open show with no club sanctioning put APHA, WSCA and MNSPHC membership sign-offs and four registration sign-offs in front of staff and counted every one as outstanding. A check nobody working that show could ever clear is one they learn to scroll past, which spends the credibility of the checks that matter. **An empty list means nothing to ask for, never everything**: there is deliberately no `associations` row for OPEN, so an Open show with no clubs drops both sections entirely.
- **Which sign-offs the desk does is the show's choice, and an unchosen one leaves the desk** (`shows.requires_membership_card_check`, `requires_horse_age_check`, `requires_registration_papers_check`, migration 160; `show_office.desk_inspections`). Plenty of shows never look at registration papers during class registration, and the desk handed them a red row per horse per association that nobody there would clear — the failure `show_associations` and migration 138 each fixed on their half of the panel. Asked on the Paperwork step's *Checked at the registration desk* card, beside the health-originals question. Defaults true. **Off, the check is not built at all** — no row, no count, not in the sweep totals — which is the one way these differ from the health originals: a health sign-off clears the horse's flag even where the show did not ask for one, so those rows stay; a card, foaling-date or papers sign-off only records, so a row nobody is asked to do is clutter. The desk payload carries the three flags, so the panel can tell "this show does not check papers" from "no papers on file"; `age_check` is null and the lists empty where a check is off. Sign-offs already recorded stay in `show_verifications` and reappear if the check is turned back on. `POST /verifications` still accepts any kind: recording a paper the office was handed is never wrong, only no longer asked for.
- `show_waivers` / `show_waiver_signatures`: the entry blank, the liability release, and venue rules (migration 099). Free text, because the words come from the venue's insurer or the fair board and this app has no business supplying them; **only the title is required** — `body` is stored as `""` when there is no wording (the column is NOT NULL), because plenty of shows hand out a paper form and only want the desk to track who signed it, and the signing screen then offers *Sign* with no text box rather than *Read & sign* over an empty one; `is_required` separates what an exhibitor cannot compete without from what the show wants read but does not chase — **the Paperwork step no longer offers the choice and creates every waiver required**, because a waiver nobody chases is indistinguishable at the desk from one somebody forgot to chase, and a show that only wants something read has the show bill for it. The column stays: rows filed before this, and a futurity's own release, may still be optional, and the list goes on marking those. Signatures arrive by two routes into **one** table — the exhibitor types their name at sign-up, or hands a paper blank across the counter and staff record it with `on_paper` set — so a show running entirely on clipboards still has a working outstanding count. `signed_by_guardian` + `guardian_relationship` because a release signed by a twelve-year-old is not a release and youth classes are a third of a schedule. Since migration 109 a waiver may carry `futurity_id`, which narrows **who is asked** rather than adding a second signature mechanism: only that futurity's entrants are counted and chased, everyone can still read it (`applies_to_me` on the exhibitor payload), and NULL keeps the original meaning of the whole show. Emergency contacts get no table here: they are the `emergency_contact_name`/`_phone` columns, read through `ShowExhibitorView` and written to the show's copy by `PATCH /shows/{id}/exhibitors/{exhibitor_id}/emergency-contact` (see The desk, above).
- **A signature is the one value the backend does not derive.** Every other sign-off reads its value off the record precisely so a caller cannot attest to something nobody has on file. There is nothing to read a signature from, so `signed_name` is accepted from the client where the exhibitor types it. `on_paper` is set by the endpoint, not the caller, so the two routes stay honest about which one a row came through.
- **At the desk every waiver is one checkbox: signed or not.** The office ticks once the signed paper is in the folder, the desk sends no name, and the endpoint writes the exhibitor's own. This was the rule for a futurity's release only; the show's own entry blank and releases asked staff to transcribe the name and say whether a guardian signed for a minor, and 422'd without a name. The show office asked for the tick — to them a release is signed or it is not, and the paper itself records who held the pen. A guardian's signature typed **in the app** still carries `signed_by_guardian` and is shown as such; a release the exhibitor signed in the app shows ticked and locked, with Undo beside it, because unticking a box is too easy a way to remove somebody's own signature.
- **Editing a waiver does not invalidate its signatures.** A show fixing a typo must not void what a hundred people already signed, and the app cannot tell a typo from a change of terms. Deleting and re-creating the waiver is the honest way to ask everyone again — and deleting it takes the signatures with it, because they were agreement to *that* text.

## Health paperwork

- **Health flags** (`GET /shows/{id}/health-flags`, and inline on the desk): not a table. Which entered horses have health paperwork that will not carry them through the show, derived on read from `horse_documents` and judged against `shows.end_date`. Covers **the documents this show requires** — Coggins, CVI, and vaccinations — never all three unconditionally. Computed rather than stored so it clears itself when the exhibitor uploads a current document; there is no row to remember to close. One implementation in `backend/routers/horse_documents.py` (`health_by_horse` / `document_health` / `health_requirements`), shared by the desk, the flag endpoint, and the exhibitor's registration screen so they cannot disagree about a horse.
- **Health paperwork does not gate entry.** A missing, undated, or lapsed Coggins used to 422 on both entry paths; it now flags. Refusing the entry never made a horse compliant — it just moved the discovery to the desk and turned the office's to-do into an "override" that recorded a bypass. Do not re-add the block.
- **The flag and the sign-off answer different questions.** An earlier version of this file argued there was no point signing off a Coggins, on the grounds that it is either current (the file says so) or lapsed (signing changes nothing). That collapses two questions. The file answers *is the date still good*. Only a person at the counter answers *does this paper describe this horse* — markings and description against the animal in the trailer, on a document that is genuine and physically present. So a health line carries both: a derived `status` and an attested `inspection`, and they can disagree in both directions. A current Coggins nobody has looked at and a lapsed one the office is holding are different situations.
- **An inspection puts the horse in good standing for this show.** `attested_health()` overlays the derived standing whenever this show's office has signed the document off, so the horse reads `valid` and drops off the chase list. *I inspected it* is one press, and its tooltip and its red row both say the claim it makes: the paper describes this horse **and covers the show**. A paper that fails that — lapsed, illegible, another horse's — is simply not signed off; the row stays red and the exhibitor is the one to chase. Anything reading `valid` this way is marked `attested: true` with "accepted for this show", because the app is not holding that document and the *next* show will ask again.
- **This reverses "only when it records a date", at the show office's request.** The earlier rule held that "I looked at this" and "this is valid" are different claims, so an inspection cleared the flag only with an `attested_expiry` covering the show, and the form asked for the date — first as a blank box over *Record without a date*, then with a deliberate *no usable date* tick and a warning for a date short of `paperwork_deadline`. Each version was reported as the same problem: an office that had just held a good Coggins went on being told to find one. The answer was to word the button as the claim the office is making rather than to ask for evidence of it. `attested_expiry` stays on the table and is quoted on the line when a recorded one covers the show; one that does not is dropped from the cleared line rather than contradicting it. Rows recorded under the old form with no date now clear their horse too — most were offices trying to clear it. `paperwork_deadline` is still on the desk payload, though no screen reads it now.
- **The attestation is an overlay, never an input to the snapshot.** `document_health()` carries `file_snapshot` — the standing derived from the documents alone — and `health_snapshot()` returns that. Snapshotting the overlaid value would have the sign-off recording its own effect, and the check would read back `stale` the instant it was written. Staleness has to keep meaning "the file changed under me".
- **A health sign-off must work with nothing on file.** `horse_health_document` is keyed on `(horse_id, document_type)`, not on a `horse_documents` row, because the ordinary case at a horse show is an exhibitor handing over a paper the app has never seen. Requiring an upload would break the sign-off exactly where it is needed. `missing:none` is a perfectly good thing to have attested to, and it goes stale the moment a document arrives — which is correct.
- **Coggins is universal; CVI and vaccinations are not.** A Certificate of Veterinary Inspection follows from crossing a state line and vaccination rules come from the venue, so both are opt-in per show (migration 097). Deriving a flat "no CVI on file" flag would light up every in-state horse at every show and staff would learn to ignore the whole panel — the second feature would poison the first. The policy has to exist before the derivation is worth having.
- **Which papers a show requires and whether it wants to see them are two questions, and only the first was ever asked** (`requires_physical_check` in `routers/horse_documents.py`, migration 138). The desk answered the second for itself, with yes: every required document produced an inspection sign-off counted as paperwork the office owed. So a show perfectly happy with the uploaded Coggins got a row per horse per document that nobody working it could meaningfully clear — the same failure `show_associations` fixed on the membership half of the same panel. `requires_physical_document_check` defaults **true**, because that is what every show already did; defaulting the other way would silently empty a live show's chase list. False does **not** hide the health rows — the office may still record a paper it was handed, and that sign-off still clears the flag — it only stops the inspection counting toward `outstanding` and the sweep totals, and the desk prints a line where those rows are, since a row that is listed and not counted otherwise reads as one staff forgot. Asked on the Paperwork step's *Checked at the registration desk* card with the other three desk inspections (migration 160), disabled with a reason while no document is required, and stored regardless, so ticking a document off and on again does not lose the answer.
- **Coggins carries its own expiry; the others are dated from issue.** `HealthRequirement.valid_days` is None for Coggins on purpose — how long a negative test stays good is a state rule (twelve months in most, six in some) and the app does not know which state the horse is standing in. A CVI is written as "issued within 30 days", so `effective_expiry()` counts the show's window from `issue_date` — but a printed expiry on the document always wins.
- Health status is judged **as of `shows.end_date`**, never today — `coggins_status(expiries, as_of)`, with `_paperwork_deadline()` picking the day. A Coggins that lapses between now and the show is the exact case staff need to chase; evaluating against today calls it valid until it is too late. Last day rather than first, because the horse is on the grounds all week. The `/profile` horse card is the one place that legitimately uses today — it has no show in hand.
- `coggins_override_audit`: **historical** (migration 082). One row per effective show-staff bypass of the Coggins entry gate, back when health paperwork blocked an entry. Nothing writes it now — an override needs a block to override — but the rows stay readable at `GET /shows/{id}/coggins-overrides`. Health shortfalls are a flag now, not a stop: see `show_health_flags` below.
