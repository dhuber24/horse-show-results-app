# Online Payments (Stripe)

Read before changing anything that takes a payment online: Trainer Registry Pro subscriptions, GaitDesk's invoices to show companies, a show company's own Stripe account, or an exhibitor paying a show company. What a fee or a bill *is* stays in [billing.md](billing.md); plans and what they cost are in [companies.md](companies.md).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Three kinds of money, and GaitDesk only holds its own

- **Trainer Registry Pro is a Stripe subscription on GaitDesk's account, card only, and GaitDesk pays the processing fee** (migration 172). The trainer has no other way to pay, so there is nothing to pass a fee on to. Checkout's price is given inline from `trainer_registry.PRO_PRICE_CENTS` rather than as a Stripe Price id in the environment, so the price is written in the app; the trainer screens print `PRO_PRICES` in `frontend/lib/trainer-registry.ts`, and **the two must agree**.
- **A GaitDesk invoice is paid by the show company on GaitDesk's account.** By bank (ACH) for the invoice amount; by credit card with the processing fee on top. **GaitDesk never takes its fee out of an exhibitor's payment** — the company is invoiced after the show and pays once, net 30. Do not add an `application_fee_amount` to exhibitor payments: the owner decided against it, and a company's books should show its exhibitors' money arriving whole.
- **Pro+ is bought up front** (migration 173). A company buys the year on My Company — by bank transfer for $1,499, or by credit card with the processing fee added — and it starts when the payment succeeds (`payment_events.apply_pro_plus_purchase`, which calls `record_pro_plus` as paid). The year runs from today, or from the day after the current one ends; **a year in force renews only in its last 30 days** (`RENEWAL_NOTICE_DAYS`), so a second press is never a second year; and while a bank payment for one is on its way (`show_companies.pro_plus_payment_pending_at`) nothing else can be bought. Each year bought is one `show_company_subscriptions` row per PaymentIntent (`stripe_payment_intent_id`, unique), with its fee in `card_fee_cents`. Without Stripe the company still asks and an admin starts the year.
- **An exhibitor's payment goes to the show company's own Stripe account and never through GaitDesk** (Connect, **Standard** accounts, direct charges). The company owns the account: it signs in to Stripe itself, Stripe pays it out and bills it the processing fees, and its disputes and refunds are its own. Stripe charges a platform nothing for Standard accounts, which is why they were chosen over Express or Custom, which cost per active account and leave the platform liable for the account's losses.
- **A company's account is made with Accounts v2, configured as v1's Standard** (`stripe_gateway.create_connected_account`): `dashboard: full`, `defaults.responsibilities` `fees_collector` and `losses_collector` both `stripe`, and the `merchant` configuration requesting `card_payments` and `ach_debit_payments`. Stripe refuses `Account.create` (v1) for a Connect platform set up since 2026 — the sandbox answered *"Stripe no longer recommends Accounts v1 for new Connect integrations"* — and its onboarding link is a v2 account link. Everything else stays on v1 calls, which Stripe answers for a v2 account id: `Account.retrieve` for `charges_enabled`, and PaymentIntents with `stripe_account` for the exhibitor's payment. Responsibilities cannot be changed once an account is made. Needs `stripe` 15.x; from 15 a Stripe object is no longer a dict, so every response goes through `_plain`.
- **Trainer Checkout names `payment_method_types: ["card"]`, which the pinned API version (`2026-08-26.dahlia`, from `stripe` 15.6.1) still accepts and the newest (`2026-09-30.endive`) refuses** — payment methods there come only from the dashboard. Card-only is the point (GaitDesk pays a trainer's card fee and offers nothing else), so **before moving the SDK to an API version without it**, create a cards-only payment method configuration for the platform and pass its id as `payment_method_configuration` instead.
- **Whether a company can take payments is refreshed when My Company is opened** (`GET /my-company/{id}/online-payments` asks Stripe while the account is not yet enabled), as well as by the `account.updated` webhook. Stripe sends v2-specific events for v2 accounts too; the refresh means the panel is right even if only those arrive.

## The card fee

- **A fee is only ever added to a credit card** (`billing.surcharge_applies`). Card network rules and federal law forbid surcharging debit and prepaid cards; a card whose funding Stripe reports as `unknown` is treated as debit, the safe reading; a bank payment costs the payee at most about $5 and is never surcharged.
- **Whether a card is credit is read on the server, never taken from the browser.** The browser turns what was entered into a Stripe ConfirmationToken; the backend retrieves it (`stripe_gateway.confirmation_token`) and reads `payment_method_preview.card.funding`. This is the whole reason payments are confirmed on the server rather than with Stripe Checkout, which cannot price a payment by card type.
- **The fee is grossed up so the payee nets the amount, and capped at 3%** (`billing.card_fee_cents`, `CARD_FEE_CAP_BP`). Visa caps a surcharge at 3% or the merchant's cost, whichever is lower. Below about $2,300 at 2.9% + 30¢ the cap binds and the payee absorbs the last few cents. The rate is `STRIPE_CARD_PERCENT_BP` / `STRIPE_CARD_FIXED_CENTS` (2.9% + 30¢ by default) — set them to GaitDesk's real rate if Stripe prices the account differently.
- **The payer agrees to a total, and is charged exactly that** (`expected_total_cents`). The form shows the quote — amount, fee, total — before "Pay"; if the amount owed changed in between, the payment is refused (409) rather than charging a figure nobody saw.
- **A show company chooses whether exhibitors pay the fee** (`show_companies.pass_card_fees`, off by default). **Turning it on requires the company to confirm surcharges are allowed where it does business** (`agree_surcharge_rules`): several states ban or cap them, and the company — not GaitDesk — is the merchant. The app does not know a company's state, so it cannot refuse for it.
- **GaitDesk always passes the fee on for its own invoices.** The company chose a card over a free bank transfer.

## Recording what was paid

- **Every settled payment is applied by one idempotent function in `payment_events.py`, whichever road reports it.** A card that succeeds is applied on the spot by the request that took it, so the page is right at once; Stripe's webhook reports the same payment later and changes nothing. A bank payment is applied only by the webhook, days later.
- **An exhibitor's online payment is a `show_payments` row like the desk's cash** (`method` `card` or `transfer`, `recorded_by_name` "Paid online", `stripe_payment_intent_id` unique), so `billed − paid = balance` holds with no new code. **The fee is in `card_fee_cents`, never in `amount_cents`**: `payment_totals_cents` reads `amount_cents` only, so a $1,000 bill paid with $1,030 by credit card does not read as a $30 credit.
- **The balance an exhibitor pays is the office's own figure** (`show_financials._load_financials`, then the caller's account). It is heavier than building one bill, and it is the only way an exhibitor can never pay an amount the office's Financials screen does not show.
- **Only the show company's own account may put money on its exhibitors' bills.** `record_show_payment` checks the event's connected account against `show_companies.stripe_account_id`.
- **A refund is still recorded by the office as a negative row** — and made in the company's own Stripe dashboard. Nothing yet turns a Stripe refund into a row automatically; until it does, both halves are the office's.
- **The amounts travel in the PaymentIntent's metadata** (`amount_cents`, `card_fee_cents`, `method_type`, card brand and last four). The webhook writes down what was quoted; it never recomputes.
- **No part of a card number is stored in GaitDesk's database** (migration 173). The card form is Stripe's own (an iframe), so the number goes from the browser to Stripe; what the app keeps is the Stripe id of each payment and a reference such as "Online, Visa card" — the brand, never the last four digits. A trainer's card for renewals is saved on Stripe's side, on their Stripe customer, never in Neon. Nothing offers to save a card for later.
- **A Pro renewal moves `trainers.pro_until` to the end of the period it pays for, and only ever later** (`apply_trainer_renewal`). The period is read off the invoice's lines, which carry it in every Stripe API version; the subscription id is read from either `invoice.subscription` (before 2025) or `invoice.parent.subscription_details`. Nothing turns Pro off: a cancelled subscription stops moving the date, Pro lapses on its own, and `customer.subscription.deleted` only forgets the subscription so the trainer may start another.
- **The trainer's Stripe customer is created before Checkout** and kept on `trainers.stripe_customer_id`, so a paid invoice can always be matched to the trainer whichever of Stripe's events arrives first.

## GaitDesk invoices

- **An invoice is lines chosen off the usage statement, priced by it** (`gaitdesk_invoices.invoice_lines`, `usage_statement`). Nothing about a line is typed, so an invoice cannot disagree with the statement it came from.
- **A show or a Pro+ year is on at most one invoice that is not void.** Voiding frees its lines; a paid invoice, or one with a bank payment on its way, cannot be voided.
- **A show is invoiced once its last day has come**, because until then it can still gain exhibitors. A Pro+ year is billed up front, so any unpaid, non-complimentary year may be invoiced.
- **Paying an invoice marks each Pro+ year on it paid** (`mark_invoice_paid` sets `payment_received_on`), the same fact an admin's *Mark paid* records.
- **An invoice is kept when its company is deleted**, with `company_name` copied onto it: it is a record of money owed or paid.
- **An invoice is due 30 days after it is issued** (`billing.INVOICE_NET_DAYS`). Overdue is a status, not an action: nothing locks a company out for an unpaid invoice.
- **A company's managers are emailed each invoice**, best-effort like all mail; it is on My Company either way.
- **A finished show is paid for before the company sets up another** (migration 173, `gaitdesk_invoices.settle_up_block`). A Basic or Pro show whose last day is before today, that costs something, and is not on an invoice that is paid or has a bank payment on its way, makes `show_setup_company_for` refuse (403) — on `POST /shows`, a show bill's Create, and moving a show onto the company on Step 1. The company settles it on My Company: *Get the invoice* issues one for every such show not yet invoiced (`issue_settle_up_invoice`, the same invoice an admin issues), and paying it by card or bank lifts the hold at once; a check is marked paid by an admin, which lifts it the same way — the block is read off the invoices each time, never stored. **A bank payment on its way counts as settled**, so a company is not held for the days ACH takes; if it bounces the hold returns. Shows that ended before `show_companies.settle_shows_from` never block — migration 173 set it to its own run date for every company, so shows already run were not suddenly owed, and a new company gets its creation day. A Pro+ show costs nothing per exhibitor and never blocks. Applies to Basic as well as Pro: both are billed the same way, and holding only Pro would leave Basic as the way round it.

## Optional, like SMTP

- **With no Stripe keys nothing offers to take a payment, and nothing breaks.** `stripe_gateway.configured()` needs both `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`. Without them a trainer asks for Pro and an admin records the date, as before; an invoice is paid by check and marked paid; the My Company panel and the What I Owe page say online payment is not available. The SDK is imported only when a call is made.
- **No webhook event is trusted without its signing secret.** An unsigned event could mark any invoice paid, so with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` / `STRIPE_CONNECT_WEBHOOK_SECRET` unset every delivery is refused.
- **Stripe's webhook reaches the API through the web service.** The API refuses any request without the internal key, so `/api/stripe/webhook` and `/api/stripe/webhook/connect` forward each delivery with the body untouched (`req.text()`): the signature is checked against those exact bytes, and parsing the JSON first would break it. Two endpoints because Stripe signs events from connected accounts with a separate endpoint's secret.
- **Each event is applied once** (`stripe_events`): its id is written in the same transaction as its effect, so a failure rolls both back for Stripe to retry, and a retry of a handled event does nothing.
- **Stripe calls run off the event loop** (`stripe_gateway.run`): the SDK makes blocking HTTP requests.

## Setting up the Stripe account

1. Create the Stripe account, and complete its business details. Use **test mode** until everything below works.
2. **Developers → API keys**: put the secret and publishable keys in `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` (`.env` locally with `sk_test_` / `pk_test_` keys; Render's `gaitdesk-api` service with live keys).
3. **Connect → Get started**: enable Connect as a platform, and fill in the platform profile. The code sets each company's account up itself (full Stripe Dashboard, Stripe collects fees and carries losses), whatever the dashboard's own defaults.
4. **Settings → Payment methods**: turn on Cards and **ACH Direct Debit** (US bank account), for GaitDesk's account and as the default for connected accounts.
5. **Settings → Billing → Customer portal**: turn it on, allowing card updates, cancellation and switching between the Pro prices — the *Manage subscription* button opens it.
6. **Developers → Webhooks**: add an endpoint at `https://<site>/api/stripe/webhook` for *Your account* with `checkout.session.completed`, `invoice.paid`, `customer.subscription.deleted`, `payment_intent.succeeded` and `payment_intent.payment_failed`; and one at `https://<site>/api/stripe/webhook/connect` for *Connected accounts* with `account.updated` and `payment_intent.succeeded`. Put their signing secrets in `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and `STRIPE_CONNECT_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.
7. Locally, `stripe listen --events checkout.session.completed,invoice.paid,customer.subscription.deleted,payment_intent.succeeded,payment_intent.payment_failed,account.updated --forward-to localhost:3000/api/stripe/webhook --forward-connect-to localhost:3000/api/stripe/webhook/connect` forwards both kinds of event and prints one signing secret: put it in both `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and `STRIPE_CONNECT_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (`stripe listen --print-secret` prints it alone). The CLI (1.53) refuses to listen without `--events`. Its `stripe trigger` fixtures are written for the newest API version and some fail on a sandbox; a real sandbox payment (`PaymentIntent.create` with `payment_method=pm_card_visa`) makes the same event. The first delivery to a fresh dev server can time out while Next.js compiles the route — warm it with any POST first.
8. Check `STRIPE_CARD_PERCENT_BP` and `STRIPE_CARD_FIXED_CENTS` match the account's card rate.
