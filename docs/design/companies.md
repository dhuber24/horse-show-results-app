# Show Companies, Paid Features And Office Access

Read before changing show companies, who works a show from the office, company staffing, or anything sold as a paid feature. Access checks as a whole are in [auth.md](../auth.md) (Backend Guards).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Companies and who works a show

- `show_companies` / `show_company_members` / `show_company_features`: **the business that runs shows, and what it has paid for** (migration 142). The show bill import is the first thing a customer pays for, and it is sold to the club or firm — not to one of its staff, who come and go, and not to one show, which does not exist yet when the import is used — and nothing in the schema named that business: a show is owned by the accounts in `show_managers` and nothing grouped them. So a company is a name and admin-only `notes` (the billing arrangement, since **the app takes no payment** — a GaitDesk admin turns the feature on by hand once it is settled); members are accounts, **many to many**, because a freelance secretary works for several clubs and gets whatever any of them has; and, until migration 170, **a `show_company_features` row was the switch** for one feature — **retired**: every feature is now part of a plan, the migration emptied the table, nothing reads or writes it, and a later release drops it. A feature is a key of `show_companies.FEATURES`, so adding a paid feature is one registry entry, a place in `PLANS` and a gate. Each registry entry also names its **`plan`** ("GaitDesk Pro") — the one place the subscription is spelled; the locked button, the show-bill pages and the 403 all read it. Managed at `/admin/companies` (ADMIN only), where every company row carries its plan; a show manager's or secretary's companies are listed on their `/admin/users/[id]` page. **Since migration 143 every show manager and secretary is in a company**: an independent gets one of their own, named after them and marked by `owner_user_id` (names are unique among organizations only), and the sign-up forms' optional Company / Organization box creates an organization or asks to join an existing one — see the Sharp Edge below. **Since migration 144 a company can ask GaitDesk for something**: a `show_company_upgrade_requests` row (one per company and key, whoever pressed it), which the admin sees on the company and as a count on the `/admin` tile, and is emailed about best-effort. Since migration 169 a company picks Basic or Pro itself, so the one thing still asked for is **Pro+** (from `ChoosePlanPanel`), answered by recording its payment; an older request for a single feature is answered by moving the company onto a plan that includes it, and an admin may dismiss either.
- `shows.company_id`: **the show company that runs a show, whose staff all work it** (migration 156). A club's secretary could not open the club's own show until somebody assigned them to it by hand, show by show. Now office access is a per-show `show_managers` / `show_secretaries` row **or** membership of the show's company, and `backend/show_access.py` (`works_show`, `worked_show_ids`, `show_office_users`) is the one place that is written down — **every access check reads it**, since a check that forgot the company would lock a club's own secretary out of one screen of a show they could open everywhere else. Only the two office roles qualify through a company; scribes and gate stewards stay per show. The per-show rows remain for a guest from outside the company (a freelance secretary hired for the weekend) and for a show with no company. **Setup Step 1's Staff section is the company's staff list** (`routers/show_company_staff.py`, `ShowCompanyStaff.tsx`), so a change there changes the company on every show it runs: adding somebody is still a request an admin approves (migration 149), only the company's own members may change it, and removing somebody takes them off every show the company runs. Which company runs the show is chosen there too — a new show gets its creator's company, and moving a show is only for the current company's staff (or an admin), to a company they work for. **Not backfilled**: an existing show stays NULL, and is reached exactly as before, until its office picks one — guessing from the creator's companies today would widen who can open it.

## Membership

- **Typing a company's name at sign-up never makes you a member of it.** `show_companies.plan_placement` creates an organization for a name nobody has used, but an existing name becomes a `show_company_join_requests` row for **the company itself** to approve on My Company (or a GaitDesk admin on the company's page) — and the person gets their own company meanwhile. Membership puts somebody on every show the club runs and in its Pro+ year, so joining by name would let anybody who knew what the club was called into its shows — do not "simplify" it into an auto-join. The rest of the rule keeps everyone in exactly the right place: **joining an organization retires a spare own company** (no features on, nobody else in it — `is_spare_personal_company`), **leaving one or deleting one re-homes** anybody it leaves in none, and removing somebody from their own company, or deleting it while it is their only one, is refused. `place_in_company` runs from the two sign-up endpoints and from `_ensure_role_profile` (admin-created accounts and role changes); a new path that creates a show manager or secretary must call it too. A rename reaches their own company through `sync_personal_company_name`, which takes the old name from the caller because the first query autoflushes the rename and the listener rewrites `full_name`.
- **A company's staff manage its people, only from inside it, and add them directly** (migration 169). `routers/my_company.py` is the second door onto membership, beside the admin's Show Companies screens, and Step 1's Staff section (`routers/show_company_staff.py`) the third; all three add through `add_company_member`. **From migration 149 until 169 every addition was approved by a GaitDesk admin**, because membership carried paid features bought once for the whole company, and a company adding people itself could share one subscription with anybody. Plans changed that: a show is billed for itself — per exhibitor, or inside the company's own Pro+ year — so a seat gives away nothing the company did not choose to give, and who works for the club is the club's call. The vouch machinery (`vouch_for_member`, `staff_request_email`) stays for the requests already on file; nothing new writes one. **Every endpoint still refuses a caller who is not already a member**, so nobody can add their way into a company they are not in. Callers are **show managers and secretaries** (an ADMIN manages from Show Companies and belongs to none); a colleague is found by the **email their account signs in with**, and the not-found answer is the same for no account and an account of another role, so the screen does not tell anybody who else uses the app. Somebody with no account gets **an invite** (`POST /my-company/{id}/invites`, a `user_invites` row with `company_id`): the response carries the link, since email is best-effort, and accepting it creates the account and adds them to the company (a manager or secretary — `add_company_member`, which is the company placement a new show manager or secretary must have) or its crew. **The billing notes are not in the payload at all** — they are GaitDesk's. One rule is this door's alone: **a company's own staff cannot leave it empty** (`member_removal_refusal` with a caller), since the last one out would strand its shows and its join requests with nobody to answer them; an admin may. A spare own company of the person added is retired as when an admin adds them.
- **A company's crew is its scribes and gate stewards** (`show_company_crew`, migration 169). Until then they were staffed one show at a time and belonged to nobody, so a company with no show yet had nowhere to keep them — and a Free company has no show. The crew is a **list, not access**: the office still assigns each person to the shows they work (`show_scribes`, `show_gate_stewards`), from the crew on Step 1 (`GET /shows/{id}/company-crew`), and a crew member works nothing they are not assigned to. An office member may assign anybody on the show's company's crew — the older "a secretary only assigns scribes they created" restriction is for somebody else's scribe. Somebody added by email must already be a scribe or gate steward; anybody else is invited. A scribe or steward who accepts **a show's** invite lands on that show's company crew too, so the office never invites them twice. **Taking somebody off the crew takes them off every one of the company's shows that has not finished** — they were there as its crew — while a finished show keeps its record of who scribed it.
- **Mail for GaitDesk's admins reaches every one of them, one at a time** (`show_companies.admin_emails` / `email_admins`) — an upgrade request, a join request, a vouch, a message from the public Contact page. The recipients are every ADMIN account that is not locked (`users.is_approved` is the lock: a shut-off account stops getting customers' messages too), each address once. Each gets **its own copy, sent in turn rather than all at once**, because Resend refuses a team's sends past its per-second limit, so a parallel burst lost the last admins on the list; **a refused send is retried once** after a two-second pause, and one admin's failure never costs the others theirs. `None` from `send_email` (no SMTP) is not retried. A **Reply-To** may be passed through — the contact form sets it to the sender. The service log carries one line per email saying it reached all N admins, or which addresses it missed, since a successful send otherwise logs nothing.

## Plans

- **GaitDesk Free, Basic, Pro and Pro+** (migrations 168, 169). Free is where every company made since 169 starts: it can staff itself — managers, secretaries, its crew — but **sets up no show** until a manager or secretary picks Basic ($2 an exhibitor) or Pro ($4) on My Company. **Choosing is agreeing to the price, and nothing is charged for it**: each show is invoiced after it runs. **Pro+** is $1,499 for a year of unlimited shows and exhibitors with every Pro feature, for a company running more than one mid-sized show a season. Companies that existed before 169 kept their plan (Pro, from 168's grandfathering).
- **Free is enforced where a show is made, not where it is offered.** `show_setup_company_for` runs on both paths that create one — `POST /shows` and a show bill's Create — and 403s a caller none of whose companies sets up shows, naming the organization and saying nothing is charged until after the show; among those that do, it picks the company as `default_show_company` always did, so a freelancer whose own company is on Free and whose club is on Basic sets the show up under the club. **Moving a show onto a company on Free is refused too** (Step 1's company picker), or it would be the way round. The New Show page shows the plan chooser (`ChoosePlanPanel`) in place of Step 1 to a caller who cannot (`can_set_up_shows` on `GET /users/me/features`), because that is where a new show manager lands from `/welcome`; the button on `/admin/shows` reads *Choose a plan to create a show*. An ADMIN is never refused.
- **So is the settle-up rule** (migration 173): `show_setup_company_for` also refuses while the chosen company has a finished Basic or Pro show not yet paid for, and Step 1's company picker refuses moving a show onto such a company. `GET /users/me/features` carries `settle_up_message`, which the New Show page shows in place of Step 1 with a link to My Company. The rule and its reasons are in [payments.md](payments.md).
- **The company picks Basic or Pro itself; Pro+ is bought up front** (`PUT /my-company/{id}/plan`, `PlanChoice.agree` required; since migration 173 the agreement includes paying for each show before setting up the next, and with Stripe set up Pro+ is bought on My Company — see [payments.md](payments.md); without it, it is still asked for). Basic and Pro cost nothing until a show runs, so there is nothing for GaitDesk to approve. Pro+ is a year GaitDesk bills for up front, so it is started **by a GaitDesk admin** — `record_pro_plus`, from *Start a Pro+ year* on the company's page; a year started *invoice later* is then put on a GaitDesk invoice, and paying the invoice marks it paid (migration 172) — and is asked for like a feature (`PRO_PLUS` is in `REQUESTABLE`; one request per company; recording the year answers it). Moving a company back to Free is an admin's.
- **Pro+ is a prepaid year, not a value of `plan`** (`show_company_subscriptions`). `plan` is the per-exhibitor plan the company is on whenever no year covers today, and returns to when one runs out unrenewed: `effective_plan` is Pro+ while `starts_on <= today <= ends_on`, else `plan`, and every feature lookup (`effective_plans`, `company_features`, `show_features`) reads it. **A Free company buying Pro+ is moved to Pro as well**, so a lapsed year leaves it still setting up shows with the features it has been using, billed per exhibitor; a Basic or Pro company keeps what it chose, and may change it during the year (*Change what follows Pro+*). A new year runs from today, or **from the day after the current one ends** when a renewal is paid early, to the day before its anniversary (`next_pro_plus_term`).
- **A GaitDesk admin can put a company on any plan without a payment.** (A Pro+ year started *invoice later* is invoiced the moment it starts, and the company emailed a link to pay it — see [payments.md](payments.md).) Basic, Pro and Free are the admin's plan picker on the list and the company's page, as ever — nothing about them is paid up front. **A Pro+ year is started with its payment as a separate fact** (`payment_received_on`, migration 171): *payment received* (today), *not yet — invoice later* (NULL: the year is in force at once and shows *Unpaid*, with *Mark paid*, `PATCH /show-companies/{id}/subscriptions/{sid}`, for when the money comes), or *complimentary* (a price of 0, never owed). Being paid for is a fact about the money, not about what the company may use, so nothing a gate reads looks at it. The usage statement lists an unpaid year with its price — it is what is owed — and says *unpaid*. Every year recorded before 171 was recorded as a payment and was backfilled as paid on its recording day.
- **A show is priced at the highest plan the company held across its dates** (`show_billing_plan`, `show_company_plan_changes`). A company now changes its own plan, so pricing a show at today's plan would let it run Pro and move to Basic the night the show closed. Every change of `plan` is logged with who made it (`set_company_plan` is the one writer, from My Company and the admin's picker alike), and migration 169 seeded one row per company dated 1970. **A show whose first day falls inside a Pro+ year is covered** — nothing per exhibitor. Free counts as Basic: no show is free. Days are UTC days, the show's dates as stored.
- **The company's show managers are told** (`company_manager_emails`: its SHOW_MANAGER members, or everybody in a company with none). Moving onto Basic or Pro — by the company or an admin — and recording a Pro+ year each mail a confirmation (`plan_confirmation_email`): the plan, the price, when it is billed, what it includes, and for Pro+ the dates and what follows. **A month before a Pro+ year ends** they are reminded once (`renewal_reminder_email`): renew for another year, or on the day after it ends the company moves to its per-exhibitor plan, with what that costs and which features turn off. Best-effort like every email here, so **My Company carries the same notice** as a banner for the last 30 days (`renewal_due`).
- **The renewal reminder runs inside the API process** (`renewal_reminder_loop`, started in `main.py`'s lifespan, every six hours) — there is no scheduler. Each reminder is **claimed before it is sent**: an UPDATE that only succeeds while `renewal_reminder_sent_at` is NULL, so several workers or a restart send it once between them. Nothing is claimed while SMTP is not configured, since a reminder claimed with nowhere to go would never be sent; the banner covers that case. A year already renewed (another year picking up by the day after it ends) is skipped.
- `show_companies.plan`: **GaitDesk Basic or GaitDesk Pro, each charged per exhibitor on a show's roster** (migration 168) — $2 and $4. Basic runs the show: registration, back numbers, the gate, scribing and judge's cards, live results on the public pages, billing and payment recording. Pro adds **financial reports** (`financial_reports`), **the show bill import** (`showbill_import`), **the association results export** (`association_export` — APHA's CSV today), **the Live Boards** (`live_boards` — monitors not included) and **online payments from exhibitors** (`online_payments`, migration 172: the company connects its own Stripe account on My Company and exhibitors pay their balance from What I Owe — [payments.md](payments.md)). What each plan includes, costs and says about itself is `show_companies.PLANS` — the one place it is spelled; the admin screens and every locked door read it from the backend (`plans` on `GET /users/me/features`). The column's CHECK names the two plans and nothing else.
- **Every company that existed before migration 168 is on Pro**, so nobody lost a screen they used; the column was added defaulting to `pro` and the default then became `basic`. That also handed every one of them the show bill import, which was sold separately until then — Pro at $4 an exhibitor is the price of it now.
- **A plan is the only way to a feature** (migration 170). Migration 168 kept the per-feature switches as add-ons — one Pro feature for a Basic company — and 170 retired them: every paid feature is in Pro and Pro+, a company moves itself to Pro from My Company, and selling features one at a time as well would be two prices for the same thing. The migration deleted every switch, so a Free or Basic company that had one lost it; a Pro or Pro+ company lost nothing. Every lookup (`combined_features`, `company_features`, `show_features`) reads the plan alone, the admin's switch endpoints are gone, and the company's page lists each feature as *Included* or *Not on this plan*. **A spare own company** — the one retired when its person joins an organization — is now one with **no Pro+ year and no show**: a switch used to be what marked one as paid for, and a show is something to invoice, which the usage statement only finds through its company.
- **A Pro feature used at a show asks the show's company, not the caller's** (`require_show_feature`, `show_features`). The charge is per show, so the plan that pays for a screen is the plan of the company the show is billed to — a freelance secretary whose other club is on Pro must not bring Pro reports to a Basic club's show, and a Pro club's guest secretary from a Basic company must not be locked out of it. The show bill import is the exception, on `require_feature`: it is used before the show exists. **A show with no company** (from before migration 156, not backfilled) is on its managers' companies' plans, since those are the people who had every screen of it before plans existed.
- **What each gate covers.** Financial reports: both `/financials/reports` endpoints, and the Reports pages behind one `layout.tsx`; the Financials summary, the exhibitor accounts and payment recording stay on Basic. Association export: `GET /shows/{id}/apha-export`, with the show dashboard's APHA section saying so in place of the button. Live Boards: the Live Screens page and every board behind one `board/layout.tsx`, and `PUT /shows/{id}/marquee` — **the one endpoint only a board uses**. A board otherwise reads the public `results-index` and the gate steward's own endpoints, which every Basic show uses, so the page is where putting a show up on a wall is decided. The Show Record reports (`/reports`) are not gated: they are the show's own record, and APHA's results submission is the export.
- **Moving a company onto a plan answers its upgrade requests** for every feature the plan includes, in the same commit, as switching one feature on does. Both moves are immediate: Basic keeps the company's work for when Pro comes back.
- **The usage statement is what GaitDesk invoices from** (`GET /show-companies/{id}/usage`, ADMIN only): each show the company runs, its exhibitors on the roster (`cancellations.is_on_roster` through `billing.billable_exhibitor_count`) at the show's own billing plan (`show_billing_plan`, priced by `billing.platform_usage`), with each Pro+ year as its own line at its price. It is never on an exhibitor's bill, and the app takes no payment for it either. Only shows with `shows.company_id` set are listed; a pre-156 show with none is not on any company's statement until its office picks one.
- **An invoice is lines ticked off the usage statement, net 30** (migration 172, `POST /show-companies/{id}/invoices`). Each line names the invoice it is on (`invoice_id`, `invoice_label`), so a show or Pro+ year is invoiced once; the company pays on My Company by bank or card, or an admin marks a check paid. The rules are in [payments.md](payments.md).
- **A show's office reads its plan from `GET /shows/{id}/plan`** (`fetchShowPlan`): the plan, the features, the company, and whether the caller may ask for the upgrade (`can_request_upgrade` — a member of the show's company; a guest secretary is told to ask the show's manager). `ProLockedPanel` shows its Upgrade link only to somebody in that company (`upgradeScopeForShow`), since the plan to change is the one the show is billed to, not one of the caller's other companies. An ADMIN is told the real plan and has every feature.

## Paid features

- **Trainer Registry Pro is not a company feature** (migration 165). It is sold to one trainer, so it lives on `trainers.pro_until` — a paid-through date an admin records, with `trainer_pro_requests` as the ask — and is enforced where a listing is built (`trainer_registry.public_card`), not with `require_feature`. Same arrangement (a date, not a switch; the request is a row; every admin is emailed), different buyer — and since migration 172 the trainer can subscribe by card instead of asking ([payments.md](payments.md)). See "Exhibitor and trainer records" in [accounts.md](accounts.md).
- **A paid feature is sold to the people who cannot use it yet.** `AutomateShowCard` puts the show bill import on `/admin/shows` and `/admin/shows/new` for every role that can create a show, with the same pitch whether or not their company has it: a live *Upload show bill* button, or a lock line naming whose company is not on GaitDesk Pro (the "You must upgrade…" headline is its hover text and screen-reader text, since the pill and the link already say it). Do not hide the card from a company that does not have it — a feature nobody can see is one nobody asks to buy. **It is a strip, not a panel**: one row on a desk screen, one sentence of pitch, because it sits above the show list and above Step 1's form on every visit, and a locked company has one action rather than a disabled upload button. **That action is *Upgrade* (`UpgradeToProLink`), a link to the plan chooser on My Company** (migration 170) — the company picks Pro itself and is billed after each show, so there is nothing to ask GaitDesk for. It replaced the *Request upgrade* button, which wrote a request for an admin to answer by turning a switch on. The same link is on both show-bill pages, `ProLockedPanel` and the APHA export, and on a show's screens only for somebody in the show's own company; a guest is told to ask the show's manager.
- **A paid feature is enforced by the endpoint and only offered by the page.** Every gated door takes `Depends(require_feature(KEY))` from `backend/show_companies.py`, which 403s naming the feature; a page reads `GET /users/me/features` (`fetchMyFeatures` in `lib/show-companies.ts`) only to decide whether to show the door, so a stale answer costs an offer and never a read. **Gate every door, not the expensive one**: the show bill import gates the upload, the list, the poll and the Create, because gating only the upload would let a company that stopped paying go on creating shows from reads it already took. `/availability` stays open and answers `available` (the server has a key) and `enabled` (the company has paid) separately — they are two different messages to two different people. Do not move the switch onto `users` to save a join: the business is what paid, and a per-account flag would have to be re-ticked for every new secretary and would outlive the company's payment on everyone who left.
