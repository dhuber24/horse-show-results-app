# Show Companies, Paid Features And Office Access

Read before changing show companies, who works a show from the office, company staffing, or anything sold as a paid feature. Access checks as a whole are in [auth.md](../auth.md) (Backend Guards).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Companies and who works a show

- `show_companies` / `show_company_members` / `show_company_features`: **the business that runs shows, and what it has paid for** (migration 142). The show bill import is the first thing a customer pays for, and it is sold to the club or firm — not to one of its staff, who come and go, and not to one show, which does not exist yet when the import is used — and nothing in the schema named that business: a show is owned by the accounts in `show_managers` and nothing grouped them. So a company is a name and admin-only `notes` (the billing arrangement, since **the app takes no payment** — a GaitDesk admin turns the feature on by hand once it is settled); members are accounts, **many to many**, because a freelance secretary works for several clubs and gets whatever any of them has; and **a `show_company_features` row is the switch** — present is on, off deletes it. `feature` is a key of `show_companies.FEATURES` with no CHECK, so adding a paid feature is one registry entry plus a gate, and a key the registry no longer names switches nothing on. Each registry entry also names its **`plan`** ("GaitDesk Pro") — the one place the subscription is spelled; the locked button, the show-bill pages and the 403 all read it. Managed at `/admin/companies` (ADMIN only), where every company row carries a switch per feature; a show manager's or secretary's companies are listed on their `/admin/users/[id]` page. **Since migration 143 every show manager and secretary is in a company**: an independent gets one of their own, named after them and marked by `owner_user_id` (names are unique among organizations only), and the sign-up forms' optional Company / Organization box creates an organization or asks to join an existing one — see the Sharp Edge below. **Since migration 144 a company can ask for a feature**: the locked door's *Request upgrade* button writes `show_company_upgrade_requests` (one per company and feature, whoever pressed it), which the admin sees on the feature's row at `/admin/companies` and as a count on the `/admin` tile, and is emailed about best-effort. **Switching the feature on answers it** in the same transaction; an admin may also dismiss it.
- `shows.company_id`: **the show company that runs a show, whose staff all work it** (migration 156). A club's secretary could not open the club's own show until somebody assigned them to it by hand, show by show. Now office access is a per-show `show_managers` / `show_secretaries` row **or** membership of the show's company, and `backend/show_access.py` (`works_show`, `worked_show_ids`, `show_office_users`) is the one place that is written down — **every access check reads it**, since a check that forgot the company would lock a club's own secretary out of one screen of a show they could open everywhere else. Only the two office roles qualify through a company; scribes and gate stewards stay per show. The per-show rows remain for a guest from outside the company (a freelance secretary hired for the weekend) and for a show with no company. **Setup Step 1's Staff section is the company's staff list** (`routers/show_company_staff.py`, `ShowCompanyStaff.tsx`), so a change there changes the company on every show it runs: adding somebody is still a request an admin approves (migration 149), only the company's own members may change it, and removing somebody takes them off every show the company runs. Which company runs the show is chosen there too — a new show gets its creator's company, and moving a show is only for the current company's staff (or an admin), to a company they work for. **Not backfilled**: an existing show stays NULL, and is reached exactly as before, until its office picks one — guessing from the creator's companies today would widen who can open it.

## Membership

- **Typing a company's name at sign-up never makes you a member of it.** `show_companies.plan_placement` creates an organization for a name nobody has used, but an existing name becomes a `show_company_join_requests` row for a GaitDesk admin to approve — which the company's own managers and secretaries may approve on their side first, from My Company Staff, so the admin sees somebody stands behind it — and the person gets their own company meanwhile. Membership carries the company's paid features, so joining by name would sell a club's subscription to anybody who knew what the club was called — do not "simplify" it into an auto-join. The rest of the rule keeps everyone in exactly the right place: **joining an organization retires a spare own company** (no features on, nobody else in it — `is_spare_personal_company`), **leaving one or deleting one re-homes** anybody it leaves in none, and removing somebody from their own company, or deleting it while it is their only one, is refused. `place_in_company` runs from the two sign-up endpoints and from `_ensure_role_profile` (admin-created accounts and role changes); a new path that creates a show manager or secretary must call it too. A rename reaches their own company through `sync_personal_company_name`, which takes the old name from the caller because the first query autoflushes the rename and the listener rewrites `full_name`.
- **A company's staff manage its people, only from inside it, and never add anybody.** `routers/my_company.py` is the second door onto membership, beside the admin's Show Companies screens. **Every addition is approved by a GaitDesk admin** (migration 149): membership carries the company's paid features, so a company adding people itself could share one subscription with anybody. Asking to add a colleague, or approving a sign-up's request on the company's side, only **vouches** — `show_companies.vouch_for_member` writes or marks a `show_company_join_requests` row with `vouched_by_user_id` and `source` (`signup` / `company`), the admins are emailed best-effort (`staff_request_email`), and an admin adding the member on the company's page answers it exactly as it answers a sign-up's request. A colleague vouching again changes nothing and mails nobody. Removing, leaving and declining stay immediate — none of them hands anybody a feature — and run through the same `remove_company_member` / `decline_join_request` as the admin's buttons. **Every endpoint also refuses a caller who is not already a member**, so nobody can vouch their way into a company they are not in. Callers and colleagues are **show managers and secretaries only** (a scribe is staffed per show; an ADMIN manages from Show Companies and belongs to none), a colleague is found by the **email their account signs in with**, and the not-found answer is the same for no account and a non-staff account, so the screen does not tell anybody who else uses the app. **Features and billing notes are not in the payload at all** — the switches stand for a payment. One rule is this door's alone: **a company's own staff cannot leave it empty** (`member_removal_refusal` with a caller), since the last one out would strand its features and its join requests with nobody to answer them; an admin may. Somebody with no account signs up and types the company's name, which is the join request this screen answers — so there is no invitation flow, on purpose. Adding somebody does not ask them first; they gain features by it, and a spare own company of theirs is retired as when an admin adds them.

## Paid features

- **A paid feature is sold to the people who cannot use it yet.** `AutomateShowCard` puts the show bill import on `/admin/shows` and `/admin/shows/new` for every role that can create a show, with the same pitch whether or not their company has paid: a live *Upload show bill & automate my show* button, or that button **disabled** with "You must upgrade to GaitDesk Pro to enable this feature" and whose company it is. Do not hide the card from a company that has not paid — a feature nobody can see is one nobody asks to buy. **And the locked card is where they ask**: `UpgradeRequestButton` sits under the disabled button and on both show-bill pages, and the row it writes is the notification — `mailer.py` does nothing without SMTP, so a request that was only an email would say *sent* and could be lost, the same reason a show's inbox is a table. Its date carries `suppressHydrationWarning`: the container renders in UTC and the browser in local time, and they disagree about the day for anything filed in the evening. And **the feature switch is immediate both ways**, on the list and the company page alike: turning one off keeps the company's work, so there is nothing to confirm.
- **A paid feature is enforced by the endpoint and only offered by the page.** Every gated door takes `Depends(require_feature(KEY))` from `backend/show_companies.py`, which 403s naming the feature; a page reads `GET /users/me/features` (`fetchMyFeatures` in `lib/show-companies.ts`) only to decide whether to show the door, so a stale answer costs an offer and never a read. **Gate every door, not the expensive one**: the show bill import gates the upload, the list, the poll and the Create, because gating only the upload would let a company that stopped paying go on creating shows from reads it already took. `/availability` stays open and answers `available` (the server has a key) and `enabled` (the company has paid) separately — they are two different messages to two different people. Do not move the switch onto `users` to save a join: the business is what paid, and a per-account flag would have to be re-ticked for every new secretary and would outlive the company's payment on everyone who left.
