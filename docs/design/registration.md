# Exhibitor Registration And The Public Show Pages

Read before changing show sign-up, the registration wizard, class entry by an exhibitor, cancelling, back numbers, My Shows, or the pages a visitor sees. The desk's side of the same records is in [desk.md](desk.md); the walkthrough is in [show-workflow.md](../show-workflow.md) (Exhibitor Self-Registration).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## The roster and back numbers

- `show_entries`: show-level exhibitor record — back number (at a show that numbers exhibitors; see `show_horse_numbers` below), plus show sign-up (migration 088). Since migration 104 it also carries `preferred_back_number`, the number the exhibitor **asked for** on the class registration screen, as against `back_number`, the number the show **issued**. `registered_at` set means the exhibitor completed sign-up; NULL means it is a shell row a secretary created while adding a late entry by hand. **Class self-registration requires a completed sign-up** and returns `409 SHOW_SIGNUP_REQUIRED` otherwise. Since migration 126 it also carries `cancelled_at` / `cancelled_by_user_id` / `cancellation_reason` — **being on the roster is now two conditions, not one**, and `cancellations.is_on_roster` is the only place that is written down. Since migration 128 it also carries `stall_request` -- "put me next to the Smith barn" -- kept apart from `registration_notes` because whoever draws the stall chart reads every one of these together and nothing else, while "arriving late Friday" is read at the gate.
- `entries`: class-level exhibitor/horse registrations.
- `show_horse_numbers`: a horse's back number at one show (migration 161), read only where `shows.back_number_per = 'horse'`. One row per (show, horse), unique on (show, back_number), with `preferred_back_number` mirroring the column on `show_entries`.
- **`registered_at` alone is half a question.** Cancelling marks the row (migration 126) rather than clearing the sign-up, so every screen that asked "is `registered_at` set?" would go on reporting a cancelled exhibitor as entered, right up to the gate. `cancellations.is_on_roster` is `registered_at IS NOT NULL AND cancelled_at IS NULL` and is what `_signup_out`, the desk, Financials, `show_office` and My Shows all read. A new reader of `show_entries` has to decide which of the two it means; it is almost never `registered_at`.
- **The exhibitor asks for a back number and gets it — the app does not queue the request.** `PUT /shows/{id}/register/back-number` writes `preferred_back_number` and, when nothing else at that show holds the number, `back_number` too. A number nobody else wants is not a decision anyone needs to make, and a "preference" the office still has to key in by hand is the workflow this replaces, with an extra table. The office keeps every power it had: the desk renumbers anyone, and `preferred_back_number` survives that so staff can see "asked for 42, has 87". Open only while the show is `PUBLISHED` — after that the numbers are printed and on backs. And `auto-assign` claims requested numbers before it fills the rest, because numbering straight through 1..N would undo every request in one click. **Somebody who asks for nothing still leaves with a number**: `PUT /signup` and the desk's `POST /desk/exhibitors` call `backnumbers.assign_back_number_if_missing`, which takes the lowest number no other row holds *or has asked for*, after the sign-up has committed so a collision on the number can never cost anyone the sign-up. A requested number somebody else holds is a `409 BACK_NUMBER_TAKEN` on both doors — the desk's bulk PATCH names who holds it — and both screens render it as an alert rather than small print, since picking another is the next thing to do.
- A back number lives on `show_entries.back_number`, never on `entries.back_number` — the latter is a legacy column nothing writes, so it is NULL on every recent entry. Resolve reads through `backend/backnumbers.py` (`back_numbers_for_show` / `resolve_back_number`). Rendering `Entry.back_number` directly produces a silent column of dashes, which is exactly how it went unnoticed on four screens at once.
- **A show numbers either its exhibitors or its horses** (`shows.back_number_per`, migration 161). `exhibitor` — the default, and what every show did before — is one number per person on `show_entries.back_number`. `horse` is one number per horse on `show_horse_numbers`, as APHA SC-160.D requires: an exhibitor with three horses wears three numbers. **Keyed on the horse alone, not the (exhibitor, horse) pair**: a horse shown by a youth and then an amateur wears one number, because numbering the horse is the point of the rule. A show reads one kind or the other and never compares them, so each is unique only within its own table.
- **Read every number through `backnumbers.back_numbers_for_show`**, which returns a `ShowBackNumbers` of whichever kind the show issues: `resolve(exhibitor_id, horse_id)` for an entry, `for_exhibitor` / `first_for_exhibitor` / `label_for_exhibitor` for an exhibitor-level row. **Exhibitor-level payloads carry `back_numbers` (every number) beside `back_number` (the lowest)** — accounts, side pot rows, My Shows, the desk — because at a horse-numbered show "their number" is a list, and showing only the lowest hid the rest. Reading `ShowEntry.back_number` directly is now the same silent-dash bug as reading `Entry.back_number`, one show type over.
- **A horse is numbered the moment it is first entered** — `assign_horse_number_if_missing`, called by both entry doors (`POST .../entries` and the exhibitor's class registration) *after* the entry commits, so a collision on a number never costs anybody the entry. It is the horse counterpart of `assign_back_number_if_missing`, which does nothing at a horse-numbered show. The exhibitor may ask for a number per horse (`PUT .../register/back-number` with `horse_id`, same grant-outright rule, only for a horse they have entered); the desk edits each horse's number (`PATCH /shows/{id}/back-numbers/horses`, refused with `BACK_NUMBER_TAKEN` naming the horse); `auto-assign` renumbers horses, requests first. A number of the wrong kind is a `409 BACK_NUMBER_KIND` rather than stored, because nothing would read it.
- **Switching a show's kind hands out the new kind straight away and keeps the old.** `update_show` numbers every entered horse (or every exhibitor on the roster) that holds none, so the desk opens on numbers rather than dashes; the other kind's numbers stay where they are, unread, so switching back restores every number as it was.
- **A side pot buy-in is the exhibitor's, so adding one by back number at a horse-numbered show resolves the horse to its rider**, and asks staff to pick by name when two exhibitors show that horse. Projected payouts are keyed by `show_entry_id` as well as by number, since two exhibitors sharing a horse can share a lowest number. A futurity nomination is the horse's, so it simply wears the horse's number.

## When sign-up and class entry close

- **Two registration questions every show answers, and `shows.entry_deadline` is the first of them** (migration 159). The show office answers both on setup Step 1 (and on the new-show form, which refuses to create a show without them), and `update_show` refuses to **publish** — or to open a draft straight to In Progress — until both are answered: *the last day exhibitors can sign up online* (`entry_deadline`, inclusive) and *until when exhibitors can enter and scratch their own classes* (`self_entry_closes`: `class_start`, until each class starts, running show included; or `show_start`, until the show starts — the status turning ACTIVE, the moment the self-cancel window reads it as too). Once answered, a published show cannot blank either. `backend/self_entry.py` is the rule (`signup_open`, `class_entry_open`, `registration_window`); the preview and sign-up payloads carry `registration`, the show payload `signup_open`, and screens read those rather than comparing dates in the browser. **The deadline closes new sign-ups only** — `PUT /signup` refuses anybody not already on the roster with `SIGNUP_CLOSED`; somebody signed up keeps amending their registration until the show starts, and signing up again after cancelling is a new sign-up. **Unanswered means what the app did before the questions**: sign-up open until the show starts, class doors open until each class starts — so no show already taking entries changed under anybody, and none was backfilled. The column was **records only** from migration 123 and existed because SC-090.C counts the approval deadline back from "the show or contest entry deadline or show date, whichever comes first"; that use is unchanged, and it is the same date — a show bill's "entries close" is the day online entries close, which is also what the show bill import reads into it. It must be **no later than the show's first day** (422 on create and update), since sign-up closes when the show opens whatever the date says. It still bills nothing: do not wire it to the `post_entry` fee as a tidy-up — that is a decision about money, and a deadline added for access must not silently change what a show charges.

## The registration wizard

- **Step one of registration is judged on the exhibitor's details as this show holds them** (`backend/exhibitor_profile.py`). Contact details, date of birth, an emergency contact, one horse — and **a parent or guardian, name and phone, for anybody under 18 on the show's first day** (real age, birthday included; the row only exists once a date of birth says minor) — enforced by `PUT /signup` — prefilled from the profile and saved to the show's own copy, never to the profile (`show_registration_profiles`, below). **What blocks and what only prompts follows the health-paperwork rule**: refuse only what nobody at the desk can produce for you. Every blocking item is a fact only the exhibitor holds and can type in a minute; **association memberships are advisory**, because a membership number is a claim the desk verifies against a card and one can be bought at the counter — and the row is omitted entirely at a show with no breed or club affiliation to hold one against. The checklist is served by `GET /register/profile-status` and rides on `preview` and `signup`, so the lock on the screen and the refusal from the endpoint are the same list. Every row carries a **`step`** (`details` or `horses`), because the wizard asks them in two sittings -- the person, then the animals -- and the split is on the backend so a step cannot go green over an item the endpoint is still refusing on. The rows' `hint` text is **no longer rendered on step one**: it was a bulleted list above a form carrying the same labels and two of the same hints verbatim, which on a phone was a screenful of small print restating the boxes underneath it. Only the memberships hint still prints, in its own row, because that one links out to the profile rather than to a box on the form.
- **The profile step blocks where health paperwork flags, and the difference is who can fix it.** A missing Coggins can be produced at the desk and a membership bought at the counter, so both are warnings. Nobody at the counter can produce somebody else's date of birth — it is one box, on the person's own screen, thirty seconds of work — so `PUT /signup` refuses with `PROFILE_INCOMPLETE` naming what is short. Do not promote the membership prompt to a block on the grounds that it looks like the others: a number typed here is not a card anyone has seen, which is the whole reason `show_verifications` exists.
- **The memberships step is a step that gates nothing, and both halves of that are deliberate.** It was a line at the bottom of step one — the checklist hint and an *Add my numbers →* link to `/profile?tab=memberships` — which is exactly the mid-registration trip out to another screen that step one's own in-place form exists to avoid, and it landed somebody on a tab with no way back to the show. So it is its own step, between the person and their horses (a *horse's* papers with the same association are a different fact, checked on the horses step). What did **not** change is that `exhibitor_profile.py` marks the membership row advisory and `PUT /signup` does not refuse over it: the horses step is still unlocked by the details alone, and `initialStep` skips memberships entirely, because a wizard that opens on an optional step is telling somebody it is required. Do not promote it to a gate on the grounds that it now looks like the others — a number typed here is a claim the desk verifies against a card, and one can be bought at the counter.
- **The exhibitor's registration screen is written for a phone, and copy length is part of the design.** Every field description is one short line; the rule behind it -- an APHA citation, where a derived bedding minimum came from, what a championship class is -- rides on the element's `title` where it costs nobody a line of screen. Nothing is stated twice: the shavings ban appears at the top of the sign-up form and against the bedding quantity, and nowhere else. Layout carries as much of it as the wording -- `ReservationFields`' fee rows stack below `sm` because the stepper is a fixed ~200px and the text beside it was being wrung out in a column barely wider than a word, and the longest thing in that row is the secretary's own `notes`, which no editing here can shorten. Do not restore an explanatory paragraph under a control on the grounds that it explains something true; it is true in the `title` too.
- **A registration screen lock is never the enforcement.** `RegisterShowForm` shuts the stalls step over an incomplete profile and the hand-off to the class page over an unfinished sign-up, but both rules live in the router and 409 regardless.
- **Classes are a page of their own, not the registration wizard's last step** (`/shows/[id]/register/classes`, `ClassEntryScreen`). Signing up is done once and classes are added and dropped for weeks, so the wizard ends at sign-up and hands off, and My Shows, the show menu and a running show's *My classes* all open the class page directly; `/register` redirects there once a show is ACTIVE. **A show that sells no stalls, shavings or camping has no stalls step** (`billing.offers_lodging`, sent as `offers_lodging` on the preview, My Shows and standing payloads): the horses step's *Sign up & continue* sends `PUT /signup` with nothing booked. A show that does sell them keeps the stalls step before classes — do not "simplify" that into one rule for both; the office wants grounds counts before a ring full of horses. `/shows/[id]/signup` stays reachable at every show, because releases are signed there. The lock exists so nobody fills in a form that is going to be turned away — a screen that is the only thing stopping a bad write is one an API client walks straight past.
- **The exhibitor's class registration screen is the desk's entry form, pinned to the caller.** `frontend/app/shows/[id]/register/AddClassEntry.tsx` mirrors `admin/shows/[id]/desk/AddEntryForm.tsx` — one class picker, one horse picker, **Enter class**, committed on the press — and carries the same filtering, because a class you cannot enter is not worth offering. It posts to `POST /shows/{id}/register` rather than the staff endpoint: same form, same rules, different door, and the exhibitor comes from the session. What it replaced was every class in the show with a horse select on each row and one Submit at the bottom; do not reintroduce a batch picker, and do not make this screen the schedule again — `/shows/[id]/schedule` is the program, and it is linked from here. Two departures from the desk form are on purpose: classes grouped by show day, and inline confirmation on remove (a secretary is standing in front of the person asking; an exhibitor is on a phone).
- **One exhibitor may show two horses in the same `pattern` class, and only there.** A pattern class is judged run by run — showmanship on two horses is two runs, two scores, and two entry fees — so `entries.py` and `show_registration.py` both let the repeat through when `score_type == 'pattern'` and 409 otherwise. `entries_class_horse_uniq` still stops the *same* horse going in twice. Anything that offers a class picker has to respect this: the desk's `AddEntryForm` and the exhibitor's `AddClassEntry` both keep a pattern class on offer while that exhibitor still has a horse not already in it, and drop it otherwise. Money is per **entry**, not per class — a screen that sums `entry_fee_cents` over selected classes silently under-charges the moment a second horse goes in.
- **A Grand & Reserve class is refused, and that is not the health-paperwork rule bending.** Everything else the exhibitor door flags rather than blocks -- a missing Coggins can be produced at the desk, a membership bought at the counter. Nobody can produce a placing in a class that has not been judged yet, so `CLASS_BY_QUALIFICATION` is a 409 and the class is dropped from the picker rather than marked in it. The picker still **says how many it dropped and why**: a class that is simply absent, next to a printed show bill that lists it, reads as the app having lost one.
- **The exhibitor's door closes on a championship class; the desk's stays open.** `entries.py` takes these entries, because the office is standing at the gate when the judge calls the horses back and the app has no idea which classes feed which championship. Do not "fix" the inconsistency by refusing both -- that would leave the call-backs with nowhere to be entered at all. Same shape as every other rule where staff know something the app does not.

## One show's copy of the profile

- `show_registration_profiles` / `show_registration_horses` / `show_registration_memberships`: **one show's copy of the exhibitor's profile** (migration 145). The registration wizard used to write the profile itself — so *Remove* on a horse at one show took it off the profile and every other show's picker. Two rules now, both in `backend/registration_profile.py`: **the profile prefills a registration and is never written by it**, and **each step follows the profile until something in that step is changed** — `details_saved_at` / `memberships_saved_at` / `horses_saved_at` NULL means the step reads the profile live, set means the rows here are the answer, even when there are none. So a registration nobody edited keeps up with a profile corrected afterwards, and no pre-145 registration needed a backfill. The first edit copies that step's profile answers across (`take_details` / `take_memberships` / `take_horses`) and applies the change to the copy; saving the details form unchanged is not an edit. **Everything the show reads about the exhibitor goes through `ShowExhibitorView`** — the desk's contact, emergency contact and membership sign-offs, the association rules' date of birth and member number (`DefaultRules.exhibitor_of`, fed `exhibitor_views` in the rule context), the compliance report and the APHA export — because a screen reading `exhibitors` directly would silently drop what somebody changed for this show. **One door still reaches the profile, by decision**: a horse *created* from the horses step goes on the profile through the add-a-horse wizard, which then `POST`s it onto that registration too (`?show=`), so it is not re-created for the next show. `/welcome` has no show and writes the profile, as before.
- **Taking a horse off a profile is refused while it is entered at a show still to come.** This is the profile's own removal, on `/profile` -- the registration's *Remove* only takes a horse off that show (`DELETE /shows/{id}/register/horses/{id}`, migration 145) and has its own guard against entries and futurity nominations at that show. Removal clears the creator (or drops the rider link) and never deletes the horse -- so without the guard the entries survive, pointing at a horse that has vanished from every picker its rider can reach, and the exhibitor goes on being billed for classes they can no longer withdraw from. Scoped to shows that have not finished, because an entry at a show last spring is history and refusing forever would mean a horse could never leave a profile once it had been shown. On the endpoint rather than on the one screen with a control, so `MyHorsesPanel` gets the same protection.

## What the show asks about the horse

- **What the show will ask about the horse** (`backend/horse_eligibility.py`, migration 128): whether it holds papers with the bodies the show runs under, judged against the same `(association_id, code)` list the membership checklist is built from so the person's card and the horse's papers cannot drift apart. **Flags, never gates** -- refusing the entry would not register the horse, a number can be typed in from the phone in somebody's hand, and whether the papers describe *this* animal is a question only the desk can answer. Nothing here says a number is current or genuine. Rendered as **one line** however many bodies are short: a dual-sanctioned show produces a flag per association, and three boxes saying nearly the same thing about one horse is how people learn to scroll past the panel.
- `exhibitor_horses.relationship_to_owner`: **how this exhibitor is entitled to show this horse** (migration 128) -- APHA AM-300.E and YP-015. It was asked on the entry form, per class, from a list of twenty-five, so somebody entering eight classes on their own horse answered "Self" eight times and could answer differently on the eighth. **Derived before it is asked**: `horse_eligibility.effective_relationship` returns Self when `horses.owner_exhibitor_id` is the exhibitor, which is almost every entry ever made, so the screen states it rather than offering a picker. The stored column only carries the case the app cannot work out -- a horse somebody else owns. **Since migration 145 the registration's answer lives on `show_registration_horses.relationship_to_owner`**, prefilled from this column and never written back to it: the horses step asks once per show and both entry doors copy that show's answer onto every entry (`registration_profile.relationship_for`), falling back to this column for a horse the registration does not list. A value on the request still wins for an API caller, but neither entry form asks any more — the desk's picker went for the same reason the exhibitor's did. On the link row rather than on `horses` because two people may show the same horse and their relationships to its owner are different answers. Checked against `rules.apha.RELATIONSHIP_OPTIONS` rather than taken as free text, for the reason a paperwork verification never takes its value from the client: the relationship goes onto an entry APHA reads, and "my mum's friend" there is a relationship nobody can report against.
- **The relationship to the owner is usually not asked at all.** `horse_eligibility.effective_relationship` derives **Self** from `horses.owner_exhibitor_id`, which covers almost every entry ever made -- people show their own horses. The question survives only for a horse somebody *else* owns, where no record anywhere says whether that owner is your mother, your aunt or your neighbour: `exhibitors` holds contact details and a guardian's name, not a family tree. Even then it is asked once on the horses step and copied onto every entry. **Do not infer the remaining case** from `parent_guardian_name` matching `owner_name` -- that is a guess at identity from free text and then a guess at which parent, and the wrong answer goes onto an entry APHA reads as a statement of eligibility. A stored answer beats the derivation, so a co-owner who wrote down something more precise than "Self" keeps it; both entry doors fill blanks the same way, because a relationship only one door fills in is a compliance sheet that disagrees with itself depending on who keyed the entry.

## Unfinished registrations

- `show_registration_drafts`: **a registration somebody started and did not finish** (migration 136). The first *per-show* write in registration is `PUT /signup` at step four unless the exhibitor changes something on the first three steps — those read the exhibitor's own profile until they do (see `show_registration_profiles`). So somebody who opened a show's registration screen, found they needed the horse's Coggins and closed the tab left **no trace against that show at all**, and `GET /my-shows/`, which finds a show through a `show_entries` row or a class entry, had nothing to list. One row per (show, exhibitor): `started_at`, `last_opened_at`, nothing else. Written by `POST /shows/{id}/register/draft`, which `RegisterShowForm` fires when it opens; deleted by `PUT /signup` in the same transaction as the row that supersedes it, and by the My Shows card's *Not entering this show*. Read as `started` on the My Shows payload, filtered to PUBLISHED shows with no `show_entries` row. **Not a `show_entries` shell row** and **no progress column** — see the Sharp Edges.
- **A bookmark is not a roster row, and this is why it needed a table of its own.** The obvious home for "started registering" is a `show_entries` row with `registered_at` NULL — the shape already exists. It is the wrong one: that row is what a secretary creates while adding a late entry by hand, and it is what `GET /shows/{id}/desk` draws the office's roster from, so writing one when somebody merely *opens a form* would fill the roster with window-shoppers and inflate the `no_back_number` and `no_entries` counts staff work down. The desk's roster is people the show is expecting; `show_registration_drafts` is people who looked. The two also disappear differently — a shell row is deleted by the office, a bookmark by the exhibitor saying they are not coming.
- **The bookmark stores no progress, because progress is derivable and intent is not.** How far somebody got is `profile_checklist` plus their horse count, answered fresh on every read — the same answers the registration screen renders and `PUT /signup` refuses on, so a My Shows card cannot claim somebody is further along than the endpoint thinks. A `step` column would have gone stale the first time they completed their profile from `/profile` instead of from the wizard. What is *not* derivable anywhere is that they were looking at **this show**, and that is the whole content of the row.
- **The dismiss deletes, and reopening the form brings it back.** `DELETE /shows/{id}/register/draft` removes the row rather than marking it, and `POST` writes it again on the next visit. That is not a lost decision: somebody who opens the registration screen again has changed their mind, and remembering that they once said no would mean a bookmark they cannot get back without the office. It also keeps the table honest about what it holds — registrations in progress, not a history of shows people considered. The dismiss exists at all because the bookmark is written on *open*, so somebody who clicked Register to see what the classes cost gets one too; without a way to say no, My Shows fills with shows nobody is entering and stops being read.
- **An unfinished registration has two shapes and only one of them needed storing.** Signed up with no classes entered leaves a `show_entries` row, so the show has always been on My Shows — as an ordinary card reading "0 classes", which says the same thing as a finished registration at a show that is halter on the Sunday. `hasNoClassesYet()` derives it (`registered_at` set, `cancelled_at` clear, no entries, show not past) and the signed-up banner on the show menu **prompts rather than warns** (*No classes entered yet — Pick your classes*), because booking stalls now and entering at the desk on the day is a real thing people do. A cancelled registration is excluded: they finished and then withdrew, which is a different sentence. **My Shows itself lists each show as one button — name, dates, where** — and the button opens the show menu, which carries everything else, this prompt included: a list of full cards, each with a bill, put the second show a screen and a half down a phone. Do not put a per-show page between the two — it was built and removed as one more tap to the same doors.

## Cancelling

- **Cancelling a registration** (`backend/cancellations.py`, migration 126): the exhibitor may call it off from *Cancel my registration* at the foot of the Manage registration page **while registration is open (the show is `PUBLISHED`) and up to the cut-off the show company chose** — `show_companies.self_cancel_days_before` (migration 157), set on My Company or the company's admin page, 0 meaning until the show starts; past it, and once the show is running, the show office does it from the desk. **A cancelled registration leaves My Shows entirely** (`my_shows.withdrawn`) — and My Show Entries and Show History with it — unless the office has since entered them in a class again; signing up again brings it back. Two doors — `DELETE /shows/{id}/register/signup` and `POST /shows/{id}/desk/exhibitors/{id}/cancel` — one implementation, because the *permission* differs and what a cancellation does must not. **Marked, never deleted**: `show_entries` cascades to `show_payments`, so deleting the row to cancel would erase money that moved, which is the rule that already makes a refund a negative row. The class entries, reservations, futurity enrollments and side pot buy-ins go; the row, its back number and its payments stay, leaving a bill of nothing against whatever was paid — a **credit**, which is precisely the prompt the office needs to refund it. Refused outright once a placing or a settled-pot payout exists: at that point the exhibitor did not cancel, they competed. Signing up again clears the marker on the same row. **The office can also remove a registration outright** — `DELETE /shows/{id}/desk/exhibitors/{id}`, `cancellations.remove_registration` — however it got there, a self-registration included: everything it booked goes and then the row. Refused while any `show_payments` row is on it (`PAYMENTS_RECORDED`, pointing at the cancel), because the row cascades to the payments and a refund is never a deletion; refused on a placing or a settled pot like a cancellation. Both paths share `registration_blocker`, which since this change also refuses a registration in a *settled* pot that paid it nothing — clearing its entry would change a pool whose payouts are already written.
- **How late an exhibitor cancels themselves is the show company's choice, not the app's** (`show_companies.self_cancel_days_before`, migration 157). It was a two-week cut-off for everybody, then briefly "until the show starts" for everybody; neither suits every club — one that draws its stall chart a fortnight out needs the fortnight, a small open show taking day-haul entries does not. `cancellations.may_self_cancel` is the rule: registration open (`status == PUBLISHED`, the same door `_load_published_show_or_403` guards) **and**, where the company set a cut-off, today on or before `start_date − days` — **inclusive of the deadline day**, because "fourteen days' notice" is met by cancelling exactly fourteen days out. 0, the default, is until the show starts and is what every show did before the column. **A show reads its company's value on every request** (`cancellations.self_cancel_days_before`, one scalar query) and never copies it, so a change reaches every show the company runs at once; a show with no company (`shows.company_id` NULL, migration 156) reads 0. It is **written outright by the company's own managers and secretaries** on My Company (`PUT /my-company/{id}/settings`) — unlike a new member it hands nobody a paid feature — and by a GaitDesk admin through `PATCH /show-companies/{id}`. Capped at 90 days by a CHECK. The money point is handled by the row, not the window: payments stay on the cancelled registration as a credit for the office to refund. The office's door has **no** window at all: a truck that breaks down on the Friday still has to come off the stall chart.
- **The closed cancellation window renders a destination, not a disabled button.** A greyed-out control with a tooltip is how somebody ends up ringing round to find out whether they are still entered; the screen shows the show office's contact link instead. Same reasoning as the show menu's sign-up bar becoming *Sign up — message the show office* once online sign-up closes, rather than a dead Register button.

## Public pages

- **`/shows/[id]` is every show's main page, and one menu for everybody — signed in or not.** It is the public results hub (`ShowHub`; what it offers to whom is `lib/show-hub.ts`): the show's six sections — Class Schedule, Patterns, Results, Leaderboard, Show Bill, Show Details — then *Message the Show Office*, the same block for every reader. It was three pages describing the same show three ways: a signed-out visitor got an event-details card and a row of links (`VisitorShowView`), a signed-in exhibitor tiles that came and went with the status (`ExhibitorShowHub`), and `/live` a third menu — and a section one of them offered was missing from another. The one exception is a scorer's, below.
- **The sections are offered whatever the show's status.** Each section page says for itself when it has nothing yet ("No patterns have been posted", "No points yet"), which tells somebody more than a tile missing until the show starts.
- **Every section's *Back to Show Menu* goes to `/shows/[id]`** (`showHubBack`, at the top and again at the foot of the page), so a reader comes back to the menu they left. The one exception is a scorer on a running show, who comes back to their class list even after leaving from `/live`.
- **`/live` renders the same menu rather than redirecting to `/shows/[id]`.** At `/shows/[id]` a scribe or an admin gets the class list they score from while the show runs, and the office's *Public Results* tile opens `/live` to see what the public sees; the Active Shows list links it too. One component and one loader (`loadShowHub`), so the two cannot drift apart again.
- **`/live`, `/schedule` and `/results` stay public.** People reach them at the rail without an account — the results board's QR code opens `/results` — so don't close them.
- **A scorer's show page is a class list while the show runs, and the menu otherwise.** ADMIN and SCRIBE get the old class list at `/shows/[id]` only while the show is ACTIVE, when the class numbers *are* the menu — each row links into a scribe screen. Before the show and after it no row can be scored, and the list was forty rows of something to read under a "Read-only" banner, so they get the menu like everybody else. For everybody else the class list is one tile away at `/shows/[id]/schedule`, which was built for it.
- **What is an exhibitor's own goes above the same menu, never instead of it.** `ExhibitorStatusBanner` when there is a standing to report — cancelled, signed up, or entered by the office — then a grid of their own tiles above the show's, so the show's tiles never move between one reader and the next.
- **An exhibitor's own tiles follow their standing.** Once signed up: Add/Drop Classes and My Registration while the show is PUBLISHED, and Add/Drop Classes — or *My Classes*, where class changes stop at the show's start — once it runs. What I Owe when they are signed up or the office has entered them, the show over included; not for a cancelled registration, whose classes are gone.
- **An exhibitor with no standing gets the visitor's page**, less the sign-in line at its foot.
- **The sign-up bar follows the show's state, for everybody it has no better answer for** (`lib/show-hub.ts`). A visitor, and anybody signed in with no exhibitor record, gets the same bar while the show has one: this is the public's page, and the office checks it from `/live`. An exhibitor gets it until they are in — not once signed up, nor where their banner carries its own way back in (*Register again*, *Complete sign-up*), which would put the same destination twice one above the other, nor for somebody the office entered once the show runs.
- **The status banner says nothing to an exhibitor with no standing.** Two of its three not-yet states (open, past the deadline) put a second sign-up button directly over the bar, and the third (closed) repeated what the bar or the status badge already says; the bar is their way in, as it is a visitor's.
- **Somebody signed in with no exhibitor record who follows the bar is told how to get one, not refused.** `/shows/[id]/register` turns the preview's `403 Only exhibitors can self-register for a show` into *This account isn't set up to enter shows*, with a link to `/welcome` — where *I also compete* creates the record — that comes straight back. Only for a 403 while the show takes sign-ups (PUBLISHED or ACTIVE), because the preview refuses on the show's status first, and only after the 403, so a backend that is down still reads as down. Hiding the bar from them instead would have made the office's check of the public page differ from what the public sees.
- **The show bill is public and must stay on the menu everybody gets.** Every fetcher behind `/shows/[id]/showbill` is anonymous, so the page works without an account — and who is judging a show, what runs when and what it costs are the questions somebody asks *before* deciding to enter. The signed-out page linked to Show Details for this while Details carried the bill inline; the bill came off Details, so the tile goes to the bill. This does not reopen the class list on `/shows/[id]` itself: that page is a menu of things to do, and the show bill is one of them.
- **Every show the public can see offers a sign-up link, and an in-progress show's goes to the office.** `lib/show-signup.ts` decides it: PUBLISHED is the registration flow **while `signup_open`**, and PUBLISHED past its last day to sign up online (migration 159) or ACTIVE is the contact form opened with `?about=entering` (subject filled in, a line saying online sign-up has closed and to ask whether the office still takes entries), COMPLETED has none. The status badges read *Online Sign-Up Closed* rather than *Open for Registration* past the deadline, and the registration screens show `SignupClosedNotice` to anybody not signed up. It is on both show lists and the show menu, and never a disabled button — the office often does take a late entry at the counter.
- **The public class schedule carries no placings.** It is the programme — what runs, when, and who is in it — and results are read on the Results page; the schedule used to mark a class *results posted* and link to its placings table. The class results page's back link goes to Results for the public, and for scorers too unless the show is running, when they came from their class list.
- **The show menu carries no shavings policy.** It is on the sign-up screen, where the bags are actually ordered, and on Show Details and the show bill only where the show sells bedding (a `per_bag` fee).
- `?next=` redirect targets are sanitized by `safeNextPath()` (`frontend/lib/safe-next.ts`) before use: same-origin absolute paths only. The value comes from a URL a stranger composes, so `//evil.com` is an open redirect, not a path.
