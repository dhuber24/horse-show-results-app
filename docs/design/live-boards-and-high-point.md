## The live boards and the marquee

- `show_marquees`: **what the marquee along the bottom of the live screens carries** (migration 139) — `results` (the default, and what no row means), `message`, `both`, or since migration 158 `high_point` — the high point standings, a line per division, the same top five as the split board (`highPointLines`), offered on both boards' pages because the marquee is one setting for the show; refused at a show with no points chart, the page greying it out with a `title` from `high_point_available` on the marquee payload, and the Results Board's page fetches the leaderboard only while it is chosen — plus one line of text. Set on the Results Board page (`/admin/shows/[id]/board/results`, opened bare — reached from the Live Screens page at `/board`) by the show office through `PUT /shows/{id}/marquee`, and **stored on the show rather than in the browser**, so a running board picks a change up on its ordinary 12-second poll wherever it is. The message survives a switch back to `results`; a message mode with nothing typed is refused, and `effective_mode` on the public `GET` falls back to `results` regardless, because a marquee must never scroll a blank band. Its own table rather than columns on `shows`, since `_serialize` builds the show payload by hand and this is not show configuration. **The board's header also carries a QR code** (top right, `components/QrCode.tsx`) to the show's public **Results** page, `/shows/[id]/results` — the at-the-rail screen that stays public precisely so a phone can open it with no account. The URL is built from the address the board itself is on, so it is right in every environment. **The board can run on a monitor plugged into the laptop or tablet, extended or duplicated.** *Extend screen* on the Results Board page opens it in a window of its own (`openOnSecondScreen`) — opened first, while the press still counts as a gesture, then moved and sized onto the other monitor through the Window Management API (Chrome and Edge; elsewhere, or with the permission refused, it stays a window to drag across). The board in that window (`?popout=1`) asks for one press on **Fill this screen**, since a browser goes full screen only on a press in that window. The device it came from stays usable. *Duplicate screen* is for a monitor mirroring the device — a tablet mounted on the back of a TV, which cannot extend: the board starts in this tab and asks for full screen on the same press, so the monitor shows the board and not the browser's bars; `?duplicate=1` makes it ask again whenever it is not full screen, and it holds a screen wake lock (`useWakeLock`), because a tablet that auto-locks takes the mirrored monitor dark with it. **A second board, *Results & High Point*** (`/board/results-high-point`), is the same `LiveBoard` with `layout="split"`: the class on the left two judges' cards at a time, the show's high point standings on the right two tables at a time — each a division, rows laid out like placings with the rosette and the back number (`topStandings` in `lib/high-point.ts`), both halves on one scale and one clock — so everything above applies to it unchanged. The QR code is drawn from the symbol's module matrix in the board's own colour tokens rather than taken as the library's SVG string, which carries literal colours.

## High-point standings

- `point_systems` / `point_system_awards` / `show_point_systems` / `circuits` / `circuit_shows`: **high-point standings** (migration 147). The public Leaderboard was a "coming soon" card that computed nothing. **A points system is data, never code, and **the associations' own charts are entered from their published rules and nothing else** (migration 151, GaitDesk standard systems): APHA SC-060.A.1, ApHC rule 701.A, FQHR's F.Q.H.R. Points, NSBA's traditional and year-end charts, and WSCA's color points and Championship Show points, each citing its rule and edition in `notes`. **AQHA is deliberately absent** — its rulebook could not be read from an American source, and a chart copied from elsewhere would be read at the rail as AQHA's; MNSPHC publishes no chart. Do not add a chart the association has not published** — every association scores its own way, most of them scaled by class size, and a chart typed from memory under APHA's name would be read at the rail as APHA's. **It belongs to a show company** (`point_systems.company_id`, migration 148): the company's managers and secretaries enter the charts their shows score by at `/admin/point-systems`, and **only the company's own people see it or can choose it** for a show or a circuit (`high_point.may_use_point_system`; another company's system is a 404, not a 403, since its name is that company's business). A GaitDesk admin sees and manages every company's. **A system under no company is a GaitDesk standard one**: every company sees it and may choose it for a show or circuit (`high_point.may_use_point_system`), but only an admin may change or delete it (`may_edit_point_system`; a company gets a 403 telling it to use the chart as a template instead, and the screen offers *Use this template*). Names are unique within their owner, so two clubs may each keep an "APHA Open Show Points". What is scoped is who may *pick* a chart, never who may read one: the public leaderboards print whatever chart a show scores by. A show already on another company's system keeps it — the picker shows it by name as *another company's* — until somebody chooses again. **Deleting a company deletes its systems, and is refused while one still scores a show or circuit** (`routers/show_companies.delete_company`): left behind with no company, a club's private chart would read as a GaitDesk standard one and appear in every other company's list. `ON DELETE SET NULL` stays as the backstop, not the path. `point_system_awards` is the chart as bands: from a class of `min_entries` horses up, `place` earns `points` (NUMERIC, because charts award half points); **the largest band at or below the class size applies** and a class smaller than the first band earns nothing, which is how a printed chart's "11 or more horses" row reads. **A row is a range of class sizes, and pays as many places as the largest class in it** (migration 152): rows are stored by their first size only, so a row runs to one below the next row's start — rows at 3, 5 and 10 are 3–4, 5–9 and 10 or more — and a 5–9 row may pay down to 9th. The last row is open-ended and pays at most its first size. `high_point.place_limits` is the rule, enforced by the router's 422 and the editor's greyed-out cells; it cannot be a CHECK because the top of a range is another row, so 152 dropped the stricter `place <= min_entries` CHECK migration 150 had added. A flat 6-5-4-3-2-1 scale is two rows, 1–6 and 7 & over. In the editor, typing where a row ends starts the next row one higher, and a cell past a row's range keeps what was typed but is greyed out and not saved — narrowing a range never wipes a row, since typing "14" passes through "1". A row with no points is refused, because saving without it would stretch the row above over its range. The show's office picks its system (`show_point_systems`, a table rather than a `shows` column for migration 146's reason; no row, no standings — the page says so rather than guessing a chart). **Since migration 153 a show keeps a chart of its own**, set in **setup Step 8, Scoring**, beside the judge cards, and on the High Point page the dashboard tile opens — the same `ShowPointChart` behind both doors. Either offers every template the caller may use plus **Custom** (an empty chart), shows the matrix before anything is chosen, and *Use this template* opens it in the same grid editor the library uses (`components/PointChart.tsx`); saving writes a `point_systems` row with `show_id` set — no company (a CHECK), one per show (a partial unique index), deleted with the show — and scores the show by it through `PUT /shows/{id}/high-point/chart`. **The template is untouched**, so a later correction to it does not move this show's standings. **A show's chart is never in the library**: `may_use_point_system` / `may_edit_point_system` refuse it for everybody, an admin included, so it is not listed, not offered to another show or a circuit, and not editable at `/point-systems/{id}` — without that it would read as a GaitDesk standard one, having no company. Moving the show off it deletes it, since nothing else can reach it. In the wizard, leaving the step with the editor open saves it like every step (`useRegisterStepAutosave`), but only when there is something to save — a template just opened, or a chart that differs from the saved one. `GET /shows/{id}/high-point` reads the chart without the standings, for the wizard; the public leaderboard computes every posted class to answer the same question. A show still on a library system as it stands keeps it, with *Customize for this show* to take a copy. `show_point_systems.point_system_id` is NO ACTION rather than RESTRICT, so deleting a show can remove the choice and the chart in one statement. A **circuit** is a season: several shows added up under the circuit's own system, managed by whoever created it (or an ADMIN), a show added only by somebody who works it, and removable by the show's own office. **The standings are derived on every read, never stored** (`backend/high_point.py`), so posting a class moves the leaderboard and changing the chart rescores everything. The rules, pinned by `tests/test_high_point.py`: only **posted** classes count; **each judge's card earns its own points** (how breed points are earned — not a combined result); class size is the class's non-withdrawn entries; only a placed card earns (`placings.is_placed`); a tie earns both horses that place's points; standings are **per division, per horse and rider, added up across every discipline** — Amateur Halter and Amateur Western Pleasure both count toward one Amateur table — matched by name across a circuit's shows, and the show's leaderboard carries each exhibitor's back number there (a circuit's does not: one exhibitor has a number per show), ranked 1, 2, 2, 4. Admin screens: `/admin/point-systems` and `/admin/circuits` (ADMIN, managers, secretaries), and a **High Point** tile on the show dashboard. Public: `/shows/[id]/leaderboard` (refreshes itself while ACTIVE) and `/circuits/[id]`.
