# Accounts, Roles, People And Horses

Read before changing roles, sign-up and `/welcome`, the profile, exhibitor or trainer records, judges, horses and who may change them, or reading horse documents. Login, guards and password reset are in [auth.md](../auth.md); document reading is in [document-extraction.md](../document-extraction.md).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Roles

- `ADMIN`: full access, including every paid feature without belonging to a show company. Only an ADMIN creates show companies, staffs them, and turns their paid features on.
- `SHOW_MANAGER`: requests and manages hosted shows; can assign staff for their shows. With `SHOW_SECRETARY`, manages the **people** of the show company they work for at **My Company Staff** (`/admin/my-company`) — ask for a colleague to be added by their account's email, approve or decline somebody who asked to join, remove somebody or leave. They also choose there **how late exhibitors may cancel their own registration** at the company's shows (migration 157). **An ADMIN approves every addition** (migration 149); paid features and billing notes stay an ADMIN's too. Whoever creates a show gets the `show_managers` row automatically; a co-manager is added in setup Step 1 (`POST /shows/{id}/managers`). Removing the **last** manager returns 409 — that row is the only way a manager reaches the show.
- `SHOW_SECRETARY`: manages assigned shows, classes, entries, back numbers, and results administration. With `SHOW_MANAGER` and `ADMIN`, also works the counter at `/admin/shows/[id]/desk` — back numbers, class entries, signing off on registration papers and membership cards physically inspected, creating horses for exhibitors on that show's roster, **creating an exhibitor record for a walk-up the app has never seen** (a first and last name is the whole requirement; no account is created), and joining two records of one person where at least one is on their show.
- `SCRIBE`: enters placings and scores for assigned shows. Named for the show-world role — the person at the judge's shoulder who records what the judge calls. Renamed from `SCOREKEEPER` in migration 093; see the Sharp Edge on why this is not "Ring Steward".
- `GATE_STEWARD`: runs the warm-up side of the in-gate for assigned shows via `/gate` — per-class order-of-go (`entries.gate_order`), exhibitor check-in (`entries.gate_checked_in`), and class gate progression (`classes.gate_status`: pending/ready/in_progress/done; ready is auto-set when everyone is checked in, in_progress is set by the steward when the first exhibitor enters the ring, and on-deck is derived per ring: each ring's first not-yet-started class of the day; exhibitor check-in is only open for on-deck classes, enforced server-side). The gate screen is scoped to one show day and is also accessible to ADMIN / SHOW_MANAGER / SHOW_SECRETARY. Only one class per ring may be in progress; starting a class while another runs in the same ring returns 409 and the UI asks whether the previous class finished (yes marks it done). Classes without a ring default to the show's first ring (a "Ring 1" is auto-created when the show has none). Mistake recovery: per-entry check-in undo, undo-start (in_progress → ready), reopen (done → in_progress), and a full class reset endpoint that clears all check-ins. Assigned/invited in setup Step 1 like scribes.
- `EXHIBITOR`: views own entries/results, manages profile/horses, and self-registers for any `PUBLISHED` show (picks classes + horses) **up to its last day to sign up online** (`shows.entry_deadline`, migration 159), after which the office signs them up at the desk. **Once the show is `ACTIVE`, a signed-up exhibitor still enters and scratches their own classes** — any class that has not started, and scratching from any that has not finished — **unless the show office answered that class changes stop when the show starts** (`shows.self_entry_closes`); sign-up, stalls, horses, back numbers and futurity nominations are the office's either way. **A finished class (gate done or results posted) is the show office's alone to scratch from** (`backend/self_entry.py`).
- `TRAINER`: manages a linked trainer registry profile used on horse records.

New Show Secretary, Show Manager, Trainer, and Exhibitor registrations are currently auto-approved. The `users.is_approved` column remains as an account lock gate. Show Managers create shows directly via `/admin/shows/new`; there is no per-show approval gate.

- The score-entry role is **`SCRIBE`**, not "Ring Steward" — do not rename it again. In the horse show world a *scribe* is the person at the judge's shoulder writing down every score and penalty the judge calls; that is what this role does. A *ring steward* (ringmaster) works the arena floor — safety, halter lineup, calling gaits, carrying the signed card to the office — which is much closer to what `GATE_STEWARD` already does. Calling this one `RING_STEWARD` would produce two unrelated "stewards" and hang an arena-floor title on a scoring screen.

## Accounts and show staff

- `users`: login accounts and roles. First/last name are the editable source of truth; `full_name` is a derived display compatibility field. Since migration 102 a user may also set **one self-written security question** — `security_question` + `security_answer_hash` (a pair, enforced by CHECK), plus `security_answer_failed_attempts` / `security_answer_locked_until`, which close the *reset route* for 15 minutes after 5 wrong answers and are cleared by a successful login. This is what makes `/forgot-password` mean forgot: the old flow proved identity with the current password, which the person who needs it does not have. Email would be the ordinary answer and `mailer.py` does nothing without SMTP, so a mailed token would accept the request and lose it. See `docs/auth.md`.
- **A certification is recorded, never verified, and it must never gate a signup again.** Both show-staff registration screens used to look the signer-up's email against APHA's certified list, and the secretary's **refused to submit without a hit**. That is three failures in one control. It turned an unverifiable claim into a hard stop — GaitDesk holds no standing with APHA and cannot tell a real certification from a typo, which is the same reason an exhibitor's membership is advisory and a Coggins flags rather than blocks. It turned away real secretaries whose email on APHA's list is not the one they sign up with, an outage with no way through it. And naming one registry on the only screen every new staff member sees told the AQHA, ApHC, FQHR and unaffiliated shows this app equally serves that they were in the wrong product. `PUT /users/me/certifications` refuses nothing but an association id that does not exist, which is a malformed request rather than an unverifiable claim. Do not reintroduce a lookup as a gate; if one is ever wanted as *information*, it belongs beside the row and not in front of the button.
- `show_secretary_certifications`: **which associations a show manager or secretary says they are carded with** — one row per `(user_id, association_id)` plus an optional `secretary_id_number`. **Named for the secretary because that was the only role that could record one when it was added**, and left that way: it is keyed on `users.id`, carries no role of its own, and a manager's certification is the same fact in the same shape, so a rename would be a backward-incompatible migration of exactly the kind that has taken this site down once (migration 133) in exchange for nothing anybody would notice. Written by the post-signup questionnaire through `PUT /users/me/certifications`, a **replace** rather than a delta — the screen shows every association at once, so unticking one is how a mistake gets corrected, and an add-only endpoint would give that correction nowhere to go. `backend/staff_certifications.py` holds the reconciliation. **Nothing here is verified and nothing is refused over it** — see the Sharp Edge below.
- **Show staff answer their questionnaire after the account exists, not on the signup form** (`frontend/app/welcome/`). `/register/show-manager` and `/register/show-secretary` collect a name, an email and a password; `/welcome` then asks which associations they are certified with and whether they also compete. Both halves of that split are load-bearing. The certification picker **cannot** work on a signup form: it reads `/api/associations`, which requires a session that somebody creating an account does not have, so it 401'd and rendered an empty list for every person it was ever shown to. And everything on `/welcome` is skippable, with **no "onboarding complete" column** — every question on it is optional, and a column recording that somebody skipped an optional question is one nothing would ever read.
- **The `exhibitors` row is the exhibitor permission — `users.role` is not.** `show_registration._exhibitor_for_user` looks the row up and 403s without one, and `my_shows` reads the same row, so the backend has always decided it this way; the frontend asked `role === 'EXHIBITOR'` in eight places, which is a narrower question and the wrong one. A show manager who competes at other people's shows holds one account, one role, and an exhibitor record — ticking "I also compete" at `/welcome` calls `POST /exhibitors/me` and changes no role. Read it through `loadExhibitor()` / `canActAsExhibitor()` (`frontend/lib/exhibitor-access.ts`), which is memoised per request so the navbar and the page under it share one lookup. **Not a session-token flag**: the JWT is minted at sign-in, so somebody who ticked the box would carry a stale `false` onto the very next screen. And **do not auto-create the row for every role** — `/profile` creates one only for an `EXHIBITOR` account that is missing it (an account predating `_ensure_role_profile`), because handing one to every scribe who opened their profile would give away the thing the questionnaire exists to ask about.
- **`/welcome` hands off with a full navigation, not `router.push`.** `ProfileStep` calls `router.refresh()` immediately before it calls `onSaved`, and a refresh landing on a pending push cancels it — the profile saved, the screen said "Saved.", and the person sat on step two with no way forward. It is also the right thing independently: finishing there creates server state the *next* screen renders from, and the navbar decides whether to show My Shows by reading that row. A soft push can serve it from a client cache built before the row existed.
- **A security-answer lockout closes the reset route, never the login.** Signing in with the password clears the counter, and an admin password reset clears it too. Locking the account itself would let anyone lock anyone out by guessing at their question five times — an outage handed to strangers, on the screen that exists to end outages. While locked the **question is withheld as well**, since the prompt is the half that hints at the answer.
- **The security question is never shown to an admin** — `GET /users/{id}/security-question` reports only whether one is set, when, and whether it is locked. An admin can already reset the password outright, so displaying a self-written question (which usually hints at its own answer) adds exposure and no capability. Admins may `DELETE` a forgotten one; they cannot set a replacement, because that would mean knowing the answer to somebody else's account.
- Do not create an `EXHIBITOR` user without also creating the linked `exhibitors` row.
- Do not create a `TRAINER` user without also creating or linking the matching `trainers` row.
- `cert_org_users.Org` uses a capital `O`.
- Email is best-effort and optional. `backend/mailer.py` returns `None` when `SMTP_HOST` is unset and never raises. Every flow that mails a link must also return that link for copy/paste — an undelivered email must never be the reason a horse can't change hands or a request can't be answered.

## Exhibitor and trainer records

- `exhibitors`: person/profile records, optionally linked to users. Since migration 140 a row may also say **who wrote it**: `created_by_user_id` is the staff member who typed this person in at a registration desk, and `email` is the address they were given at the counter. Both exist because an exhibitor record was only ever created two ways — somebody signed up, or somebody seeded test data — so `GET /exhibitors/names` filtered to `user_id IS NOT NULL` and the desk told staff that anybody else "needs an account before they can be entered". That is not a thing a secretary can do for a person standing at the counter with a paper entry blank. `created_by_user_id` is what tells a real accountless exhibitor apart from the seed leftovers, so the pickers can offer one without offering the other; `email` is a contact detail and **never a login** (where `user_id` is set, the account's address is the authority), and it is the strongest hint available when deciding whether an office record and a new account are the same person. See the Sharp Edge on merging below.
- **The profile screen reads the record it edits.** `EditAccountForm` (`/profile`, Account tab) writes `exhibitors` through `PATCH /exhibitors/{id}`, and so does `ProfileStep` on `/welcome`; inside a show's registration `ProfileStep` writes that show's copy instead (migration 145), so the two no longer share a writer there. The reader was the bug: `/profile` took its exhibitor from `GET /dashboard/exhibitor/{userId}`, whose payload is `{id, full_name}` because it exists to title the dashboard's entry list. So every contact box on the profile rendered **empty whatever was stored**, and a form of empty boxes sends `null` for each -- pressing *Save Changes* to correct a surname wrote nulls over the date of birth, phone, address, emergency contact and guardian that registration had just collected. It reads `GET /exhibitors/by-user/{userId}` now, the same full row `/register/preview` prefills step one from. Two rules: **a screen that edits a record reads that record**, never an aggregate that merely names it (the desk, dashboard and preview payloads all name an exhibitor for their own labels); and **a form whose boxes are the whole message only submits when a box changed** -- `EditAccountForm` diffs its exhibitor half against what it loaded, the same no-op-when-clean rule `StepAutosave` follows, because a form that renders blank for any reason other than the record being blank destroys the record on the next press. Clearing a box on purpose is still a change, so it still sends the null.
- **A rename on the account has to reach the name the show prints.** `users.first_name`/`last_name` is what `/profile` edits; `exhibitors.full_name` is what entries, back-number lists, results and the show bill print, and it was written once by `_ensure_role_profile` at account creation and never again. So renaming yourself changed the login and left every entry under the old name, with nowhere at all to correct it -- registration step one *blocks* on that row and gives it no box, because the name belongs to the account. `PATCH /users/me` and the admin `PATCH /users/{id}` call `_sync_linked_exhibitor_name`, scoped to the exhibitor row **linked to that user**: a row a secretary typed in by hand is the office's record of somebody who may have no account, and renaming it from here would rewrite a stranger who happens to share a name. `POST /exhibitors/me` exists for the same reason -- `/profile` had called it since it was written and it had never been implemented, so an EXHIBITOR whose row was missing saw no contact fields at all and the 405 was swallowed.
- `trainers`: trainer registry; first/last name mirror the linked user when present, and derived `name` remains for trainer-list display. Carries private contact, public ad-ready profile (`business_name`, location, `website`, `bio`, socials, `is_public`), compliance dates (`safesport_completed_at`, `background_check_expires_at`), and a self-attested `has_liability_insurance` flag.
- `trainer_registrations`: per-association trainer membership with `status` (`professional`/`non_pro`/`general`) and optional `expires_at`. Mirrors `exhibitor_registrations`.
- `trainer_documents`: trainer-uploaded images stored as BYTEA. Currently restricted to one `HEADSHOT` per trainer.

## Judges

- `judges` / `judge_associations` / `show_judges`: the judge registry (migration 085). `judges` is the person — name, email, phone, identified by name + email; `judge_associations` is what they are carded with, pointing at `associations` (not `show_types`); `show_judges` is only the assignment of a registry judge to a show plus `sort_order`. Show setup **picks** a judge and displays their details read-only; corrections are made on the registry (admin-only `PATCH /judges/{id}`) so one fix reaches every show that judge works.
- Judge details are not per-show data. `show_judges` carries no name or contact columns since migration 085 — read them through `judge`, and resist re-adding a per-show copy "just for this screen".

## Horses and who may change them

- `horses`: horse records with owner/trainer text, free-text `sire_name`/`dam_name` pedigree (migration 079), breed/color/pattern, registrations, documents, and APHA SPB flag. `name` is the **registered (association) name** and is required — it is what the horse is entered and published under; `barn_name` is the optional stable/call name (migration 081). Owner + sire + dam are the program columns shown on the public class schedule and the admin entry list.
- `horse_colors` / `horse_patterns`: **the two axes of a coat, and they are not one list.** Tobiano, Overo, Tovero, Sabino and the six Appaloosa patterns sat in `horse_colors` beside Bay and Buckskin until migration 116, so whoever entered the horse had to choose which half of "Bay Tobiano" to keep — and the half they dropped could not be reported to the association. A pattern is a *pattern*; `horses.pattern_id` is independent of `horses.color_id`, and either may be NULL. `coatDescription()` in `frontend/lib/horse-coat.ts` is the one place they are joined for display — rendering `color_name` alone silently hides the half that identifies the horse across a warm-up pen.
- `horse_access_requests`: consent, pending, for a horse changing hands (migration 087). `kind='link'` is someone asking the owner to put their horse on that person's profile; `kind='transfer'` is the owner handing ownership over, which the recipient must accept. `approver_exhibitor_id` is always whoever must press the button, so approve/decline is one code path. The token is emailed **and** shown for copy/paste — see the mailer note in Sharp Edges.
- **A horse-access link finds the request; it does not authorize the answer.** `/horse-requests/[token]` requires the caller to be signed in as `approver_exhibitor_id`, and both `by-token` endpoints 401 without a session and 403 for anyone else. This follows directly from the rule above: because the link is handed to the **requester** so an undelivered email can't strand a horse, treating possession of it as consent let somebody approve their own request to take a horse they don't own — the one thing the table exists to prevent. The link still travels by any route that works; it just picks the request out of the table rather than granting anything. A `link` request against an owner with **no account** is refused at creation for the same reason: nobody could ever answer it.
- Linking a horse someone else owns is not a one-click action. `POST /exhibitors/{id}/linked-horses` only links outright when `horses.owner_exhibitor_id IS NULL`; otherwise it returns `409 OWNER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` and the caller goes through `horse_access_requests`.
- Who rides a horse and who trains it are the **owner's** calls, and creating the record is not a way around that. `_check_horse_access` (ADMIN or `owner_exhibitor_id`) covers `PATCH /horses/{id}` and the rider endpoints; `POST /exhibitors/{id}/created-horses` drops the trainer fields unless the caller claims the horse, and opens a pending `link` request instead of self-attaching when the owner named already has an account. An open trainer field is not a small thing — naming a trainer who isn't on file *creates* a `trainers` row. See "Who May Change A Horse" in `docs/auth.md`.
- Horse age is derived, not stored.
- Deleting a horse preserves entry history by setting `entries.horse_id` to `NULL`.

## Horse documents and extraction

- `document_extractions`: one row per AI read of an uploaded horse document (migration 083) — what the model suggested (`extracted`), what the human saved (`accepted`), and which suggestions they changed (`overridden_fields`). Written *before* the document is saved and linked to it on save, in the same transaction. `horse_id` is nullable (migration 084) because the add-a-horse wizard reads documents before the horse exists; it is attached on save. See `docs/document-extraction.md`.
- Document extraction only ever *suggests*. Nothing read off a scan reaches `horse_documents` without a human pressing save — `horse_documents.expiry_date` is what the health flags are computed from, so a misread date would quietly clear a horse the office should have been chasing. The upload form's auto-upload-when-complete shortcut is deliberately suppressed once a document has been read.
- `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is optional. Unset (or an API outage, or an unreadable scan) degrades the upload form to manual entry, which is how it worked before extraction existed. Extraction must never be the reason someone can't file paperwork.
