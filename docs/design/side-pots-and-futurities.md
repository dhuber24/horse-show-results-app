# Side Pots And Futurities

Read before changing a side pot, a futurity, their entry rules, or how either reaches an exhibitor's bill. The general billing rules are in [billing.md](billing.md); the operational narrative is in [show-workflow.md](../show-workflow.md) (Side Pots, Futurities).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Side pots

- `side_pots` / `side_pot_classes` / `side_pot_entries` / `side_pot_payouts`: optional money pool spanning multiple classes; one **side pot entry** per exhibitor (the UI's name for it — staff enter a pot, and `entry_fee_cents` is surfaced as the **buy-in** so it doesn't read as the class fee), payouts written on settle. Entries are added by picking from the show roster (`GET .../side-pots/{potId}/roster` → `show_entry_id`), never by typing a back number. **`paid` defaults to true and the UI never asks**: being in the pot is what owing the buy-in means, and the buy-in is now a line on that exhibitor's show bill (`billing.side_pot_lines`) — so `paid` is about the *pool*, not about the charge. A pot whose rows all read unpaid has a $0 pool and pays out nothing while the bill goes on asking for the money, which is the right way round. The column, the standings "Unpaid" flag, and `PATCH .../entries/{id}` stay for pots that tracked it the old way. **Set up in setup Step 6, Futurities & Side Pots**, beside the futurities — a pot is a set of classes with money of its own, decided while the show is built — and **run from the Side Pots tile** on the show dashboard (`/admin/shows/[id]/side-pots`, the same `SidePotsManager`), because on the day it is desk money and live standings, not part of the published fee schedule. The tile's old home on `/admin/shows/[id]/fees` was orphaned by the setup-wizard rebuild, and until migration 154 the wizard never asked about side pots at all. Declined in setup, the tile greys out (see `show_setup_skips`). `/admin/shows/[id]/side-pots/[potId]` is a hub — pool figures plus buttons to Settings, Side Pot Entries, and Standings; **Settle lives on Standings**, next to the table it freezes.
- **A class an open side pot bundles cannot be entered by somebody who is not in the pot.** `backend/side_pot_membership.py`, enforced at both entry doors with `409 SIDE_POT_REQUIRED` and satisfied by `side_pot_ids` on the entry request, which buys in inside the same transaction — a pot joined by a separate call before a rejected entry is money owed for a class nobody is in. Four parts of the shape are load-bearing. **At least one covering pot, never all**: a class two pots bundle is a choice the show meant somebody to make, and requiring both charges two buy-ins for one entry. **A `settled` or `closed` pot never obliges**, because nobody can be added to one and the class would be unenterable by any route — which is exactly the late entry into a class whose pot has paid out. **Membership is per `show_entries` row**, so one buy-in covers every class the pot names and the second class asks for nothing. And an opt-in is **checked, not trusted** — another show's pot, a non-open pot, or a pot covering none of the classes being entered is a 422, since a buy-in for something nobody entered is a charge the exhibitor never chose. This is the one place the exhibitor's door refuses something the desk's also refuses: unlike a Coggins, the person on the screen can satisfy it on the spot, and both forms ask in the same press.
- **Scratching the last class in a pot releases the buy-in.** `release_scratched_pots`, called by **both** deletion doors after the delete is flushed -- a rule only one honoured would mean a buy-in that survives or not depending on whether the exhibitor or the office pressed the button. This is the other half of entering-is-buying-in: if the class is what bought them in, scratching it is what takes them out, and nothing at a show is paid until it settles, so there is no collected money to strand. Three things narrow it, each a way of not clearing a row somebody meant to keep. **Only pots that bundled the scratched class** -- a legacy buy-in in some other pot predates the rule and is the office's to judge, not this deletion's business. **Only where nothing of that pot is left**, read off the exhibitor's whole remaining entry list, which is what makes a second horse still in a pattern class count. **Only an `open` pot**: a settled pot's buy-in funds a pool already paid out and a closed one was frozen deliberately. The flush is load-bearing -- read before it, the scratched class still counts as an entry and nothing is ever released.
- **A side pot's buy-in is not a "per class" fee.** A `per_entry` row bills every entry in its ticked classes (see "A per class fee charges" above), while a pot's buy-in is `side_pots.entry_fee_cents`, charged only to those in the pot and covering only the classes bundled into it. One per-class amount cannot describe pots at different buy-ins, and pricing a jackpot in the Class Fees box charges everybody. Note the label reads **per class**, not "per entry": see `UNIT_LABEL` above.
- **A side pot buy-in is on the exhibitor's bill, and `side_pot_money()` is the pool rather than a second copy of it.** This reverses the older rule that kept pot money out of `build_bill` entirely. That rule's reason — folding buy-ins into a balance would make Financials disagree with the bill the exhibitor reads on My Shows — only held while the two read different things, and both read `build_bill`; what changed underneath it is that entering a class an open pot bundles *is* the buy-in, so the charge is no longer a decision made on the pot's own screen but a consequence of an entry, and **a charge nobody chose has to appear on the bill they are handed**. Until this it appeared on none: `build_bill` had no side pot concept at all, and the desk's old Side Pots section printing `$10.00 buy-in` per pot was the only place the money was visible anywhere — so removing that section left an exhibitor committed to a charge no screen showed. `billing.side_pot_lines` charges **one buy-in per pot** however many of its classes were entered (membership is per `show_entries` row), and charges **every pot they are in whatever its status and whatever `paid` says**: `OPEN_POT_STATUSES` decides whether a pot may *oblige* an entry, not whether a membership already taken is owed, and `paid` is a flag the UI never sets. The consequence to keep in view is that `side_pots[].buy_ins_cents` on the Financials payload and the pot money inside `totals.billed_cents` are now **the same money answering two questions** — what the pot will pay out, and what the exhibitor owes. Do not add them together, and do not "fix" the apparent duplication by removing either.
- **The desk has no side pot section, because entering the class is the buy-in.** It had one per-pot *Add to pot* / *In the pot* toggle per exhibitor, and once a class an open pot bundles could only be entered by somebody in that pot, the toggle was a second way to do what the entry form now does as part of the entry — and the more misleading of the two, since it could put somebody in a pot whose classes they had never entered. What stays on the panel is the **count** on the summary row, plus a buy-in line per pot in the Classes section — the money the removed section used to print, now quoted off the bill that charges it rather than summed in the browser. What the payload carries is unchanged — `side_pots` (with `class_ids`) and each exhibitor's `side_pot_ids` are what `AddEntryForm` reads to know which classes oblige a buy-in and which pots they are already in, so **do not strip either from `GET /shows/{id}/desk`**. Buying in or out on its own is the pot's own Entries screen, where the pot is the subject and the roster is the list. And the gap this opened is closed rather than documented: scratching the last class an exhibitor holds in a pot now releases the buy-in at both deletion doors. See the Sharp Edge below.
- Settling a side pot is irreversible — `status` flips to `settled`, payouts are written, and edits/deletes are blocked.

## Futurities

- `futurities` / `futurity_fee_tiers` / `futurity_membership_options` / `futurity_classes` / `futurity_divisions` / `futurity_division_classes` / `futurity_entries`: a futurity programme within a show (migration 107, entry form in 109). It was a single `show_fees` row with code `futurity` — one flat per-entry amount next to the jackpot fee — and that cannot describe one. A real futurity prices the same class **three ways depending on how the horse got there** ($75 / $100 / $150), closes entries on a stated date after which every class carries a late fee, charges an office fee per horse that depends on club membership, and hands out **Hi-Point awards** over a named subset of its classes. Shaped after `side_pots`, with two differences. **An entry is an enrollment of a horse, not a bet on classes** — the futurity's classes are ordinary `classes` entered through ordinary `entries`, and the money is derived by `billing.futurity_charge_cents` as *tier × classes entered + office fee + late fee × classes*. Independent of each other but no longer *separable*: a nomination now requires at least one of the futurity's classes and is released when the last is scratched (`backend/futurity_enrollment.py` — see the Sharp Edge). Enrollments filed before that rule may still carry zero classes, billing the office fee alone, which is exactly what the desk's Withdraw exists to clear. **The futurity supplies the class price**, so a futurity class carries `entry_fee_cents = 0`; pricing one double-charges, and the UI warns rather than silently zeroing it (the fix belongs on the class). `futurity_entries.entered_at` is stored and never `now()` — same rule as `reserved_at`. Migration 109 added the other half — **what the entry form says**: the deadline to the minute (`entry_deadline_time` / `entry_deadline_timezone`, display only), `award_notice`, `rules_notice`, `entry_instructions`, `refund_policy`, `requires_horse_pedigree`, award names on each Hi-Point division, the optional club membership the futurity **sells** (`futurity_membership_options`, charged once per enrollment and *not* the same question as `is_member`, which decides the office fee), `futurity_entries.shown_by_name` for "exhibitor if different than owner", and the release as a `show_waivers` row scoped by `futurity_id`. Reached as **setup Step 6** and from the **Futurities** tile on the show dashboard — same route; exhibitors enter through `/shows/[id]/register`, which prints every one of those notices because that is where somebody agrees to them.
- **Defining a futurity is setup; taking its entries is not.** An earlier version of `docs/show-workflow.md` argued a futurity could not be a wizard step because it takes entries and is worked alongside the desk. That is true of the entries and false of the programme: the deadline, the categories, the classes and the words on the form are all decided while the show is being built, exactly like the class schedule in Step 4. So Step 6 points at the existing `/futurities` hub and its Entries and Standings screens stay desk work. The fees step no longer offers a `futurity` fee slot and must not get one back — one amount cannot say that the same class costs $75, $100 or $150 depending on the entrant's category — and it offers to delete a leftover pre-107 `futurity` fee row, because a show carrying both bills its futurity entrants twice.
- **The futurity card is the decision; the show bill is the programme.** The awards, the rules its classes run under, how the categories work and the refund policy are published in full in the Futurities section of `ShowbillDocument`, which `/shows/[id]/showbill` renders (beneath an uploaded bill too, so `#futurities` always resolves). The registration step prints only what you cannot choose without -- the deadline, the categories with their rates, the office fee, any late fee -- and links to the rest. Reprinting the programme there pushed the four controls somebody came to the screen to use below several screens of text they had already read. Do not restore it: the link is what keeps one copy of those words.
- **Ticking "I already hold a club membership" shuts off the membership picker, and changes nothing about the office fee.** `futurity_entries.is_member` selects between the member and non-member office fees; `membership_option_id` sells a card. They stay independent -- somebody joining on the day pays the non-member office fee *and* the membership, which is what the paper form charges -- and the only thing the tick does is stop offering to sell a card to somebody who has just said they hold one. Wiring the two together would quietly discount every new member.
- **The club membership a futurity sells is not the membership its office fee asks about.** `futurity_entries.is_member` picks between `office_fee_member_cents` and `office_fee_nonmember_cents` — the card the entrant already holds. `membership_option_id` is a card they are **buying** at entry, charged once by `billing.futurity_charge_cents`. Somebody joining on the day legitimately pays the non-member office fee *and* the membership, which is what the paper form charges them; wiring one to the other would quietly discount every new member and disagree with the printed bill.
- **A futurity's pedigree requirement stops an exhibitor and never the office.** `requires_horse_pedigree` refuses the enrollment on `POST /shows/{id}/register/futurities` naming what is missing, because the caller owns the horse and can add its foaling date, sire and dam in a minute. The staff endpoint reports the same shortfall as `missing_horse_details` on the entries payload and takes the entry anyway — refusing it at the counter does not produce the sire's name, which is the same reasoning that took the block off health paperwork.
- **A futurity nomination requires one of its classes, and scratching the last of them releases it.** `backend/futurity_enrollment.py`, deliberately the same shape as the side pot rule above, because it is the same relationship read from the other side: a futurity class carries `entry_fee_cents = 0` and the **nomination is what prices it**, so the two facts are useless apart. They were nonetheless entirely independent, and it went wrong in both directions. A nomination with no futurity class still owed the office fee (`billing.futurity_lines` charges it per enrollment) for a programme the horse was judged in nothing of — and the desk, where the rest of that exhibitor's money is worked, could only *link out* to the futurity screen to clear it. Meanwhile scratching the last futurity class left the nomination behind, still charging. So `POST .../entries` now refuses with `409 FUTURITY_CLASS_REQUIRED` at **both** enrollment doors, the desk grew a **Withdraw** on each futurity line, and `release_scratched_enrollments` runs beside `release_scratched_pots` at both deletion doors. **The key is the horse, not the roster row** — that is the one place it departs from a side pot, whose membership hangs off `show_entries`: two horses of one exhibitor in the same futurity are two nominations, and scratching one horse's last class must not take the other's with it. **The enroll form books the classes rather than the endpoint doing it**: the form posts each ticked class to the ordinary class-entry endpoint first — the one place an entry is validated against association rules, closed classes and any side pot buy-in it obliges — and nominates once they are in. Classes first is deliberate; the failure it leaves behind is a horse entered and unenrolled, which every desk screen already flags in as many words, where the reverse leaves the bare nomination this rule exists to remove.
- **A futurity class must carry `entry_fee_cents = 0`.** The futurity's fee tier supplies the per-class rate, because it depends on which category the entrant qualifies for and a class row cannot know that. A priced futurity class is billed twice — once in `class_lines`, once in `futurity_lines`. Nothing enforces it (the class screens know nothing about futurities), so the futurity screens warn on read instead: zeroing it server-side would hide a real mistake in the schedule, and the fix belongs on the class.
- **A futurity late fee is decided by `futurity_entries.entered_at`, never by today.** Same rule as `fee_rate_cents` and for the same reason: comparing the deadline against `now()` would drop a late fee on every enrollment already taken the moment the deadline passed, including people who entered in April. `is_past_deadline` on the exhibitor payload is the *quoting* question — what someone would pay entering right now — and is not what an existing enrollment is charged.
