# Billing: Fees, Bills And Payments

Read before changing `backend/billing.py`, a fee unit, a fee editor, the Club Sanctioned Fees box, the bill on My Shows or the desk, payments, or a financial report. Side pot and futurity money has its own file, [side-pots-and-futurities.md](side-pots-and-futurities.md). The workflow narrative is in [show-workflow.md](../show-workflow.md) (Financials, My Shows and the Bill).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## Where money is computed

- Money is computed in one place: `build_bill()` / `charge_lines()` / `fee_rate_cents()` in `backend/billing.py`. The class-registration screen, the sign-up screen, the My Shows bill, and the Financials screen all read it. Do not re-derive a total in a router or a component. The show-wide rollup is `build_account()` (one exhibitor: bill + payments + balance) then `summarize_accounts()` over the results — **not** a SQL `SUM` over `entry_fee_cents`, which would be faster and would eventually disagree with the bill the exhibitor reads on My Shows.
- **`build_bill` reads `show.fees` and `show.judges` off the Show row.** Same as `sanctioning`, and every caller must eager-load both — `show_financials._get_show_or_404`, `show_registration._load_published_show_or_403`, and both queries in `my_shows`. An unloaded relationship is a MissingGreenlet 500, which is loud; defaulting to "this show has no fees" would silently under-bill an entire show, which is not.
- **A show fee bills only if its unit says how.** `build_bill` has one branch per family: reserved units come through `show_entry_reservations`, automatic units through `billing.charge_lines`, and everything else through nothing at all. That last group is not an oversight. `flat` cannot bill because its *occurrence* is not derivable — a stall cleanout penalty applies to whoever left a mess, and no query answers that — and `per_class_per_horse` / `percent_of_entry` stay out of `AUTOMATIC_FEE_UNITS`. `per_entry` used to stay out too, on the grounds that a class fee on top of `classes.entry_fee_cents` doubles a class; it moved in once show managers priced classes that way in Step 7, and the double charge is now a catalogue that must not state one price in both places. When adding a unit, decide which family it is in first; a unit in no family is a line on the printed price list and nothing more.
- `show_fees`: the show's non-per-class-entry fee catalog (stalls, shavings, camping, late/post entry, surcharges, and the show's own per-horse or per-judge charges), each with an `amount_cents` and a `unit`. **The unit decides how the row bills, and there are three families** (migration 112 puts them in a `COMMENT` on the column). *Reserved* — `per_stall` / `per_bag` / `per_night` / `per_day` / `per_show` — are quantities the exhibitor books at sign-up, bill through `show_entry_reservations`, and all but bedding may carry an **early-bird rate** (`early_amount_cents` + `early_deadline`, migration 092, a pair that only counts when both are set; `amount_cents` is always the standard, post-deadline rate) — a bag count has no reserve-early convention the way a stall or a camping spot does, so `EARLY_RATE_FEE_UNITS` leaves it out. *Automatic* — `per_exhibitor` / `per_horse` / `per_judge_per_horse` / `per_judge_per_exhibitor` / `per_judge_per_entry` / `per_entry` — are charged to everyone who has entered a class, derived from their **entries**, their distinct horses and the size of the judge panel by `billing.charge_lines`; nobody books them and there is nothing to tick. Every automatic row counts only the breed association's own classes — see below. *Neither* — `flat` / `per_class_per_horse` / `percent_of_entry` — is published price-list text and bills nobody; `per_class_per_horse` is withdrawn from the picker and survives only on rows priced before. A reserved row may also carry a **`min_quantity`** (migration 128) -- on a bedding line only, see the Sharp Edge below -- the fewest an exhibitor must book, which is what a show banning outside shavings needs in order to mean anything. It is a *requirement*, checked against the whole booking rather than line by line: zero and an omitted line are both below it, because leaving the line out is the easiest way to book none of something. A show that takes day-haul entries leaves it unset. Staff set the reserved ones in setup Step 3 and on `/admin/shows/[id]/fees/boarding`, and every class fee — the office charge, the other automatic ones, every fee quoted per class, anything else a manager names — in Step 7's **Class Fees** box and on `/admin/shows/[id]/fees/entry`, which also folds in the per-class entry-fee table. Step 7 edits nothing else: the **Club Sanctioned Fees** box moved to Step 5, where the clubs are picked and their classes designated, because a different body's money on a different set of classes is meaningless apart from which classes it applies to. It does *quote* them — the step comes after Sanctioning and Futurities, and closes with each club's rate and approved-class count and each futurity's categories, office fee and late fee, read-only, with a link back to the step that sets them (`setup/fees/EarlierFees.tsx`). **Below** the Class Fees box, not above it: the step exists to set the show's own fees, and opening on a screen of somebody else's read-only figures pushed the one box a manager came to fill in down the page. Both routes render the same `ShowChargesEditor` — the office charge among the rows, since migration 132 made it an ordinary fee — with `frontend/lib/fee-units.ts` holding the one copy of the vocabulary. A fee added there is written with a `sort_order` **below every existing row**, so it lands at the top of the list rather than under a dozen others; each row carries a **Note for the show bill**, which is where a scope or an arithmetic the row itself cannot express gets written down, and what a row *charges* is the unit picker's tooltip rather than a line of prose under every fee. **The vocabulary says "class", not "entry"**: `UNIT_LABEL` renders `per_entry` as *per class* and `percent_of_entry` as *% of class fee*, because a show fee is charged against a class — the word on every show bill — and "per entry" sitting beside "per judge, per class" was two names for one thing. The column values are untouched. No class fee is set up anywhere else.
- **Changing a fee's unit can move it between billing families, and nothing used to say so.** The MNSPHC catalogue's class-rate rows were seeded `per_entry` — published price-list text that bills nobody, because `classes.entry_fee_cents` is what charges per class. Somebody then corrected them in the editor to the units the rows' own notes described: "$45 per judge × 4 judges" genuinely *is* `per_judge_per_horse @ $45` rather than a hand-multiplied `per_horse @ $180`. That instinct was right and the consequence was not — four rows moved from the family that bills nobody into the family that bills everybody, on top of the class fees they duplicated, and a $36 amateur class billed $552. Two rules fall out. A class rate lives in **one** place: the class row's `entry_fee_cents`, or a `per_entry` ("per class") fee ticked against its classes — never both, since both now charge. `per_judge_per_entry` is for a levy *on top of* the class price (SC-125.B), not for the price itself. And an **elective bundle is not automatic**: an All Day fee replaces per-class fees rather than adding to them, the exhibitor chooses it, and nothing stored says whether they did — so it is `flat`, the family whose whole meaning is "occurrence is not derivable". Do not price one `per_horse` to make it bill; that bills everybody who entered anything.

## Fee units

- **`per_show` is not `flat`.** A flat fee is charged once however many you have; a `per_show` fee is charged once **per thing reserved**, however long the show runs. An electrical hook-up sold as "$60 for the weekend" is `per_show` — two of them cost $120, and a three-day show still costs $60 each. Pricing it `per_night` silently doubles it on a two-day show, which is why the unit exists (migration 106).
- **Camping and the electrical hook-up are one line item, priced three ways.** `per_night`, `per_day` and `per_show` are how a venue charges for the same spot, not three products. The Lodging & Boarding setup step (Step 3) offers one camping line with that choice, and migration 108 folded the `hookup` fee code back into `camping`. It was two slots, which asked the manager which product they were selling and let one who answered both put two camping charges on the same bill with nothing to say so. On the exhibitor's picker all three units sit under one "Camping & hook-ups" heading, with the noun (`nights` / `days` / `spots`) against the number being typed — that, rather than a separate heading, is what stops someone booking two *nights* of a $60-for-the-weekend hook-up.
- **A day is not a night.** `per_day` (migration 111) exists because some venues sell the electrical hook-up by the day, and a Friday-to-Sunday show is three days and two nights. Pricing a per-day rate as `per_night` is not a rounding difference — it is one unit's worth of money on every camper, in the direction the exhibitor notices. `build_bill` multiplies rate × quantity and never reads the unit, so nothing downstream can recover the difference; the unit has to be right at the point the quantity is typed, which is why the noun sits against the box on the sign-up screen rather than only in the heading above it.
- **A fee's price may change; its unit may not, once anybody has reserved it.** `PATCH /shows/{id}/fees/{fee_id}` returns 409 on a unit change when `show_entry_reservations` rows exist. `build_bill` multiplies rate × quantity and never reads the unit, so flipping camping from per night to per show turns "3 nights" into "3 spots" and reprices every booking with nothing in the data to record it — where a price change is decided honestly by `reserved_at`. `ShowFeeOut.reserved_count` (staff list endpoint only; the public price list does not report how many people entered) is what lets both fee editors lock the control rather than offer it and then refuse. Changing how a line is charged means removing the fee and adding it again, which drops the reservations in the open.
- **"Per judge" does not say what it multiplies, so the unit does.** `per_judge_per_horse` and `per_judge_per_exhibitor` differ by however many horses somebody brought — three judges at $5 is $15 or $30 — and migration 112 split the old `per_judge` for exactly the reason `per_day` was split from `per_night`: `build_bill` multiplies rate × quantity and never reads the unit, so nothing downstream can recover a unit that was wrong where it was chosen. Do not re-merge them into a "per judge" with a basis flag elsewhere.
- **And "per judge" means that association's judges, not the panel.** The MNSPHC show runs four APHA judges, two of them also carded WSCA, and a WSCA fee priced per judge was multiplying by four. `billing.carded_judge_count` counts the panel judges carded with the association (`judge_associations`): a club's per-judge sanction fee counts that club's, and the show's own per-judge charges — already scoped to the breed body's classes — count the breed body's (`breed_judge_count`, whole panel at an Open show). **Nobody on the panel carded with it counts the whole panel**, because a regional club like MNSPHC cards no judges at all and its classes are judged by whoever is there; counting zero would silently drop the fee from every bill. Lines carry `judge_association_code` so the bill reads "× 2 WSCA judges" when the count was narrowed and "× 4 judges" when it was not, and the Step 5 and Class Fees previews use the same rule (`cardedJudgeCount` / `breedJudgeCount` in `lib/fee-units.ts`). A judge's carding on the registry is therefore a billing input — record it.
- **`per_judge_per_entry` is the assessment a breed body makes the show collect, and it is not `per_horse` by another name.** APHA SC-125.B: "a fee per entry per show (Judge)", forwarded to APHA before results are processed. One horse in six classes is **one horse and six entries**, so `per_judge_per_horse` bills a sixth of it and nothing downstream can recover the difference. Migration 125 adds it to the automatic family with an `association_assessment` template. It is a levy *on top of* the class price — multiplied by the judges — which is why it needs a unit of its own beside `per_entry`, the show's plain per-class fee. `charge_multiplier`'s `entry_count` defaults to 0 so a caller written before the unit bills it at nothing; under-billing a fee nobody has created yet is recoverable, over-billing a whole show is not.

## Automatic charges and the classes they count

- **Every automatic `show_fees` charge counts only the breed association's own classes.** Found on a real show bill: two hand-built `per_horse` rows named "APHA All Day fee," each carrying a note reading *"$45 per judge x 4 APHA judges, one horse, APHA classes only... All Breed (MNSPHC or WSCA) classes... are not included"* — the secretary had worked out the scope and the arithmetic by hand ($45 × 4 = $180, typed as a flat amount) because nothing on the row itself could say either. `per_judge_per_entry` (migration 125) already scoped itself unconditionally — that unit is the breed body's own per-entry assessment (SC-125.B) by definition. This used to be an opt-in flag on the other four automatic units, `show_fees.breed_association_only` (migration 130, dropped again in 131) with a checkbox in `ShowChargesEditor` — removed because there was no real case for turning it off: a show with no club sanctioning set up (most shows) has no club-sanctioned classes to exclude, so scoping is a no-op there, and it changes nothing for a show like this until it also sanctions a club's own classes. `billing.charge_lines` takes the raw entry list rather than precomputed counts, so it can compute the scoped horse/entry counts once per bill: `is_club_sanctioned_class` (a `class_sanctioning` row, migration 113) marks the classes to exclude, the same test `class_sanction_cents` already uses.
- **Every automatic charge is scoped to the breed association's own classes, unconditionally, and there is nothing to configure.** `per_judge_per_entry` always worked this way -- it is the breed body's own per-entry assessment (SC-125.B and its kin) by definition -- and every other automatic unit (`per_exhibitor`, `per_horse`, `per_judge_per_horse`, `per_judge_per_exhibitor`) now works the same way by default. Discovered on a real show bill: two hand-built `per_horse` rows carrying a hand-typed note reading "APHA classes only... All Breed (MNSPHC or WSCA) classes... are not included," because nothing on the row itself could say it and the judge multiplication ($45 × 4 = $180) had been done by hand too. This used to be a per-fee opt-in checkbox (migration 130); it was removed (migration 131) because there was no real case for the other choice -- a show with no club sanctioning set up has no club-sanctioned classes to exclude, so scoping is a no-op for it and nothing changes until it also carries a club's own classes.
- **A scoped `per_exhibitor` or `per_judge_per_exhibitor` fee needed a fourth argument, because neither has a count of its own to zero out.** `per_horse` and `per_judge_per_entry` scope by substituting a smaller number into an existing multiplication; `per_exhibitor` returns a flat `1` and `per_judge_per_exhibitor` returns `judge_count`, neither of which cares how many horses or entries there are. `charge_multiplier`'s `has_relevant_entries` parameter is the gate that lets either read `0` when the exhibitor's only entries are in club-sanctioned classes -- defaulted to `True` so a caller that predates this scoping is unaffected.
- `show_fee_classes`: **which classes a fee applies to** (migration 137). **No rows means the whole schedule** — including classes added later — which is the ordinary case and what every fee at every show had before this; rows narrow it, and an exhibitor who entered none of them owes nothing for that line. The bullet above scopes every automatic charge to the breed body's own classes — one coarse rule, and until this the only one the app had. A real catalogue is drawn far finer: the MNSPHC bill carries an "APHA Youth class" rate, an "All Breed WSCA" rate, an "MNSPHC All Breed" rate and two All Day fees split Open/Amateur against Youth, and every one of them billed **every** exhibitor whatever they had entered — a single $36 amateur class came out at $552. Deliberately the same shape as `class_sanctioning`, because it is the same fact read from the show's side rather than the club's. It **narrows and never widens**: migration 131's club-sanctioned exclusion still applies on top, so ticking a WSCA class into the breed body's own assessment does not make the assessment reach it. A reserved fee bills from a booked quantity and a flat price bills nobody, so the endpoint refuses a list on either. Set per row in the **Class Fees** box (`ShowChargesEditor`) under *Choose which classes use the fee*.
- **Every fee quoted "per class" carries a class list, every class ticked by default** — `per_entry`, `per_judge_per_entry` and `per_class_per_horse`, exactly the units whose label says *per class* (`billing.PER_CLASS_FEE_UNITS`, `offersClassList` in `lib/fee-units.ts`; a test pins the list to the labels). The manager narrows a fee by **unticking**. **The list opens the moment a per-class unit is picked** — on a new fee, or when a saved fee's unit is changed to one — because a collapsed one-line toggle under the row read as though no list existed; a fee that loads already per class starts closed, so a dozen of them do not bury the page. The show bill prints "Classes 1–3, 7" under a narrowed fee (`feeClassesText` in `ShowbillDocument`) and nothing extra under one on every class. **The list never offers a club-sanctioned or a futurity class** (`offeredForScope` in `ShowChargesEditor`, which says how many it left out): the club prices the first in Step 5 and the futurity's categories the second in Step 6. **Billing leaves the same classes out of both per-class charges** (`billing.is_futurity_class`, off the `Class.is_futurity_class` column property, on top of the club-sanctioned exclusion every automatic charge already had), so *Select all* charges exactly what the list shows. A per-horse fee is not per-class and still counts a futurity horse.
- **A "per class" fee charges** — `per_entry` is in `AUTOMATIC_FEE_UNITS` and bills once per class entered in its ticked classes, **on top of** whatever the class row's own `entry_fee_cents` carries, and with no judge panel needed. It billed nobody until a show manager priced a class in Step 7 — "$5 per class", ticked against the class — and the desk read $0: a unit offering a class list and then charging nothing is a trap, whatever the reason. Two horses in one class are two entries and two charges, which is why **`per_class_per_horse` was withdrawn** from the picker (`CLASS_FEE_UNIT_OPTIONS`): a row still carrying it keeps showing in the box with a warning-coloured *no longer charges anyone — switch it to per class* line (`chargesNobody`, `ChargesNobodyNote`) and bills nothing. **A catalogue that restates its class prices as `per_entry` rows now charges them twice** (the MNSPHC test show's $36 / $28 / $32 / $10 rows) and has to drop those rows or zero the class prices; likewise a per-class row named for a jackpot now charges every entry, when a side pot's buy-in belongs on the pot. `per_entry` is counted in `charge_lines` rather than in `charge_multiplier`, which keeps returning 0 for it so a club's `per_entry` sanction fee cannot be billed a second time by `sanction_charge_lines`. The seed templates' late and cross-entry fees are `flat` for the same reason: they apply to *some* entries, which nothing stored says. **Every class ticked is saved as no rows, never as a list of every id** — `ClassScope` is `'all' | string[]`, and ticking the last unticked box goes back to `'all'` — because a list would quietly leave out a class added to the schedule later. **Every class unticked cannot be saved** (`[]` would say every class while every box read empty), and `isDirty` counts it as an edit, so the step's Next stops on the error rather than carrying the manager on and dropping what they did; the same goes for an unparseable amount. **Per-class units left the Boarding Fees screen** — its unit picker no longer offers them and its list no longer shows those rows, which appear in the Class Fees box instead, next to the list that may narrow them. On a per-horse or per-exhibitor charge the list is not offered, unless the row already carries one from before, which stays on screen until cleared because billing still honours it. **Club sanction fees are not part of this**: a club's `per_entry` rate is charged on the classes designated for that club in Step 5, which is already its class list.
- **The Class Fees box can fill unpriced breed-association classes in bulk, and deliberately never touches three kinds of $0.** `EntryFeesEditor`'s default-price fill (`isDefaultFillable`) only ever raises a class still at `entry_fee_cents = 0`, and excludes classes that are $0 **on purpose**: club-sanctioned ones (`sanctioning_codes`, already priced by that club), Grand & Reserve callbacks (`entered_by_qualification`, nobody enters them), and futurity classes (`is_futurity_class` -- priced by the futurity's own fee tier, and a price here would double-charge every entrant). `is_futurity_class` is computed the same way `sanctioning_codes` and `procedure_note` already are on `GET /shows/{id}/classes/` -- an `EXISTS`-shaped subquery keyed on `futurity_classes`, added there rather than derived client-side. A row that is priced despite carrying either flag is marked `⚠ priced anyway` rather than silently accepted, since that combination is never supposed to happen and is worth a manager's second look when it does.
- **The office charge is a `show_fees` row, not a column, and that is what finally made it stop being a special case** (migration 132). It was `shows.office_charge_cents` + `office_charge_basis` from migration 055, when it was the app's only non-class charge -- which meant its own box, its own state, its own save button, its own function in `billing.py` and its own line in every bill payload, for a charge that is to an exhibitor one more automatic fee beside a drug fee. Migration 112 had already added `per_exhibitor` to `show_fees.unit` *because* `per_back_number` was "a basis for that one charge rather than a unit any fee can carry"; 132 completes that -- `per_back_number` is `per_exhibitor`, `per_horse` is `per_horse`, and `charge_lines` already billed both. Two consequences worth knowing. It is now **renamable and removable** like any other row, and a show that wants one adds it with **+ Add a fee**. And it is **scoped like every other automatic charge** (migration 131), so an exhibitor entered solely in a club's All Breed classes no longer owes it -- one rule applying to one more row rather than a new exception. Moving it back to a column would reintroduce every one of those bespoke code paths.
- **There is no "Standard class fee" and no separate "Jackpot" slot on `show_fees` any more.** Both were `per_entry` rows pinned to fixed codes (`standard_class`, `jackpot`) in a dedicated section of the fees step, and both were published price-list text that billed nobody -- what an entry is actually charged is `classes.entry_fee_cents`, and a side pot's buy-in is `side_pots.entry_fee_cents`. The slots added a second, narrower "class fee" concept next to the real one for no computed benefit. A jackpot's buy-in belongs on the side pot itself; a `per_entry` row named for one would now charge every entry, since a "per class" fee bills. **There are no quick-add presets either**: six pre-named chips above the list read as six fees the show already had, two of them were one unit under two names, and a fee is a name, an amount and a unit, which is what the form asks for. A show that already had `standard_class` or `jackpot` rows keeps them -- they now surface as ordinary, renamable rows in the Class Fees list rather than under dedicated labels.

## Reservations, minimums and early rates

- `show_entry_reservations`: how many of each `show_fees` row an exhibitor booked at sign-up (stalls, bags of shavings, camping nights). Points at the fee catalog rather than restating those as columns; which fees are reservable is derived from the fee's `unit` (`per_stall` / `per_bag` / `per_night` / `per_day` / `per_show`), so a show's own custom per-stall fee is offered with no code change. `reserved_at` is the day the line was booked and is what selects between the fee's two rates.
- **A minimum booking is two facts, and only one of them is a number.** `show_fees.min_quantity` is what a secretary typed; `shows.shavings_ban_outside` is the show saying bedding must be bought here, and it is what almost every show that means it actually records. They are the same requirement, and until `backend/reservations.py` only the first was enforced — so a show banning outside shavings took sign-ups with **no bedding at all**, because leaving the line out is how you book none of something. The derived floor is **one bag**, not a guess at how many: how deep a stall is bedded is the show's to state, and an explicit `min_quantity` always wins where it is higher. **Conditional on a stall**, because a day-haul entry that ships in on the Saturday and goes home has nothing to bed — which is why the floor is computed from the booking in hand rather than seeded when the form opens: dropping to no stalls drops the requirement in the same save.
- **A minimum quantity is a requirement, and it is checked against the whole booking.** `reservations.minimum_shortfall` walks the fee catalogue, not the lines that were sent: leaving a line out entirely is the easiest way to book none of something, so "at least four bags" would have been satisfied by sending no bags at all. **Zero is not an escape hatch** -- a show sets a floor because it will not have horses bedded on less, which is a statement about everybody who stables there. A show that wants day-haul entries exempt says so in the fee's `notes`. Guarded to `REQUIRABLE_FEE_UNITS` -- bedding alone -- which is **narrower than the early rate beside it**, and narrower still than it first was. An early rate applies wherever a quantity is booked (minus bedding, see below); a minimum is a *policy about the grounds*, and only bedding states one: "every stall gets bedded this deep" is a real venue fact, while "every rig takes a stall" is not -- an exhibitor books however many stalls they need, full stop, so the stall minimum offered at launch was removed again once nobody found a use for it. No show requires an exhibitor to book a camping spot in order to be allowed to enter either. Offering the box on either put a control on the setup screen whose own explanation ("required of everyone who signs up") read as nonsense against the line it sat under. `required_quantity` returns 0 for every non-bedding unit as well as the endpoint refusing them, because the column is older than the guard -- twice over, now.
- **The bedding floor is derived from the shavings ban, not only from `min_quantity`, and it took a real show to notice.** MNSPHC bans outside shavings, sells bags, and left `min_quantity` at 0 -- so sign-ups went through with no bedding booked at all, and the screen said nothing, because the ban and the minimum are the same requirement recorded in two different places and only one of them had a reader. `reservations.required_quantity` reconciles them: one bag where the show bans outside shavings *and the exhibitor has reserved a stall*, an explicit `min_quantity` winning wherever it is higher. **One bag, not a guess at more** -- how deep this venue wants a stall bedded is the show's to state, and the fix for MNSPHC's "2 bags per stall" is still to set the number. **Conditional on the stall**, because a day-haul entry has nothing to bed and charging them for a bag they cannot use is the requirement misfiring in the one direction nobody can argue out of at the desk.
- **The sign-up form derives the quantity in the box; it does not write the floor back into state.** An effect that raised the number turned the state feeding the floors into the state the floors then changed, which is a render loop the moment it is not perfectly idempotent -- and it was not. `quantities` is `rawQuantities` with each floor applied on read, so there is no stored value that can disagree with the floor and no update to loop on. The picker is still only an affordance: `PUT /signup` refuses the same booking whether or not a screen was involved.
- **A show's stall-minimum may be per *stall*, and this is per *exhibitor*.** The MNSPHC bill reads "Minimum 2 bags per stall", which is a floor that scales with another reserved line; `min_quantity` is a flat number required of everyone who signs up. A show wanting the scaled rule sets the flat equivalent and says so in the fee's `notes`. Scaling it would mean one `show_fees` row referring to another, which is a relationship the table does not have and `build_bill` could not price.
- **Shavings do not get an early rate, and this is not the same restriction as the minimum quantity above it.** `EARLY_RATE_FEE_UNITS` (billing.py) excludes `per_bag` from `RESERVABLE_FEE_UNITS`, while `REQUIRABLE_FEE_UNITS` (reservations.py) is `per_bag` *alone* -- the two lists move in opposite directions for the same reason: a bag count has no "book by this date, pay less" convention the way a stall or a camping spot does, but it is the one line a show states a real venue policy about ("every stall gets bedded this deep"). `has_early_rate` re-checks the unit on read as well as the endpoint refusing it on write, the same defense-in-depth as `required_quantity` -- a value stored before the guard existed must not go on quoting a discount from a control no screen renders any more.
- A fee's early-bird rate is chosen by `show_entry_reservations.reserved_at` — the day the exhibitor booked that line — **never by today's date**. Pricing off "now" would silently reprice a reservation the moment the deadline passed, which is the one promise an early rate makes. This is also why `PUT /shows/{id}/register/signup` updates surviving reservation lines in place instead of replacing them wholesale: recreating a row re-dates it, and someone who reserved in April would lose their rate by editing their arrival date in July. Read the rate through `fee_rate_cents(fee, reserved_at)`, never `fee.amount_cents` directly.
- An early rate is a **pair**: `early_amount_cents` without `early_deadline` (or vice versa) is a half-finished edit, not a price. `POST`/`PATCH /shows/{id}/fees` rejects it rather than storing a discount that would never fire, and also rejects one on a non-reservable unit — class entry fees live on `classes.entry_fee_cents` and are never reserved, so there would be nothing for it to apply to.

## Club sanctioning

- `show_sanctioning`: per-show club sanctioning overlay set in setup Step 5 — `show_sanctioning(show_id, association_id, fee_amount_cents, fee_unit)` referencing club rows in `associations`. Admins manage the registry; non-admin wizard users may submit `sanctioned_association_requests` for admin review. Show setup asks for a breed association (or Open) as the show type, then optional club sanctioning. **The fee is an amount and a unit** (migration 133), the pair `show_fees` has always been: `per_entry` (per class entered — the original behaviour, the default, and what the column name `per_class_fee_cents` used to assert), `per_exhibitor`, `per_horse`, `per_judge_per_horse` or `per_judge_per_exhibitor`. Drawn from the same vocabulary as the show's own automatic charges and priced through the same `billing.charge_multiplier`, because "per judge, per horse" cannot mean two different things depending on whose fee it is. `per_judge_per_entry` is **not** offered: that is the breed body's own per-entry assessment (SC-125.B and its kin), it belongs to the show's fee catalog, and a club billing per class entered is `per_entry`. Whichever unit is chosen, every count is taken over **that club's approved classes only** — the same rule migration 131 settled for the breed body's charges, read from the other side.
- `class_sanctioning`: **which classes each sanctioning club actually approves** (migration 113). A club sanction is not a property of the show — NSBA approves a list of classes, and an NSBA-sanctioned show runs plenty of classes NSBA has nothing to do with. Before this, both halves of the money were wrong in opposite directions: NSBA billed **every** class (one show-wide boolean plus a hard-coded 6%/$3-min rule), and WSCA/MNSPHC billed **nothing** (`show_sanctioning`'s fee — `per_class_fee_cents` then, `fee_amount_cents` since migration 133 — was printed on the public show bill and read by no code path). One join table on `(class_id, association_id)` fixes both, because both are the same missing fact. Points at `associations`, not `show_types` — and is **not** `class_associations`, which answers a different question (the breed body's *class code*, for catalog imports). Designated in **Step 5** (`/admin/shows/[id]/setup/sanctioning`), beside the clubs and their rates — the three facts a sanction needs, in the order the answers come. `/admin/shows/[id]/classes/sanctioning` redirects there.
- **A club sanction fee is charged on the classes that club approves, and nowhere else.** `billing.class_sanction_cents` sums `show_sanctioning.fee_amount_cents` over the clubs with a `class_sanctioning` row for *that class*. The old code read one show-wide boolean and put a fee on every line of the bill, which charged an exhibitor eight sanction fees for eight classes when the club approved one of them. A dual-sanctioned class legitimately carries both clubs' fees — that is two clubs collecting, not the larger of two rates. Migration 133 changes what the amount *counts*, never this: a `per_horse` club charges for the horses that entered its own classes, and an exhibitor who entered none of them owes it nothing whatever the unit says.
- **A club bills through exactly one of the two paths, and both ends filter.** `sanction_rates` returns the `per_entry` clubs (whose money rides on each class line as `sanction_cents`); `sanction_charge_lines` returns the rest (one charge per exhibitor, itemised on the bill with its arithmetic). A club reaching both would be charged twice, so `sanction_rates` filters on the unit rather than leaving it to the caller — `class_sanction_cents` runs per class from two routers, and a filter a caller has to remember is one somebody will forget — and `charge_multiplier` returns 0 for `per_entry` as the second guard. `sanction_total_cents` is still both together, which is the figure every screen prints; `class_sanction_total_cents` is the per-class half, so a bill can show it beside the club lines and foot without summing anything in the browser.
- **There is one sanction rate and it is the flat per-class amount.** The NSBA 6%-of-entry-fee, $3-minimum rule is gone. It was hard-coded, ignored the amount the manager typed in setup, and meant two clubs on the same bill billed by different mechanisms. Consequence to keep in view: an NSBA show that leaves the box at $0 charges nothing, so Step 5 says so against the club — on its row as it is ticked, and in the arithmetic beside its class count. Do not reintroduce a percentage rule without deciding what happens to the flat amount beside it.
- **Enrolling a club and pricing it charges nobody until classes are designated.** *Which clubs*, *how much*, and *which classes* are three separate facts and the fee only bills where all three exist. They were on three screens — a step, a box on the fees step, and a screen hanging off the class list — visited in an order nothing told you, which is how a show ends up with a sanction that charges nobody. Step 5 asks all three, in the order the answers come, and states the arithmetic against each club as its rate, its unit and its classes are set: a $3 per-class fee against zero approved classes is indistinguishable from a working one otherwise, and "$45.00 × 4 judges = $180.00 for each horse they enter in its 21 approved classes" is the number a manager is checking against a paper show bill. `sanction_rates` also drops a club with no fee rather than putting a $0.00 line on every entry, and a designation left behind by a club the show later dropped prices at zero rather than at what it used to charge.
- **`Class.sanctioning` is `lazy="selectin"`, like `Class.associations`.** Every path that prices a class reads it — the bill, the registration quote, the public schedule, the show bill — and an unloaded relationship in an async request is a MissingGreenlet 500. Loading it with the class costs one SELECT and removes the eager-load footgun from every caller. Do not switch it to a `selectinload` the callers have to remember.
- **The show bill has to mark which classes carry a sanction fee.** "NSBA — $3.00 per class" over an unmarked schedule leaves an exhibitor unable to work out their own bill, which is the same failure as publishing a price the app does not charge. `sanctioning_codes` rides on the class list payload for exactly this, and the CSV export carries the column.

## Payments, balances and who may see them

- `show_payments`: what the office **recorded collecting** against an exhibitor's account at one show (migration 096) — amount, method, reference, the day it was received, and who took it. Scoped to `show_entries`, not to an individual charge: the desk takes one check for the whole bill. `amount_cents` is signed, so a refund is a negative row. **Recording, not processing** — no card is handled and no processor is called. This is the half that makes `billed − paid = balance` mean anything; without it an outstanding balance is the full bill for everyone, forever. Read through `billing.build_account` / `summarize_accounts`. `/admin/shows/[id]/financials` is the summary — headline money figures and side pots only, since registration counts and revenue-by-category are reports; per-exhibitor accounts and payment entry live on `/financials/exhibitors`, reports on `/financials/reports`, both reached from buttons on the summary. Both screens self-refresh (`AutoRefresh`) so a totals board left open at the desk does not silently go stale while a second staff member takes money.
- **The payment form's Received date starts at today**, in the reader's own zone, set after mount because the server renders in UTC. The office nearly always records money as it is handed over, so the date it was received is usually the day it is typed; a cheque that came in the post last week is the case for changing it. Blank still means the database's `CURRENT_DATE`, which is UTC and therefore tomorrow by a US show's evening — the reason the form fills the date in rather than relying on the blank.
- **Outstanding and credit are never netted against each other.** `summarize_accounts` sums positive balances into `outstanding_cents` and negative ones into `credit_cents` separately, because one exhibitor paying twice must not reduce what the show is owed by everyone else. `net_balance_cents` is the netted figure, kept alongside for the books. A single signed total would quietly under-report arrears.
- A refund is a **negative `show_payments` row**, never an edit to or deletion of the original payment. The original is a fact about money that moved; erasing it balances the account and loses that the money moved twice. `DELETE /shows/{id}/payments/{pid}` exists only for a row typed in error.
- Financials is the **show-office tier** — ADMIN, or the SHOW_SECRETARY / SHOW_MANAGER assigned to that show (`require_admin_or_show_admin` + `_assert_show_access`). `SCRIBE` and `GATE_STEWARD` are show staff too and are deliberately excluded: neither has any business reading revenue. "Show staff" is ambiguous in this app — check which tier before widening a money endpoint.
- **The automatic charges are rolled up apart from the reservations.** `summarize_accounts` returns `fee_lines` (booked) and `charge_lines` (applied) as two lists. Both are `show_fees` rows, but the Stalls, Shavings & Camping report reads `fee_lines` as "what exhibitors reserved at sign-up" and foots it against `reservation_total_cents`; a drug fee nobody booked appearing there would leave that sheet's rows disagreeing with its own total. Same reasoning as keeping side pot money out of account balances.

## Financial reports

- **There are two report registries and one renderer.** `backend/financial_reports.py` is the money; `backend/show_reports.py` is the record of the show — results, entry cards, judges' cards, the compliance sheet — reached from the **Show Record** tile at `/admin/shows/[id]/reports`. Both return the same shape and `components/ReportTable.tsx` draws either, so adding a report to either is one function and nothing else. Keeping them apart is deliberate: the money is the show-office's own business and the record is what gets sent to an association, and only one of them is allowed to be wrong about a placing.
- A financial **report is data, not a page**: `backend/financial_reports.py` holds a slug → (title, description, builder) registry, and one generic frontend renderer draws whatever columns and rows it returns. Adding a report is a function in `_REPORTS` — no route, no component, no migration. Reports are built from the payload the overview already assembled and never query, so a report cannot quote a different number than the screen it was opened from.

## Presenting fees

- **A published fee schedule is grouped, not listed** (`FEE_GROUPS` / `groupFees` in `frontend/lib/fee-units.ts`). A flat column of amounts with a parenthetical unit after each cannot be read as "what will this weekend cost me": a bag of shavings you order, a per-judge assessment that simply arrives, and a stall-cleanout penalty the app never charges anybody all render identically. The groups are the families `billing.py` already bills by — reserved, automatic, and neither — split once more where the exhibitor's decision differs, and each carries a line saying whether the amounts under it are theirs to control. A unit in no group falls into **Other charges** rather than vanishing: a fee the show published and this list has never heard of is still one somebody will be asked for.
