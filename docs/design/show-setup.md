# Shows, The Setup Wizard, The Class Schedule And The Show Bill

Read before changing the `shows` row or its payload, a setup step, the Class Builder, class numbering or order, the show bill, or the show bill import. The step-by-step narrative is in [show-workflow.md](../show-workflow.md) (Show Setup Wizard, Class Setup Wizard); the import pipeline is in [showbill-import.md](../showbill-import.md).

These are the rules this part of the app keeps and the reasons behind them, moved here from `Claude.md` so they load when the work needs them rather than on every request. One claim per bullet, the claim first. Add new ones under the heading they belong to.

## The show row

- `shows`: event shell with venue, dates, `entry_deadline` (the day entries close — migration 123 — and since migration 159 **the last day to sign up online**; it still bills no post-entry fee), `self_entry_closes` (how late exhibitors enter and scratch their own classes, migration 159 — see below), `back_number_per` (whether the show numbers exhibitors or horses, migration 161 — asked on Step 1 under *Back numbers*; see [registration.md](registration.md)), primary association, status, optional APHA show number, AQHA approval metadata, and `shavings_ban_outside` policy bool, which surfaces on the exhibitor self-registration screen. It carried `office_charge_cents` + `office_charge_basis` until migration 132 turned that into an ordinary `show_fees` row -- see the Sharp Edge below. Since migration 097 it also states **which health papers it requires** — `requires_coggins` (default true), `requires_health_certificate` + `health_certificate_valid_days` (default 30), `requires_vaccination` + `vaccination_valid_days` (default 365) + `vaccination_notes`, and since migration 138 **whether the originals have to be produced at the counter** — `requires_physical_document_check`, default true, which is what every show did before the column existed. Set at `/admin/shows/[id]/setup/paperwork` (Step 9) and `/admin/shows/[id]/desk/paperwork` — the same screen behind two doors.
- **`_serialize` in `routers/shows.py` builds the show payload by hand, so a column left out of it reads back as `ShowOut`'s default.** `apha_zone` was missing from migration 119 until now: every show reported `apha_zone: null` whatever was stored, and the edit form — loading that null and posting it back — wiped the zone on the next save. Nothing failed loudly, because the gate note reads the column off the ORM row server-side and kept working. Add new `shows` columns to **both** `ShowOut` and `_serialize`.
- `show_setup_skips`: **what the show office said does not apply** (migration 154). The wizard's optional steps always offered a *Skip*, but it was a link to the next step and nothing remembered it, so a show that runs no futurity carried the step, and the dashboard tile, at full size for the rest of its life, looking like work nobody had done. One row per (show, key), written by `PUT`/`DELETE /shows/{id}/setup-skips/{key}` (`routers/show_setup.py`, show-office tier) — the keys are the skippable steps plus `sidepots` and `highpoint`, the second feature of Futurities & Side Pots and of Scoring, each declined on its own there. **A skip only counts while the step is still empty**, decided on read in `buildSteps` rather than stored: somebody who skipped a step and then set something up on it has changed their mind, and the step comes back without an undo. A skipped step **folds away** — behind an *N skipped* toggle in the tab bar, to one dashed line on the hub — and Back / Next walk past it; standing on one says so and offers *Bring this step back*. A declined feature **greys out its tile on the show dashboard** (`featureDeclined`: Futurities, Side Pots and High Point) with a *Change this in setup* link, rather than disappearing. Recorded, never enforced: nothing is refused over a skip.

## The setup wizard

- **A setup step is a position in a flow, not a folder.** Six of the ten live under `/setup`; Step 1 is `/admin/shows/[id]/edit`, Step 4 is `/admin/shows/[id]/classes`, Step 6 (Futurities & Side Pots) is `/admin/shows/[id]/futurities` and Step 8 (Scoring) is `/admin/shows/[id]/classes/judging`, because all four were screens before the wizard reached them and are deep-linked from elsewhere. `StepLayout` is what makes a route a step. Moving those under `/setup/` to tidy the tree would break every existing link and buy nothing. **The numbers are the step's position**, computed in `buildSteps` and prefixed onto each title by `StepLayout` — a page passes its title unnumbered. They were typed by hand, and the Show Bill page read "Step 9" on a tab labelled 10. `/classes/sanctioning` is still a redirect only — Sanctioned Classes is part of Step 5.
- **The setup wizard's steps are tabs that wrap, and the tab bar is a `<nav>` rather than a `role="tablist"`.** Each tab is a route, so it carries `aria-current="step"` and nothing else — the ARIA tab roles promise a screen reader a panel relationship that does not exist when the press navigates away (`scribe/JudgeTabs.tsx` is the case where they *are* right: it swaps a panel in place). They wrap because nine labelled steps is about 1,000px of tabs, and the `overflow-x-auto` rail this replaced put steps five onwards off the right-hand edge of every screen this app is used on, with nothing to say they were there. Sticky only from `sm` up: pinned to a phone, a four-row bar is half the viewport. **`stepName()` lives in `_wizard/steps.ts`, not in the tab bar's own `'use client'` module** — `StepLayout` is a server component, and calling a client module's plain export from the server is a runtime error neither `tsc` nor ESLint sees.
- **On the desktop layout the steps are listed in the staff sidebar, and the tab bar hides.** Under **Setup**, with the same tick, dash or number on each and the same *N skipped* fold; `StepLayout` and the setup hub pass the tab bar `className="desktop:hidden"`. The sidebar is a parallel route so its ticks are current on every page, and its links save the step they leave because the autosave registry lives in the root `layout.tsx`, which `StepLayout`'s provider joins rather than shadowing. The steps also get the full width beside it — the Class Builder's matrix was the screen that most needed it. The mobile layout (every role but the office's, and any office browser switched to it) keeps the tab bar. Why each of those, in [frontend.md](../frontend.md) under "The Desktop Layout And The Staff Sidebar".
- **A setup step that can be skipped says so above the form, and only where empty is a real answer.** `StepLayout`'s `skip` renders between the tabs and the content, because the person who needs it is the one this step is not their show's business at all — at the bottom of the page they find out only after reading the whole screen for something to fill in. Two rules: it is offered **only while the step is empty** (a show that has set a futurity up is not skipping anything), and **only on a step nobody is required to complete** — Judges, Lodging, Sanctioning, Futurities & Side Pots, Fees, Scoring. Basics, the Class Builder and the Show Bill have none; `PUBLISHED` requires a class, so a button telling somebody they may skip the schedule is worse than silence. The label names the answer ("Skip — no club sanctioning"), not the act of leaving. **Since migration 154 the skip is recorded** (`show_setup_skips`, above) — that is what lets the step fold away and a dashboard tile grey out. Futurities & Side Pots and Scoring are two features each on one step, so the second feature of each — side pots, and the high-point chart beside the judge cards — is also declined on its own inside it (`DeclineFeature`), and the step folds away only once both halves are declined.
- **Leaving a setup step saves it, and the navigation is what does the saving.** `setup/_lib/StepAutosave.tsx`: a step's client registers a `flush`, and every control that leaves the step — the footer's Back / Next buttons and every tab — awaits it first. Every step used to end in a Save button, and walking the stepper without pressing it discarded the form silently, which looks like progress rather than like loss. Two rules make it safe to bolt onto forms that already had Save buttons. **A flush that throws stops the navigation**, because the step's own error is on screen by then and carrying somebody forward from a 422 is the failure this replaces. And **a flush must be a no-op when nothing is dirty** — these fire on every press of every tab, so an unconditional PUT would rewrite a step somebody only passed through; `LodgingClient` and `SanctioningClient` each compare against what the screen loaded rather than tracking a flag, since typing a 5 and deleting it again has changed nothing. The Save buttons that remain are a second door onto the same function, not a fallback.
- **The steps that need a class schedule come after the one that builds it.** Sanctioning was Step 3 — before any classes existed — so only one of its three questions could be answered there and the other two had been scattered: the per-class rate into a box on the fees step, the class designation onto a screen hanging off the class list. Judge Cards was a yellow notice at the top of the class wizard for the same reason. Both are steps after the Class Builder now. Do not move either back up the flow to group it with something it resembles; what orders this wizard is what each step needs to already exist. **Fees follows the same rule from the other side**: it comes after Sanctioning and Futurities (order 4 Class Builder, 5 Sanctioning, 6 Futurities, 7 Fees), so it can show what the clubs and the futurity already charge beside the show's own class fees, and so narrowing a fee to some classes has classes to pick from. It sat before the Class Builder, where that picker was always empty on a show set up in order.
- **Paperwork is a setup step *and* a desk screen, and that is one implementation with two doors.** It was a step, moved to `/admin/shows/[id]/desk/paperwork` because what a show requires of an exhibitor is the desk's standing order -- read every time somebody registers, revised by the people working registration, and "set it once in a wizard nobody reopens" is exactly the failure. All of that still holds and the desk screen is unchanged. What it got wrong is that the *first* answer is given while the show is being built: a manager setting a show up is asking "what do I require of an exhibitor?" at that moment, and the wizard did not ask. So `/setup/paperwork` is Step 9 and renders the same `PaperworkClient` against the same endpoints. **Before the Show Bill**, because the bill prints these requirements -- the same ordering rule that puts Sanctioning and Futurities before Fees. Its tick is `done: true` like Basics rather than a count: every show requires a Coggins from the moment it is created, so there is no un-started state, and a tick true of every show on arrival would say nothing. Do not resolve the "duplication" by deleting either door -- a second *implementation* is the thing to avoid, and there is only one.
- **The Lodging step finds its rows by `code`, so anything that creates fees in bulk must use its codes.** `LodgingClient` manages three rows — `stall`, `shavings`, `camping` (and the pre-108 `hookup`) — and knows no other. The show bill import first named its rows from their labels, so the bill's stall landed as `horse_stall`, Step 3 opened with an empty Stalls card, and filling it in would have billed a second stall. `showbill_import.fee_codes` gives the *main* line of each family the slot code (a tack stall or an early-arrival night is not the main line and stays on the Boarding Fees screen) and never hands those codes to anything else. The seed scripts did this by hand; a future bulk creator has to as well.

## Disciplines, divisions and classes

- `disciplines`: per-show **riding styles** (Halter, Western Pleasure, Trail, Barrels). Each carries `default_score_type` (`placement` / `pattern` / `time`) that new classes inherit when score_type is omitted. Renamed from `divisions` in migration 074.
- `divisions`: per-show **age/skill brackets** (10 & Under, Walk-Trot, Amateur, Youth 14-18). Each division belongs to one or more disciplines via `discipline_divisions` (M2M). A division with no discipline memberships is unusable on classes. Renamed from `sections` in migration 074.
- `discipline_divisions`: join table on `(discipline_id, division_id)`. A `(discipline_id, division_id)` pair on a class must exist as a row here — enforced by a Postgres composite FK on `classes`. The single-class create endpoint (`POST /shows/{id}/classes`) upserts the membership on demand, so any valid in-show pair is accepted; the update path still 422s on an unregistered pair.
- `standard_disciplines` / `standard_divisions` / `standard_discipline_divisions`: curated lookup lists for the setup picker; `show_type_id NULL` is the generic fallback. The class wizard's standard library follows the show's own show type — a breed show gets that association's curated list plus the generic fallback, and OPEN, having no association of its own, gets the AQHA + APHA shared universe merged by name. Where both a curated row and a generic row carry the same name the association's wins, since it carries that association's score type and running order.
- `standard_classes`: canonical per-show-type class catalog (migration 068) backing the Matrix setup picker on `/admin/shows/[id]/setup`. Each row pairs a class to a `(standard_division, standard_section)` cell via composite FK to `standard_division_sections`. AQHA is fully seeded (~1589 classes from the 2026 Class Master Listing) via `scripts/generate_aqha_standard_library_seed.py` — re-run after `database/seeds/aqha_standard_classes.csv` changes. APHA/NSBA/WSCA/ApHC seeding is upcoming Phase 2/3 work.
- `classes`: competition classes, sorted by `sort_order`. **Both** `division_id` (discipline) and `section_id` (bracket) are required (migration 061). `score_type` is `placement` (judges rank — rail/halter), `pattern` (judges score numerically — showmanship/horsemanship/etc.), or `time` (clocked event); derived from `division.default_score_type` at create time when omitted. `entry_fee_cents` is informational and surfaced on the exhibitor self-registration screen — the app does not collect payment. APHA/AQHA catalog imports auto-route class names through `backend/rules/disciplines.py`, create any missing divisions/sections, and register the (div, sec) membership. The Standard Library picker (any show type) lets the secretary check `(discipline × bracket)` cells from `standard_divisions` / `standard_sections` and commit them in one click via `POST /shows/{id}/classes/from-library`. Schedule-builder picks with no section use the per-show "Unassigned" section placeholder.
- `classes.score_type` is derived from `division.default_score_type` at create time when not explicitly set. APHA/AQHA bulk imports and the free-text class-list importer are **auto-routed** via [backend/rules/disciplines.py](../../backend/rules/disciplines.py): the class name is classified by ordered keyword match into a discipline (Division) with a default `score_type`. For APHA/AQHA catalog imports the std-class `division` column is used as the section (bracket); for pasted class lists the secretary can provide a default bracket or per-line bracket using `Class Name | Bracket`. Divisions/sections are created on the fly and the (div, sec) membership is registered. Anything the classifier can't match (currently 0% of AQHA/APHA std classes) falls back to "Unassigned". Side pots with `sum_scores` only see `pattern`/`time` classes.
- Migration 061 made `classes.division_id` and `classes.section_id` NOT NULL and enforces `(division_id, section_id)` membership in `division_sections` via a composite FK. Code that creates a class must either provide a valid pair or call `_get_or_create_unassigned()` from `routers/classes.py`. Removing a section from a division it still has classes in returns 409.
- Migration 048 dropped `class_templates` and the `category` concept; "Division = discipline" and "Section = bracket" are the canonical vocabulary. Per-show `divisions` rows that pre-date 048 may still contain bracket-named entries — secretaries clean those up in the Setup UI.
- `classes.entered_by_qualification`: **the class you place into rather than enter** (migration 129). A Grand & Reserve Champion halter class calls back the first- and second-place horses from each qualifying class once those classes have been judged, so at the moment somebody is filling in an entry form there is nothing to sign up for — and all eight of them on the MNSPHC schedule were in the exhibitor's class picker, priced at $0. **Derived, then stored**, exactly like `score_type`: `rules.disciplines.entered_by_qualification` reads the name when the class is created, the column is the authority thereafter, and the Class Builder's class list carries a **Must qualify** checkbox for it — a name is a guess and somebody has to be able to correct it in both directions; unticked is open entry, and there is no second label for it. The grid names every class "{Discipline} {Division}", which no championship class is, so **+ Add Class** (with an *+ Add a Grand & Reserve Class* shortcut: Halter, the name started, Must qualify ticked, placed after the last class that day in the same cell) is how one gets onto the schedule at all. Pressing that shortcut twice suggests the same name twice, which is half the reason for the same-day duplicate rule in the Sharp Edges below; the form closes on a save and opens the class list at the row it made, because a class you have just named is one you want to see on the schedule. The name test is deliberately narrow, because "champion" alone is an award (Hi-Point) as often as a class. **The app does not work out who qualified**: that needs a relationship between a championship and the classes feeding it, which nothing here holds. So `POST /shows/{id}/register` refuses with `CLASS_BY_QUALIFICATION` and the **desk still enters them** — the office is standing there when the judge calls the horses back.

## Class numbers and running order

- `classes.class_number` is **published identity**, not a display index. It appears in the show program, on entry forms, and is how the gate and exhibitors refer to a class, so it is never renumbered to match a filtered or reordered view. The show page folds finished classes away for scribes on an active show — a list starting at "14" is that fold, not a numbering bug. Fix the affordance, never the numbers.
- **Do not sort a schedule by discipline and division. The show bills say no.** It was built -- `_renumber_classes` ordering by day, then discipline name, then division name -- and removed again after checking it against the two uploaded bills (`show_documents`), which are the authority for how a class list is structured. Three things came out of that. The **performance half of a real bill is already exactly this**: on the MNSPHC Paint-O-Rama, Showmanship is 13 classes in 13 slots, Hunter Under Saddle 16 in 16, Hunt Seat Equitation 10 in 10, Trail 9 in 9 -- contiguous without anybody sorting anything. **Alphabetical is wrong on both axes**: the bill opens with Halter first thing in the morning and runs the speed events last, where alphabetical opens the show with Barrel Racing; and halter divisions are *horse ages*, so alphabetical gives Four Year & Older before Three Year Old before Two Year Old before Yearling, exactly backwards. And **halter cannot be reproduced by any (discipline, division) sort**, because the bill sub-groups it Stallions -> Geldings -> Mares and the sex is only ever in the class *name* -- sorting dissolves those groups and lifts every Grand & Reserve away from the qualifiers it calls back from. The order is the show office's to manage; the app numbers sequentially as classes are added and offers the drag.
- **The show bill's discipline headings are runs, never buckets.** `runsOf` groups *consecutive* classes sharing a discipline, so a heading describes the printed order rather than re-sorting it. The bill prints `class_number` beside every row, and those numbers are how the gate, the entry form and the office refer to a class — bucketing would pull class 44 up under a heading printed at class 8, which is published identity rewritten by a display. A discipline the show genuinely runs twice in a day gets two headings: on the MNSPHC bill the halter section really does run Halter, Performance Halter, Halter again, and all of those are true. A day that is all one discipline gets no headings at all, where they would say nothing the class names do not.
- **The show bill's class schedule is grouped under discipline headings** (`ShowbillDocument`, via `runsOf` in `frontend/lib/class-order.ts`). Each day's classes print under the discipline they run in, with the division left on the row's own line beneath the class name — so the bill reads by discipline, then division, the way a printed prize list does. **Nothing is reordered and nothing is renumbered**: see the Sharp Edges on why the grouping is runs rather than buckets, and on why a schedule must not be sorted by these two axes.

## The Class Builder

- **The Class Builder serves every show type, not just OPEN.** The class wizard was gated to `show_type_code === 'OPEN'` behind a "being rebuilt, per-association flows come next" placeholder while the old APHA/AQHA pickers were removed — and the second half never shipped, so an APHA show could not reach its own class schedule. Nothing in the wizard was ever OPEN-specific: it works entirely in per-show `disciplines` / `divisions` / `classes`, and the only show-type coupling was a hard-coded AQHA+APHA standard-library fetch. That now follows `show.show_type_id`. Do not reintroduce a per-association class-setup screen — the association-specific part of class setup is the *catalog*, and that lives in `association_standard_classes`.
- **Disciplines and divisions are the Quick Class Picker's own columns and rows — not steps, and not a panel either.** They were parts 1 and 2 of a three-part wizard inside setup Step 4 — pick the riding styles and save, pick the brackets and save, then reach the grid that crosses the two. A discipline is a **row** of that grid and a division is a **column** of it; **neither is read anywhere else in setup**, and a show is no closer to having a schedule for having named either, so the sequence was three screens to answer one question. The picker draws **every** column and row the show could use — its own rows merged with its show type's standard library, alphabetically (`mergeAxisOptions`) — so a show with nothing set up opens on a full grid and a clicked square creates the discipline, the division and the class together. Do not reintroduce a screen, a step or a panel for "adding a discipline": there is no longer a moment in the flow where that is a thing anybody does.
- **A cell creates at most one row per axis per drain.** `axisIdFor` takes a `known` map that `drainQueue` builds once and carries across every job, so a sweep of six cells down a column the show has never used creates that column once rather than six times. It is safe for that map to be the only cache because **nothing else creates a discipline or a division any more** — the drain's own closure list plus `known` is the complete picture. Ids come out of the bulk create's response rather than a re-read, and each axis the drain opened is re-read exactly once in the closing `finally`.
- **A name goes to `/disciplines/bulk` one at a time rather than to the single create, and that is about score types.** The bulk endpoint infers a discipline's `default_score_type` from its name (a `standard_disciplines` row's if it matches one, `_infer_score_type`'s otherwise); the single create makes the caller state it. A class inherits its score type from its discipline, so a client picking a default here would be guessing how every class in that column is judged.
- **How many classes use a discipline is counted from the loaded class list, never from the endpoint's `class_count`.** That column only refreshes when the axis is re-read, and deleting a class re-reads the *classes* — so the picker went on believing a column was in use and withheld its ×. The client holds every class for the show, so counting them is exact and cannot drift. Same rule as anywhere else here: derive from the thing that changed, rather than refetching something that happens to carry the number.
- **The by-name form's "+ Add a new …" name is held locally until the save uses it.** `resolveAxisPair` creates the row on submit; typing a name and closing the form leaves nothing behind. Writing it straight to the show would put a column on the grid that nobody asked for and that only the × could clear. Once it does become real it arrives in the picker's options too, so `withLocalOptions` merges by key — concatenating gives React two `<option>`s under one key and shows the name twice.
- **A class-create click during the drain's closing refresh must be able to start the next drain.** `drainQueue` clears `processingRef` at the top of its `finally` and *then* awaits `refreshClasses()`, so there is a second or so where nothing is processing, the loop has already exited, and a click can still land. `addCell` therefore calls `void drainQueue()` **unconditionally** and lets that flag be the only guard. Gating the call on a "drain in flight" promise instead silently loses the click — the running drain will not pick it up and nothing starts another — which is exactly what happened when the step's leave-guard was first written that way. For the same reason the `StepAutosave` flush waits on `queueRef`/`processingRef` rather than awaiting a stored drain promise: there is no single promise that spans a drain ending and the next one beginning.
- **One class of a name per show day, and the rule is keyed on the name rather than on the grid cell.** `_assert_name_free` in `routers/classes.py` refuses a second class whose `lower(btrim(class_name))` already runs that day — both halves of the Class Builder post to `POST /shows/{id}/classes`, so the grid's greyed-out cell and the by-name form's disabled Add button are affordances and this is the enforcement. Keying it on the **cell** was the obvious alternative and is wrong: a show legitimately runs "Grand & Reserve Amateur Mares" and "Grand & Reserve Amateur Geldings" on the same day in the same Halter × Amateur cell, and a cell-keyed rule refuses the second one. For anything the grid creates the two tests coincide anyway, since it names every class "{Discipline} {Division}". The **day** is part of the key because the same class run on the Saturday and again on the Sunday is two classes, judged and numbered separately. Scoped to the single-class endpoint: the catalog and library bulk imports are a different job with their own dedup, and turning a duplicate there into a 409 would fail a 172-class import over one row.
- **A bulk delete refuses a class anybody has entered; the single Delete does not.** `POST /shows/{id}/classes/bulk-delete` (`bulk_delete_blocker`) takes the whole ticked list or none of it, and names the classes to untick. A class cascades to its `entries` and `results`, so a sweep over forty ticks is the one place "delete" quietly costs somebody their entry — which is not the deliberate act that pressing Delete on one named row is, and that row's behaviour is unchanged. It exists at all because every delete calls `_renumber_classes`: clearing a mis-built schedule one row at a time was a round trip per class, each rewriting every class number, with the list shuffling in between. The class list's checkbox is disabled on an entered class for the same reason, with the count beside its name.

## The show bill

- `shows.showbill_source` / `show_documents`: **whose show bill the Show Bill button opens** (migration 127). `generated` — the default, and every show before this — draws the bill from that show's own classes, judges, fees and policies; `uploaded` renders the file the show put on record. `show_documents` is the file, shaped after `horse_documents` (bytes in the row, MIME sniffed from the magic bytes, uploader recorded), typed from the start so a venue map or a club rules sheet has somewhere to go later. **The choice and the file are two separate facts**: `showbill_source` may only read `uploaded` while a SHOWBILL row exists — enforced in `routers/show_documents.py`, since a CHECK cannot see another table — deleting the document resets the column in the same transaction, and every read returns `effective_source` beside `source` so a renderer cannot be handed an empty frame. Set at setup **Step 10** (`/admin/shows/[id]/setup/showbill`); read publicly at `GET /shows/{id}/showbill-document`, with the bytes at `.../file`.
- **The show bill's choice and its file are two separate facts, and only one endpoint may pair them.** Uploading a bill does not publish it — `POST /shows/{id}/showbill-document` puts it on record and `PUT /shows/{id}/showbill-source` is the second, deliberate press. That is not ceremony: a manager comparing their club's PDF against the generated bill has to be able to look at it without every exhibitor's Show Bill button changing underneath them mid-comparison. The pair is enforced in both directions — selecting `uploaded` with nothing on file 422s, and `DELETE` of the document resets `showbill_source` in the same transaction, because "delete the file, then remember to change the setting" is not a sequence to hand somebody mid-setup. Readers still resolve rather than trust: `effective_source` falls back to `generated`, since the one thing a show bill must never be is blank.
- **The Show Bill step does not go green just because the manager arrived.** Every show defaults to the generated bill, so a "done" that meant "has a source" would be true of every show on creation and the tick would say nothing. `showbillReady` is whether the published bill is a bill yet — an uploaded file, or a generated one with classes on the schedule. It reads `effective_source`, so a show pointed at a file that is no longer on record is not counted as finished. **Scoring ticks on the same principle** (as it did when it was Judge Cards, or once the show has a high-point chart): `classCount > 0 && (scoredClassCount === 0 || cardedClassCount > 0)` — once there is a schedule, the step is done when there is nothing to mark or something has been marked. A show with no classes has not decided anything yet, and a schedule with no scored classes on it has nothing to ask.
- **There is no staleness check on an uploaded show bill, and adding one needs columns that do not exist.** Telling an exhibitor "this PDF predates the current schedule" would mean comparing the upload date against the last change to `classes`, `show_fees` and `show_judges` — and none of the three carries an `updated_at`. The honest substitute is what `UploadedShowbill` does: stamp the upload date on the page, say in as many words that classes and fees may have changed, and link out to the live schedule. Do not fake it by comparing against `shows.created_at` or a class's `created_at`; a check that is wrong in the reassuring direction is worse than none.
- **The generated show bill is the default, and an uploaded one never replaces the app's own data.** `/shows/[id]/showbill` is drawn from the show's own classes, judges, fees and policies, and "download" is the browser's print-to-PDF (plus a CSV of the class list for spreadsheet work). That is still the recommended answer, for the reason it always was: a generated bill cannot go stale the moment a secretary adds a class, and a stale PDF is worse than none because people trust the one they printed. Migration 127 adds the other option anyway — see `shows.showbill_source` below — because refusing the upload never made a club with a designed, already-printed show bill use the generated one; it made them e-mail a PDF this app never saw. What did **not** move is the reason `GET /shows/{id}/fees/public` exists: the price list on the bill has to be the price list the app charges from, so `/shows/[id]/showbill` prints the generated document beneath an uploaded one, under its own heading. A show picks what the *button* shows first; it does not get to make the live schedule unreachable. Show Details used to carry that guarantee by printing the generated bill itself; the bill came off Details, so the guarantee moved to the only page the bill is on — do not put the uploaded view back on its own.

## Setting a show up from its show bill

- `show_bill_imports`: **a show set up from its own printed show bill** (migration 141). The model reads the bill in the background (a twenty-page bill takes minutes, so the upload answers 202 with a `pending` row and the review screen polls it); `showbill_import.prepare_draft` matches what the bill *names* — breed body, venue, judges, clubs — against what the app *holds* and routes each class to a discipline; the reviewer edits everything on `/admin/shows/new/from-showbill/[importId]`; and `POST /show-bill-imports/{id}/apply` creates a **DRAFT** show, its venue, judges, club sanctioning, classes and fees in one transaction, from the **reviewed payload only** — `extracted` is never read on apply, so nothing reaches a record without passing in front of somebody. The bill is put on file as the show's SHOWBILL document with `showbill_source` left on `generated`. Side pots, a futurity's programme and show staff are listed as not imported rather than guessed at. See `docs/showbill-import.md`.
- **A show bill's class prices are read as rates, never as fees, and never multiplied by the model.** The prompt in `extraction/showbill.py` sends every class price to `class_rates` and has each class point at one by key, because a class price stored as a `per_entry` fee row bills every entry on top of the class's own price — the $552 amateur class, again. A bill quotes "$9 per judge"; the model reports $9 and `per_judge`, and the review screen multiplies by `cardedJudgeCount` over the panel as the reviewer has left it, so unticking a judge reprices every class that depends on them. When unsure whether a fee applies to everyone, the model is told `flat`: a wrongly flat fee bills nobody and can be promoted, a wrongly automatic one bills everybody who enters.
